Not vacated. Not recently departed. Empty in a way that made the air feel pressed thin, like the skin of a bubble about to break. The needle's stone floor still pulsed with residual heat where Vale's form had stood, where the roots had aligned and collapsed, where the node had engaged and the correction had snapped.

But Vale was not there.

Sera's hand drifted to the wall. Her fingers found a patch of stone that was warm on one side and cold on the other, as though space itself had been peeled back and resealed a fraction too quickly.

"Brak," she said, and her voice was careful now, tightly held. "He was here. I felt him. The needle sang his key."

The giant's silhouette filled the doorway behind her, milk-white eyes fixed on nothing. "He is not gone," Brak rumbled, the words slow and heavy as settling stone. "He is elsewhere."

Sera's jaw tightened. "Same thing."

"No." Brak's huge hand settled on the stone where Vale had stood. His palm covered the patch of warmth, and his breathing deepened. "The stone remembers where it lost him. That means he is still in the pattern. Just... not here."

Sera stared at the empty air.

"He just got back," she said quietly. "Two weeks walking. I was going to put less salt in the stew."

Outside, the Node Gamma roots still hummed their single, sustained tone. The needle's resonance, for the first time in six thousand years, was asymmetrical.
