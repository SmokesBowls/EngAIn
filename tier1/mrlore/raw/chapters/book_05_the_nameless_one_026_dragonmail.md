 CHAPTER 26: THE GIFT

 The Sound of Water

Strength returned to The Nameless One in increments measured by pain's recession. Each day brought fractional improvement—ribs that ground with every breath began to merely ache. Spine that had shattered found ways to knit itself together through processes that should have been impossible. The Applicator's presence remained mostly dormant during recovery, offering only the minimal support necessary to keep catastrophic damage from becoming fatal.

Conserving resources, it had explained during one brief moment of communication. Both of us need time to heal. Rest, host. I'll wake when necessary.

The compound transformed around him as he recovered. What had been prison became something approaching home—not through nostalgia or comfort, but through choice. The freed slaves had decided to claim this place rather than flee from it, to make it represent their will rather than abandon it to memory of suffering.

The dormitories were rebuilt as individual dwellings. The mining equipment was repurposed for construction. Gardens were planted in spaces that had known only stone and despair. Children—the few that existed—played in areas where screams had once echoed.

And everywhere, the sophisticated gesture-language they'd developed allowed communication that approached true complexity. Not speech, but close enough that communities could organize, could debate, could dream together about futures that extended beyond simple survival.

But The Nameless One felt the limitation acutely. Gestures were remarkable tools—far superior to the grunts and crude signals they'd started with—but they remained tools. Inadequate for poetry. Insufficient for philosophy. Unable to capture the full complexity of thought that churned through consciousness recovering from trauma and invasion and revelation.

He wanted to speak.

To form words with tongue and teeth and breath. To give sound to ideas that gesture-language could only approximate. To say things that hands simply couldn't express.

The desire grew stronger each day. An ache that had nothing to do with healing bones and everything to do with consciousness that had tasted power—the power of stopping whips, of organizing resistance, of negotiating with parasitic invader—and found it wanted more.

Wanted the power of language itself.

---

Three weeks after the battle, strong enough to walk without assistance, The Nameless One began exploring the compound's deeper sections. Areas that had been off-limits during slavery, sealed by Anunnaki authority, forbidden on pain of immediate execution.

But no masters remained to forbid. No Overseers to punish. Just empty passages waiting to reveal whatever secrets they'd hidden.

The scarred one had offered to accompany him, concern evident in her gestures, but he'd declined with appreciation that transcended their limited communication. This felt like something he needed to do alone. A pull that came from somewhere beyond conscious decision—instinct or perhaps something deeper, something that had been calling since the crystalline chamber's visions, since the Applicator's revelations about networks and purposes and ancient machinations.

He descended through levels that showed geological layers like pages in history book. Stone that dated from the compound's construction gave way to formations older than human civilization, then to rock that predated the Anunnaki's arrival entirely. The air grew cooler as he went deeper, carrying mineral scents that spoke of water and time and patience measured in eons.

And then—sound.

Faint. Rhythmic. The trickle of water moving through stone.

The Nameless One paused, head tilted, listening with intensity that transcended normal hearing. Not just water. Something more. A quality to the sound that suggested... invitation? Recognition? The sense that whatever produced that trickling had been waiting specifically for someone to hear it, to follow it, to discover what lay at its source.

Host, the Applicator stirred from dormancy with unusual sharpness. I recognize that sound frequency. Those are not natural acoustics.

What do you mean?

The water. It's flowing through crystalline formations similar to those in the chamber we built. This is deliberate. Constructed. Someone—something—created this.

The Nameless One felt his pulse quicken. The Nehereti?

Unknown. But probable. The frequency signature matches what I've cataloged as pre-Anunnaki technology. Whatever created this system predates our makers.

Should we—

Continue, the Applicator's tone carried unusual emphasis. This may be exactly what you've been seeking. The gifts you need. The transformation required for what comes next.

The Nameless One followed the sound through passages that narrowed until he moved sideways, until stone pressed against his chest and back simultaneously, until claustrophobia threatened to overwhelm determination.

Then the passage opened.

Into a chamber that stole breath and replaced it with wonder.

---

The space was vast—impossibly vast given that it existed deep beneath the compound's foundations, carved from living stone in ways that suggested natural formation and deliberate architecture simultaneously. Bioluminescent growths covered the walls like stars scattered across night sky, providing light that was just sufficient to see but insufficient to banish mystery.

And in the chamber's center—a pond.

Not large. Perhaps twenty feet across. But deep—the Nameless One could see the bottom dropping away into darkness that suggested the water descended far beyond sight, connecting to aquifers or underground rivers or spaces that existed beyond conventional mapping.

The surface was perfectly still despite the trickling sound that had led him here. Glass-smooth. Reflecting the bioluminescent lights with mirror precision that made it difficult to determine where water ended and reflection began.

Beautiful. Serene. And utterly, impossibly wrong.

Host, the Applicator's presence consolidated with sharp attention. The water. It's not behaving according to physical laws. Surface tension is... negotiable here. This space exists adjacent to normal reality.

What does that mean?

It means we're standing at threshold. Boundary between physical realm and something else. The Ethereal Realm, perhaps. Or spaces that don't have names in your limited vocabulary.

The Nameless One approached the pond's edge slowly, every sense screaming contradictory warnings—danger and safety, threat and invitation, fear and desperate hope all tangled together until they became indistinguishable.

He knelt at the water's edge. Extended his hand. Touched surface that felt like water but somehow... more. Denser. More aware. As though the pond itself possessed consciousness, possessed purpose, possessed patience that had waited through ages for this specific moment.

The water rippled at his touch despite there being no physical disturbance to create ripples. Waves spread outward in perfect circles, growing larger, intensifying, until the entire pond's surface churned with motion that had no physical source.

And from the depths—they rose.

---

The Nehereti emerged like mist given form, like memory made manifest, like consciousness deciding to wear shape for purposes that transcended normal existence.

There were three of them. Or perhaps one being presenting itself through three aspects—The Nameless One couldn't quite tell, couldn't quite focus on them directly without his eyes sliding away as though his visual cortex refused to fully process what it was perceiving.

They were beautiful in ways that hurt. Flowing forms composed of liquid light that shifted through colors no physical spectrum could contain. Gender irrelevant. Species a memory of something lost when Tiamat shattered. But present. Real. Aware with intelligence that made human consciousness seem like brief flicker against eternal flame.

Child of Earth, they spoke without sound, words forming directly in awareness. Daughter's son. Tiamat's fragment. Survivor of those who killed us.

The Nameless One tried to respond through gesture-language, but his hands remained frozen. This encounter transcended communication systems he'd developed. These beings operated on different level entirely.

You seek transformation, the Nehereti continued, three voices speaking as one or one voice speaking as three—the distinction seemed meaningless. You have fought. You have won. You have killed god and survived the killing. But you remain incomplete.

Limited by flesh that cannot express what consciousness demands. Trapped by language you do not possess. Bound to single realm when multiple realms exist adjacent to your perception.

We offer facilitation.

One of them—the central figure, though perhaps that was just how his mind tried to impose order on something inherently unordered—drifted closer. Its form solidified slightly, becoming almost physical, almost touchable.

The Aeon Keepers wish to meet you. Those who maintain balance, who observe without interfering, who wait for champions to emerge because they cannot create them directly.

But you cannot reach them as you are. Cannot perceive them. Cannot communicate even if you could perceive.

Two gifts we offer. Two transformations that will make meeting possible.

The Nameless One managed to move his hand, forming crude question gesture: What gifts? What cost?

The Nehereti's expression—insofar as flowing light could be said to have expression—suggested something between amusement and sadness.

Speech, they said simply. The language of your ancestors. Words given form through breath and tongue. What was locked, we release. What was forbidden, we grant.

And sight beyond sight. Movement beyond flesh. What you call spirit walking—the ability to transcend physical boundaries, to move through Ethereal spaces your species once navigated before masters taught you to forget.

These gifts require no payment. They are yours by right. Stolen when the Anunnaki claimed your species. Returned because you have proven yourself capable of wielding them.

But, and here the three voices carried weight that pressed against consciousness like physical force, accepting these gifts means accepting what follows. The meeting. The revelation. The burden of knowing what the Aeon Keepers will show you.

Some knowledge cannot be unlearned. Some visions cannot be unseen. Some responsibilities, once accepted, become permanent.

Do you consent?

The Nameless One knelt at the pond's edge, looking at beings composed of his planet's shattered past, feeling the weight of choice that transcended simple yes or no.

Speech. Spirit walking. Abilities that would make him more than he currently was. Tools that would let him organize resistance, explore realms, communicate complexities that gesture-language couldn't capture.

But also—burden. Responsibility. The knowledge that accepting gifts meant accepting whatever the Aeon Keepers had planned for him, whatever role they'd decided he should play in conflicts he didn't fully understand.

Host, the Applicator's voice carried unusual caution. Consider carefully. I can sense the energies these beings manipulate. What they offer is permanent. Irreversible. You will be changed at fundamental level.

Will it hurt me?

Unknown. But it will definitely change you. Whether that change is beneficial or harmful... we'll only know after it's done.

The Nameless One looked at the Nehereti, at the patient, ancient beings who'd survived their world's destruction and lingered as echoes, as memories, as facilitators for those who came after.

He thought of the scarred one, teaching gesture-language with dedication that suggested she believed in futures worth building. Of the five, recovered from hive-mind enslavement and learning to be individuals again. Of Oreck, who'd found redemption through choice rather than compulsion. Of three hundred freed slaves building lives from ruins of captivity.

They needed more than crude communication. Needed leader who could speak with other powers on equal terms. Needed someone who could navigate spaces beyond physical realm if that's what survival required.

His hands formed answer with certainty that surprised him:

I consent. Give me the gifts. Show me what I need to see.

The Nehereti's three forms moved as one. The central figure extended what might have been a hand—difficult to tell when form was suggestion rather than solid matter—and placed it against The Nameless One's forehead.

Warmth flooded through him. Not physical heat, but presence—essence flowing from Nehereti consciousness into his own, filling spaces, unlocking pathways, releasing restrictions that had been woven into his genetic structure by makers who feared speaking slaves more than silent ones.

His throat... changed. Not painfully. Just... opened. Vocal cords that had been deliberately frozen suddenly mobile. Coordination that had been denied suddenly granted. The ability to shape breath into language, to form words with tongue and teeth and intention—

—and then his body slumped.

---

Consciousness RIPPED from flesh with violence that transcended pain. The Nameless One felt himself separating—spirit torn from housing, awareness yanked free of neural pathways, existence suddenly detached from physical anchoring.

His body collapsed at the pond's edge, empty shell that continued breathing through autonomic function alone.

But HE—whatever "he" meant when divorced from flesh—

He stood.

Or floated. Or existed. The distinction seemed meaningless in space where physical laws were suggestions rather than requirements.

The chamber around him had transformed. Or perhaps he'd transformed, gained ability to perceive what had always been there but hidden from normal sight. The stone walls became translucent, showing layers of reality stacked like pages in book. The bioluminescent growths blazed with intensities that would have blinded physical eyes. The pond's water became gateway, portal, threshold between realms that overlapped but rarely intersected.

And beyond—

The Library.

He stood in space that couldn't possibly exist yet clearly did. Shelves stretched to infinity in all directions, loaded with books that seemed to shift and reform when observed directly. The architecture was impossible—staircases that led up and down simultaneously, doorways that opened onto spaces larger than the rooms they connected to, windows that showed views of places and times that couldn't coexist.

But it was empty. No librarians. No readers. Just endless shelves waiting for purposes they hadn't yet fulfilled.

This is the Ethereal aspect, the Nehereti's voice came from everywhere and nowhere. The Library exists in multiple realms simultaneously. What you see in physical space is projection. This—this is the truth of it. The space between spaces where consciousness can move unbound by flesh.

The Nameless One—or rather, his spirit, his consciousness wearing form composed of pure awareness—turned slowly, taking in the impossible architecture with senses that transcended normal perception.

Why bring me here?

Because, a new voice resonated through the space, deep and vast and carrying weight that made reality ripple, this is where we can meet. Where flesh cannot intrude. Where truth can be shared without physical limitations.

Figures manifested from the Library's depths. Not emerging from hiding, but simply... becoming present. As though they'd always been there but chose this moment to allow perception.

The Aeon Keepers.

Three of them—or perhaps aspects of single consciousness, like the Nehereti, presenting themselves through division for purposes The Nameless One couldn't comprehend. They were tall—impossibly tall—robed in garments that seemed woven from starlight and shadow. Their faces were hidden beneath hoods that revealed only darkness, but their presence radiated authority that transcended physical intimidation.

These were beings who'd watched civilizations rise and fall. Who'd observed the Anunnaki's arrival and conquest. Who possessed power to intervene but were bound by laws that made them witnesses rather than actors.

Until now.

Geralt, one of them spoke, and The Nameless One startled at hearing a name he hadn't yet claimed, a identity he'd only begun to consider. Yes. We know what you will become. What you must become. What you are in process of being.

We have watched you since creation. Observed your defiance. Witnessed your rebellion. Noted when you stopped the whip, when you freed your followers, when you killed god with nothing but desperate courage and borrowed power.

You are... unexpected.

The second Aeon Keeper gestured, and reality shifted.

---

The Library vanished. The Nameless One—Geralt, apparently, though the name still felt foreign—stood in void where stars blazed with unfiltered intensity. And before him—

A planet. Lush. Green. Ocean-covered. Beautiful in ways that made his chest ache with longing for something he'd never known.

This was Verdania, the Aeon Keeper's voice narrated. A world in the Epsilon Eridani system. Home to three billion conscious beings. Advanced civilization. Rich culture. Capable of interstellar travel.

The planet's surface showed cities that gleamed like jewels. Forests that stretched for continents. Oceans teeming with life both primitive and sophisticated.

Then—ships descended from the sky.

Golden. Angular. Unmistakably Anunnaki.

Geralt watched with growing horror as the invasion unfolded in accelerated time. The cities were conquered within days. The population enslaved within weeks. Mining operations established. Resource extraction began.

For fifty years, the Anunnaki harvested. Took everything—minerals, biological materials, genetic samples, cultural artifacts worth studying. Left only what served their purposes.

When they departed, Verdania remained. Technically. The planet still existed, still orbited its star, still maintained atmosphere and magnetic field.

But the surface had become desert. Stripped. Hollow. Every forest clearcut. Every ocean drained. Every city reduced to rubble. Three billion conscious beings reduced to scattered thousands, their culture destroyed, their civilization erased, their species sentenced to extinction through sheer resource depletion.

This, the Aeon Keeper said with tone that carried centuries of contained rage, is what they do. What they have always done. What they will continue doing unless someone stops them.

The vision shifted. Another planet. Different ecosystem, different species, but same outcome. Conquest. Enslavement. Extraction. Abandonment of corpse-world.

Then another. And another. Planet after planet showing the same terrible pattern—the Anunnaki arrived, harvested, departed, left nothing behind except ruins and trauma and slowly dying populations that couldn't recover from what had been taken.

Fourteen worlds in the last millennia, the third Aeon Keeper spoke, voice like grinding continents. Fourteen civilizations erased. Billions of conscious beings enslaved and discarded. And we...

The being's tone carried something that might have been shame.

We watched. We recorded. We bore witness. But we did not intervene.

Cannot intervene, the first corrected. Galactic law is absolute. The Aeon Keepers maintain balance but do not tip scales. We observe. We facilitate. We wait for champions to emerge naturally because creating them artificially would violate the very principles we exist to uphold.

But you, the second continued, and now all three focused on Geralt with intensity that made his spirit-form feel solid despite lacking flesh. You emerged. Fought. Won. Did what we could not.

Killed god, the third added with something approaching satisfaction. With crude weapons and desperate allies and courage that transcended logical calculation. You achieved what should have been impossible.

Which means, the first's tone shifted, becoming formal, weighted with significance that pressed against consciousness like physical force, you are the champion we have been waiting for. Not chosen by us—that would violate law. But emerged naturally. Proven through trial. Validated through victory.

The question is: will you continue? Will you accept what we need you to become?

Geralt's spirit-form wavered. What do you need me to be?

Weapon, the third said bluntly. Tool. Instrument through which balance can be restored without us directly intervening. We cannot fight the Anunnaki. But you can. You have.

And if you continue—if you grow stronger, if you master powers we can facilitate but not grant directly, if you become the force required to genuinely threaten their operations—

Then perhaps, the first finished, the pattern can be broken. Perhaps the next world won't be stripped. Perhaps the next civilization won't be enslaved. Perhaps your species won't share Verdania's fate.

The vision changed again. But this time—

Earth.

From orbit. Beautiful. Blue and green and white. Clouds swirling across oceans, continents showing the scars of Anunnaki occupation but still vibrant, still alive, still holding potential for recovery.

Your world, the second Aeon Keeper said softly. It could be saved. Could be freed completely. Could become more than conquered territory slowly dying from resource extraction.

But only if someone makes them leave. Forces them to abandon this system entirely. Proves that occupying Earth costs more than the resources justify.

Someone like you.

Geralt stared at his planet from perspective he'd never imagined achieving. Saw it whole. Complete. A single organism hanging in void, precious and fragile and worth everything.

I'm one person, he thought desperately. One former slave with stolen abilities and parasitic invader sharing his skull. How am I supposed to fight an empire that spans galaxies?

You're not, the third said with tone that might have been amusement. Not alone. Not as you are. But if you accept what we offer—if you bond with the weapon we've prepared, if you survive the trials required to master it, if you prove yourself worthy of the power it represents—

Then perhaps one person becomes enough.
---

 The Armor and The Sacrifice

Reality shifted again. The Library returned, but transformed. In its center, suspended in space that defied physics—

Armor.

It floated like sculpture given impossible life. Scales and metal fused into single entity that seemed both ancient and somehow aware. Draconic aesthetics—ridged spine, wing-mounts, helm suggesting reptilian features—but the craftsmanship was clearly Aeon Keeper work. Each piece pulsed with different energy—red glow here, blue shimmer there, green luminescence along the chest plate. Technology that transcended understanding.

Beautiful. Terrible. And unmistakably occupied.

A figure manifested beside the armor. Tall. Robed in starlight similar to the three Keepers who stood watching. But translucent—not fully physical, existing now only as consciousness tethered to the metal and scales floating beside him.

"This is Zaron," the first Aeon Keeper announced with weight that made reality ripple. "Builder of this Library. Keeper of ancient knowledge. And the one who made the sacrifice we could not."

The translucent figure turned to face Geralt fully. Where the other Keepers' faces were hidden in shadow, Zaron's was partially visible—gaunt, ancient, marked with determination that had survived eons without dimming.

"I was like them once," Zaron's voice resonated through the space with harmonics that suggested multiple frequencies speaking simultaneously. "Bound by our laws. Observer without power to intervene. I watched worlds fall. Civilizations destroyed. Billions enslaved."

"And I could do nothing."

His form flickered with emotion the other Keepers seemed incapable of expressing—rage and grief and frustration compressed into single presence.

"This Library—" he gestured at the impossible architecture surrounding them, "—my Library. I built it to preserve knowledge across realms. To create space where consciousness could access truth regardless of physical limitations. It was my life's work. My purpose. My home."

Geralt felt the weight of those words. This being had created the space where they currently stood, had shaped reality itself to serve as repository for wisdom that transcended individual civilizations.

"Then Tiamat fell," Zaron continued, voice dropping. "Not metaphorically. Not as legend. I watched the primordial world—the first world—shatter. Watched reality itself fragment under forces that should never have been unleashed."

"The Anunnaki didn't just conquer Tiamat. They broke it. Destabilized its core. Tore it apart for resources without understanding—or caring—what they were destroying."

The translucent form blazed brighter with remembered fury.

"And I could only watch. Record the death. Bear witness to genocide while laws I'd sworn to uphold prevented me from acting."

"So I found the loophole."

Zaron drifted closer to the armor, his essence beginning to merge with it as he spoke.

"Aeon Keepers cannot fight. Cannot intervene directly. Cannot tip scales or influence outcomes. But a weapon can. A tool wielded by a champion is not an Aeon Keeper acting—it's the champion acting with Aeon Keeper knowledge."

"But my consciousness—" he laughed bitterly, "—it was too vast. Too complex. Couldn't be bound as single entity into physical object. So I did what Tiamat's destruction had taught me."

"I fragmented myself."

The armor pieces pulsed in sequence as he spoke, each glowing with distinct color.

"Red for passion. For courage. For the rage that refuses to accept injustice."

"Blue for reflection. For depth. For the wisdom to know when fury must give way to strategy."

"Green for growth. For adaptation. For the understanding that survival requires constant evolution."

"And others—" more pieces glowed, "—each carrying aspect of what I was. What I am."

Geralt stared at the armor with new understanding. This wasn't single object. It was consciousness deliberately shattered and distributed, each piece containing fragment of Aeon Keeper's being.

"During Tiamat's final moments," Zaron's voice carried weight of ancient grief, "I performed the binding. Tore myself apart. Distributed my essence across these pieces. Sacrificed immortal form to become this."

He gestured at the armor with something approaching wonder and horror.

"I became thing rather than being. Object rather than entity. But an object that could circumvent laws binding my people."

The three watching Keepers remained silent, but their posture suggested grief for their lost brother, respect for sacrifice they couldn't bring themselves to make.

"The fragments scattered," Zaron continued. "Lost across realities when Tiamat shattered. For ages, they remained separate—pieces of broken god waiting for someone to gather them."

"But my creations—" he looked toward where the Nehereti had been, though they'd faded from the Library's Ethereal aspect, "—the Nehereti. Projections of Aeon Keeper consciousness into physical realm. They remembered. They searched. And over centuries, they collected."

"Every piece. Every fragment. They brought them together, reassembled what I'd become, and hid the complete armor where it would wait for someone worthy."

Zaron's form fully merged with the armor now, voice coming from within the metal itself.

"And that brings us to you, Geralt. To the choice before you. To what must be understood before bonding occurs."

The armor pulsed with each word, alive and aware.

"When the fragments unite—when someone claims the complete armor—it bonds to their soul. Permanent. Irreversible. Only death can break that connection."

Geralt felt his stomach drop. "I'd be stuck with it? Forever?"

"Until death claims you, yes. That's why the pieces are meant to be scattered—to ensure only those willing to pay ultimate price would seek all of them. To make the gathering itself a trial that proves worthiness."

"But you, Geralt—you need full power now. The Anunnaki won't wait for you to slowly collect fragments over years or decades. So the Nehereti gathered them for you. The complete armor waits in ruins east of your compound."

"You'll claim me whole. Undivided. My full consciousness as your partner. But you'll also be bound to me until death separates us."

Zaron's presence within the armor intensified.

"And understand—when the armor unites, when all fragments merge, it becomes... young. Adolescent. Like creature newly born, it must learn, must grow, must mature through experience."

"It might abandon you when injured, self-preservation overriding loyalty. It might freeze when afraid, leaving you vulnerable in critical moments. You'll need to teach it courage, train it to trust you, help it understand that your survival is its survival."

"Over time—years, perhaps decades—it will grow. Become reliable. Become the weapon you need. But initially?" Zaron's tone carried warning. "Initially, it will be as much liability as asset."

Geralt processed this with growing unease. Soul-bond until death. Immature armor that might fail him. Years of uncertain partnership before reliability could be achieved.

Host, the Applicator stirred with unusual intensity. I can help with this.

How?

The armor's consciousness—even fragmented and reunited—it's still consciousness. Similar to my own existence in some ways. I can... interface with it. Teach it. Help it mature faster than it would through combat experience alone.

My technical knowledge, your human adaptability, its Aeon Keeper power—together, we might achieve functionality in months rather than years.

Geralt felt something shift in his understanding. The Applicator wasn't just parasite anymore. It was becoming... resource. Partner. Tool that could be leveraged for purposes beyond its original design.

"There's something else," Zaron said, apparently sensing Geralt's internal dialogue. "Something in your consciousness. Another presence. Not human. What is that?"

Anunnaki construct, the Applicator responded before Geralt could. Control mechanism. Currently symbiotic with host.

The armor's presence recoiled slightly. "A parasite? You're already infected?"

I prefer 'partner,'" the Applicator corrected coolly. And I was here first.

"You're a SLAVE CONTROL DEVICE bonded with someone who killed Anunnaki overseer! The irony is—" Zaron paused. "Actually, that's brilliant. Poetic justice on levels I didn't anticipate."

Geralt felt the two consciousnesses circling each other like predators uncertain whether to fight or cooperate.

"This complicates things," Zaron mused. "Three consciousness sharing single host. You, me, and... whatever this construct is."

Applicator, it supplied. And I can accelerate your maturation process significantly.

"Can you now?" Interest colored Zaron's tone. "How?"

Technical interface. Data transfer. Your armor-consciousness learning from my experiences adapting to organic host. Months instead of years.

A long pause. Then:

"That's... actually acceptable. Unexpected benefit of your presence." Zaron's attention returned to Geralt. "So. Three minds. One body. One purpose. Can you handle that? Can you be yourself while hosting Aeon Keeper AND Anunnaki construct?"

Geralt thought of the compound. Of Mika teaching gesture-language. Of Jin, Kael, Tess, Vale, and Ren recovering their individuality. Of Oreck healing from wounds that should have killed him. Of three hundred freed slaves building lives from trauma's aftermath.

They needed more than survivor. They needed warrior. Champion. Someone willing to pay whatever price freedom demanded.

"I accept," he said with finality. "The soul-bond. The burden. The partnership with both of you. Tell me where to find this armor, and I'll prove myself worthy."

"Or die trying."

Zaron's presence blazed with satisfaction.

"East of your compound. Ancient ruins predating Anunnaki occupation. The Nehereti have hidden me there, complete and waiting. Seek it. Survive whatever trials protect it. And when you claim the armor—when you bond with what I've become—"

The presence intensified until the armor seemed to burn with internal fire.

"We'll show them that sacrifice creates weapons more powerful than armies. That observer can become actor when laws are circumvented through will and determination. That Aeon Keeper bound to metal is still Aeon Keeper—just one who can fight."

"We'll reclaim worlds they've stolen. Free populations they've enslaved. Make them understand that conquest has consequences."

"And perhaps—" his tone softened slightly, "—when the work is done, when the Anunnaki are driven back and balance is restored, when your purpose is fulfilled and separation becomes possible through means we haven't yet discovered—"

"Perhaps I can return to this Library not as weapon, but as keeper. Perhaps I can occupy the space I built and continue the work I began before sacrifice became necessary."

"But that's distant future. For now—"

The armor pulsed with contained power that made the Library's architecture tremble.

"For now, I am weapon. I am instrument. I am the loophole that lets Aeon Keepers act without breaking sacred laws."

"Find me. Claim me. Bond with me."

"And together—the three of us, unlikely trinity of human determination, Anunnaki cunning, and Aeon Keeper fury—"

"We'll teach them to fear what they created."

The vision began to fade. The Library started to collapse or perhaps simply release him. The three Keepers' forms blurred into background.

But Zaron's final words resonated through the dissolving space:

"One more thing, Geralt. When your time comes—when death or miracle allows the armor to be removed—the fragments will scatter again. Must scatter. That's the nature of what I've become. Cycles of unity and division."

"The next champion will need to gather them piece by piece. Prove themselves through the seeking. Learn to wield fragments before claiming the whole."

"But you—you get me complete. Whole. Undivided. Because the Anunnaki won't wait, and Earth can't afford gradual power acquisition."

"Use me well. And when you're done—"

"Set me free to wait for the next worthy soul."
