That was the horror. It was methodical. Inevitable. The clouds dissolved into the same blank whiteness, as if the entire planet were being dipped in liquid porcelain.

Kyh's rasping voice cut through the silence. "It's calibrating." She struggled upright on her cot, shadows flickering at her fingertips like frayed threads. "The intervals—count them."

Each flash left behind a lingering afterimage—ghostly, interlocking hexagons that pulsed once before fading. Not a storm. A synchronization pulse. The Queen wasn't just cloaking Earth; she was installing a celestial metronome, her rhythm etching itself into the planet's very rotation.

Oreck's claws flexed against his scaled thighs. "We could burn for the exosphere—"

"Too late." Geralt's voice was hollow, not from emotion, but from the sheer weight of causal certainty. The Dragon Mail's sensors showed the truth: the white sky wasn't an overlay. It was a rewrite. The lightning strikes were stitching a new baseline reality into the Akashic record, thread by thread. By the time their shuttle could reach geosynchronous orbit, Earth would no longer recognize blue as a possible color for its sky.

Kulla, hunched over his half-repaired knee, made a wet grinding noise deep in his clay throat. "Bad architecture. No expansion joints." He crumbled a piece of mortar between his fingers. "No room for breath."

The third strike lit the Atlantic. Geralt exhaled through his teeth. This was the Queen's gambit—not to destroy, but to perfect. To render Earth's story so seamless, so unassailably uniform, that defiance would become unthinkable. A white sky. A white world. A white truth.

And somewhere beneath that spreading blankness, the Five Mikas were still breathing.
