BOOK 9 - CHAPTER 49 ARRIVAL IN FIRE

ACT 1: THE BURNING SKY

The emergency Void Jump to Pyroclast wasn't a transition—it was a violent expulsion.

They were spat out mid-air into a world screaming in thermal agony. Twin suns burned through an ash-choked sky the color of a fresh bruise. Below them stretched pure, multi-faction chaos.

The Dragon Wars were not a myth here. They were geology.

· Dragons of every elemental allegiance—Golden Flame, Void-touched, Water-callers, Earth-wardens—clashed in aerial duels that painted the sky with fire and lightning.
· VALDRASIL-backed Anunnaki constructs, massive biomechanical horrors, tore at the planet's crust, digging for something deep below.
· Arcturian energy-weavers and Lyrian blade-dancers held desperate ground positions against waves of Zeta Reticulan tech-corpses.
· Pleiadian skim-ships strafed in precise, lethal patterns, targeting the Anunnaki dig-sites.

And through it all, the planet itself was hemorrhaging. Magma vents exploded randomly across the landscape, vomiting fire and molten rock into the already choked air.

Geralt's armor deployed its wings with a thunderous snap, catching them in a sickening lurch. The heat hit them like a physical wall. The applicator flashed: < Ambient Temperature: 127°C. Atmospheric Toxicity: Extreme. Survival Estimate: 17 minutes without protection. >

Fire is screaming, whispered Red, her presence a pained ember in the thermal chaos. It does not want to burn like this.

The earth is being torn, rumbled Green, a deep ache in the bond. It bleeds.

They descended into the hellscape, the dragon armor's wings shedding molten ash with each beat.

ACT 2: AELXION'S INTERCEPTION

They hadn't fallen a hundred feet before something massive and fast intercepted them.

Aelxion, the Warden of Breach, was a dragon of weathered bronze scales and eyes that held the cold, hard light of broken stars. He moved with terrifying precision, cutting off their descent, his maw opening to reveal a plasma-gland ready to incinerate them.

"Another insertion!" his voice boomed, vibrating through Geralt's bones. "More rats for the burning maze!"

"WE'RE HERE FOR THE SPIRE!" Geralt roared back, the armor amplifying his voice.

"Liar! Everyone wants the spire! For conquest! For fire!"

There was no time. Aelxion drew breath to burn them from the sky.

"NOW, LUMINAIRE!"

A pulse of pure, harmonic Keeper-signature—Luminaire's unique resonance—erupted from Geralt, not as an attack, but as a identifier. A calling card of the network's original architect.

Aelxion choked off his fire. The plasma died in his throat. His star-broken eyes narrowed, scanning Geralt with sudden, intense focus.

"You... you're with the Spire? Not just another scavenger?"

"We're here for Aetheric Coil."

A sound like grinding stone escaped Aelxion—a draconic sigh of profound exhaustion. "The planet is screaming. This isn't conquest anymore. It's collapse. If you're here to help Coil..." He looked at the carnage below, his wings beating to hold them steady in the superheated updrafts. "...you're already too late. But follow. Try anyway."

He banked sharply, leading them through a hail of energy fire and falling debris.

ACT 3: BATTLEFIELD ALLIANCE

Aelxion brought them down behind an Arcturian energy-ward—a shimmering dome of blue light holding against a tide of Anunnaki constructs. Inside, a Lyrian commander with skin like polished oak coordinated her blade-dancers with an Arcturian whose form flickered with contained starlight.

"Commander," Aelxion rumbled, landing with a ground-shaking impact. "These two claim they can help Coil. I'm inclined to let them try."

The Arcturian commander, his voice a chorus of harmonics, didn't turn from the ward. "The magma regulation is failing. Chambers are exploding at random. We've lost three squads to flash-venting. If you have a solution, implement it. We'll hold this line for seven more minutes. Then we retreat or die."

No ceremony. No debate. Survival had pared communication down to bare essentials.

Geralt and Oreck joined the line. Oreck's cleaver met Anunnaki alloy with showers of sparks. Geralt's armor tanked searing energy blasts, the plates glowing cherry-red. They fought alongside aliens, a coordinated pocket of chaos in a sea of madness. It was ugly, efficient, and desperate.

A strange harmony, Violet observed softly in Geralt's mind. Different musics, same song of survival.

ACT 4: MEETING COIL

Aelxion cleared a brutal path to the Spire—a crystalline structure of obsidian and frozen fire at the edge of the massive central caldera. It was marred by hairline fractures weeping molten light.

Inside was an oasis of comparative calm and crushing despair.

The chamber was cooler, the air humming with a stabilizing frequency. At its center, suspended above a pool of shimmering liquid metal, rotated Aetheric Coil.

He was a geometric crystalline being, facets shifting and reflecting the chamber's light. His voice, when it came, was a harmonic layered with raw, metallic panic.

"Anchor. Luminaire. HELP."

The words were a distortion wave that made Geralt's teeth ache.

"The planet's core temperature is rising. The magma arteries are blocked. I regulate the thermal flow, but the blockage—I can't reach it. If I leave this chamber, even for a moment, the planetary systems I'm manually stabilizing collapse immediately. I am trapped. The war outside damages the crust faster than I can compensate. I am... failing."

He is the heart trying to pump through a clenched fist, Green rumbled, feeling the planetary distress. The fist is winning.

ACT 5: THE DIAGNOSIS

Luminaire extended her perception through Geralt, examining Coil's fraying network connection.

"Something is consuming the excess thermal flow," she reported, her voice tight. "Not regulating it—overfeeding. Creating pressure differentials. That's what's causing the explosive venting."

Coil's rotation hitched. "Yes. Deep in the primary caldera nexus. I feel it. An alien presence. Not draconic. Not any known species in my database. It is... hungry. And growing."

Aelxion, who had followed them in, let out a low growl. "Anunnaki seeder organism. We've suspected they planted something when they began their digs. A biological weapon to cripple the planet before full invasion. We could never confirm—the caldera maze is collapsed. Impassable."

Geralt looked from the panicking Keeper to the weary dragon. "Can we kill it?"

Coil's facets flashed. "You must. But the path is sealed. Rubble, magma flows, tectonic shifts... You would need—"

A shadow detached itself from the corner of the chamber.

ACT 6: KYH ARRIVES

A portal of seamless darkness opened, and a figure stepped through, supporting a wounded Lyrian blade-dancer.

Kyh. A Shadow Weaver.

He was young, battle-worn, his clothes simple and stained with soot and alien blood. His eyes were the color of a twilight sky, holding a weight beyond his years. He gently set the wounded Lyrian down against the wall, his movements efficient and kind.

Then he saw Geralt. Saw the faint, silver luminescence of Luminaire's presence shimmering around him. He froze.

"You're... the Anchor?"

His voice was quieter than expected. Tired.

Geralt nodded. "You've been using the spire's tendrils."

"I've been using shadow-paths to evacuate wounded from the front lines. Walking the spire's energy currents without knowing what they were." He looked at Coil, then back at Geralt, realization dawning. "I thought I was just... hiding from the war. Using old, quiet places."

"You navigate collapsed spaces," Geralt said. "You portal through solid walls."

Kyh met his gaze. There was no bravado there. Only the grim acknowledgment of a skill that had become a curse and a responsibility. "I do."

"Then we need you."

Kyh looked at the wounded Lyrian he'd brought, then at the fractured Spire, then at the distant sounds of a world dying in fire. He let out a slow breath.

"I'm already standing on the planet's arteries," he said, his voice firming. "Might as well try to clear the blockage."

He stepped forward, joining the circle. The party was formed.

In Geralt's mind, the Five Mikas stirred, their attention focusing on the new presence.

His shadow is not cold, Violet observed. It is shelter. He does not fear the dark. He uses it to protect.

Then let's begin, Geralt thought.

The mission to save Pyroclast—and its trapped, panicking Keeper—was now a race into the deepest, hottest, and most collapsed maze on the planet, guided by a Shadow Weaver who knew how to walk through walls.

Epic 2 : THE THREE CRYSTALS

ACT 1: NYXTHORIA - CRYSTAL OF WATER

They found Nyxthoria, Keeper of Void Flames, at the edge of a reality-wound.

It was a place where the war had cut too deep—a jagged, vertical rift in the air, through which poured not light, but a slow, chilling anti-flame: void-fire that consumed heat and left absolute cold in its wake. Nyxthoria, a dragon of polished obsidian scales and eyes like smoked quartz, stood guard before it, her wings spread to contain the bleed.

She did not turn as they approached. “Aelxion sent you. For the crystal.”
Her voice was the sound of ice forming on dead stars.

“The planet’s thermal regulation is blocked,” Geralt said, the heat of Pyroclast making the words steam in the air. “We need the Hydros Crystal to re-establish the pressure gradient after we clear it.”

Nyxthoria finally looked at them. Her gaze was heavy, ancient. “This crystal is not a tool. It is a seal. Its resonance contains the absorption principle—the ability to take in excess heat and pressure without breaking. I use it to guard these rifts. If I give it to you, I cannot hold the void-fire back. It will bleed into your physical realm. Not today. Not tomorrow. But eventually.”

She speaks true, whispered Blue, her presence a cool tremor in Geralt’s mind. The water here is not wet. It is absence given shape. The crystal is the cork in a bottle of freezing dark.

“What comes through those rifts,” Nyxthoria continued, “will be on your conscience. Not mine.”

Geralt looked at the rift. Through it, he saw not another place, but a concept: the end of thermal energy. The death of fire. It was terrifying in a way violence could never be.

He looked back at the burning sky, the exploding magma vents, the desperate battle to save a world.

“We’ll deal with what comes through later,” he said. “Right now, we need to ensure there’s still a ‘later’ to deal with it.”

Nyxthoria held his gaze for a long moment. Then, with a sound like a glacier calving, she lowered her head. A single scale on her chest, darker than the others, irised open. From within, she drew forth the Hydros Crystal.

It was the size of Geralt’s fist, pulsing with a deep, resonant blue light. It did not glow—it drank the light around it, holding it in frozen suspension. The air near it grew still and cold.

“Use it well, Anchor,” Nyxthoria said, her voice already fading as the void-fire behind her began to seep forward, slower, but inevitable. “And remember: you have traded one apocalypse for another. The debt will come due.”

As they left, Geralt felt Blue reach out toward the crystal, not to take it, but to hum in recognition. A kindred spirit. It knows how to hold what is too hot to touch.

ACT 2: ZERATHUNE - CRYSTAL OF EARTH

Zerathune was not fighting. He was persuading.

They found him in a shattered basin, his verdant scales the only green in a landscape of black glass and red flame. He had his claws sunk deep into the bedrock, his body vibrating with a low, sub-sonic frequency. Before him, a massive tectonic fault line was slowly, agonizingly, knitting itself back together.

He did not stop his work as they landed. “Aelxion’s scent is on you. You seek the Terros Crystal.”

“We do,” Geralt said.

“You understand what it is?” Zerathune’s voice was the grind of continental plates—patient, immense, and tired. “It is not a rock. It is a binding agreement. My geomantic resonance given solid form. It tells stone how to be stone. It tells fractures they are whole. I have worn it for eight centuries.”

He finally lifted his head, emerald eyes regarding them. “Giving it to you does not merely disarm me. It scars me. Severs a part of my soul from the earth-song. It will shorten my lifespan by centuries. The stone will forget my name sooner.”

Oreck stepped forward, his scales dusty with ash. “How much time do you lose?”

“Three hundred years. Perhaps four.”

“And if the planet’s core regulation fails? How much time does everything lose?”

Zerathune was silent. The ground beneath them trembled, not from instability, but from his consideration.

He is the earth’s memory, Green rumbled, a profound respect in the bond. To ask this of him is to ask a mountain to forget its own peak.

“A shorter life on a living world,” Zerathune said at last, the words heavy as bedrock, “beats an immortality of echoes on dead ash.”

With a sound of deep, personal rupture, a scale over his heart split open. From it, he drew the Terros Crystal.

It was dense, brown, and seemingly inert—until you held it. Then you felt the thrum, the deep, structural pulse of a planet’s heartbeat. It was stability incarnate.

As the crystal left his grasp, Zerathune visibly dimmed. The vibrant green of his scales dulled towards olive. The fault line before him groaned, but held. He had given them his foundation.

“Do not let my sacrifice be for cinders,” he whispered, before turning back to his work, his song to the earth now fainter, more fragile.

ACT 3: VAELTHANE - CRYSTAL OF FIRE

Vaelthane was a storm of crimson scales and controlled fury.

They found him not on the ground, but in the midst of an aerial engagement, tearing through a flight of Anunnaki skirmishers. Aelxion gave a piercing shriek—a draconic battlefield signal—and Vaelthane broke away, landing before them with a wash of heat that made the air shimmer.

He was smaller than Aelxion, but radiated a more focused, dangerous energy. His eyes, the color of molten brass, scanned Geralt, and for a fleeting moment, something unrecognizable flickered in them.

“Aelxion vouches for you. And you smell of my son’s cause.” His voice was sharp, tactical, devoid of the warmth Geralt might have hoped for. “You want the Pyros Crystal.”

“We need it to reignite the core’s flow after we clear the blockage,” Geralt said. “To restart the heart.”

Vaelthane’s nostrils flared, venting smoke. “This crystal is the regulator of my internal flame. The focus of my combustion. Without it, my fire is wilder. Less efficient. I am weakened in battle.”

He looked past them, to where the war continued unabated. “You ask me to fight a war with a crippled weapon.”

“We’re asking you,” Oreck said, his voice flat, “to choose between winning a battle and saving the battlefield.”

Vaelthane’s gaze returned to Geralt. The scrutiny was intense, personal, and utterly devoid of sentimental fatherly recognition. He was assessing a tool, a variable in the equation of survival.

“You are not my son,” Vaelthane stated, the words clean and surgical. “But you carry his purpose. His… conviction.” He paused, the fire around his claws guttering slightly. “Humility is a dragon’s rarest virtue. To admit the war cannot be won by flame alone.”

He opened his jaws. From the furnace at the back of his throat, he coaxed forth not fire, but a condensation of it—the Pyros Crystal. It blazed a fierce, contained red, hovering before them. It didn’t burn; it contained the principle of ignition.

“Take it. Restart the heart. I will fight my war with tooth and claw and rage. It will have to be enough.”

He took the crystal back only long enough to breathe upon it once, a final binding, then let it float to Geralt’s hand. The moment it left his possession, the brilliant corona of heat around him dulled. He was no less deadly, but the sure, controlled focus was gone. He gave them one last, unreadable look, then launched himself back into the sky without another word, his flight just slightly less precise than before.

His fire is now a wildfire, Red observed, a note of sorrow in her crackling voice. It will burn bright, but it will burn out faster.

ACT 4: DESCENT PREPARATION

Back in the relative calm of Coil’s chamber, the three crystals now hung from Geralt’s armor harness in a rough triangle: the cold blue of Hydros, the steady brown of Terros, the blazing red of Pyros.

Aetheric Coil rotated above his pool, his facets catching the crystals’ light. “The harmony is… correct. The elemental logic is sound. But the caldera maze is a tomb. Collapsed, unstable, flooded with toxic heat and seismic ghosts. Your Shadow Weaver’s portals will be your only map.”

Kyh, who had been quietly observing, rubbed his hands together—a nervous habit. “I can move through shadow, not solid rock. If the way is fully collapsed…”

“Then we dig,” Oreck said, checking the edge of his cleaver. “Or we blow a path. The crystals can interact with the environment. We’ll make a way.”

“I cannot follow you,” Luminaire said, her voice faint. She had manifested a barely-visible shimmer next to Coil. “The depth, the thermal chaos… it would shred my coherence. I will remain here, maintaining the surface network connection. I will… monitor your vital signs through the Anchor bond. If you die, I will know.”

It was the closest she had come to stating concern.

Aelxion, who had remained at the entrance, spoke up, his star-broken eyes serious. “The worm—the Anunnaki seeder—is ancient. It has been buried in the planet’s deepest heat for centuries. It won’t expect visitors. Use that. It is a creature of consumption, not combat. Surprise is your greatest weapon.”

Geralt looked at his team: Oreck, solid and ready; Kyh, nervous but resolved; the three crystals, heavy with sacrifice; and in his mind, the quiet chorus of the Five Mikas, each attuned to the element they were about to confront.

We are with you, Anchor, Violet whispered for them all. The deep dark awaits.

They turned toward the entrance to the lower vents—a gaping maw of darkness and remembered fire that led into the planet’s scorched arteries. The empty caldera yawned before them, a silent, sprawling scar from a past eruption, its passages a labyrinth of collapsed magma tubes and trembling, half-molten rock.

Without another word, they began their descent into the belly of the burning world.

Epic 3: THE WORM

ACT 1: THE MAZE

The caldera wasn't a cave. It was a claustrophobic cathedral of failed geology.

They descended into a maze of collapsed magma arteries, tunnels that had burned hollow, then been crushed by seismic shifts. The air wasn't just hot—it was aggressive, searing the lungs with each breath. The walls glowed a dull, dangerous orange, and the floor was unstable, a crust over flows that hadn't fully cooled.

Kyh led, his hands moving in precise, quiet gestures. Shadows pooled at his command, forming fleeting doorways through walls of semi-molten rock. He wasn't creating paths; he was finding the memory of paths that had once existed, the negative spaces the rock had forgotten to fill.

"Left here," he'd whisper, voice strained. "The shadow is thinner. The way is… almost remembered."

Progress was a brutal, methodical slog.

· When a tunnel ahead pulsed with the promise of a flash-vent, Geralt would raise the Hydros Crystal. A wave of cold, silent absorption would wash forward, quenching the thermal build-up, leaving the passage dark and brittle but safe.
· When the way was blocked by a tumble of sharp, glassy rubble, Oreck would plant the Terros Crystal against it. A deep, subsonic thrum would pass through the stone, not exploding it, but convincing it to rearrange, to settle into a stable, passable slope.

The earth is grateful, Green rumbled, feeling the stone's relief at being guided, not shattered.
The heat is confused, Red crackled softly. It wants to flow, not explode.

Deeper they went. The pressure grew, a physical weight on the chest. The light from the walls grew brighter, harsher.

Kyh’s portals began to flicker. He stumbled, catching himself against a hot wall and hissing in pain.

"Something down here is… wrong," he gasped. "It’s not just heat. It’s a… silence in the shadow. The paths don’t want to form. It’s like something is eating the possibility of them."

He is correct, Violet whispered, her harmonics tinged with unease. This deep, reality is not just physical. It is metabolic. Something is consuming potential itself.

ACT 2: THE WORM ENCOUNTER

They reached the heart of the blockage: a vast, spherical chamber that had once been a magma confluence. The walls were smooth, glassy, scarred by ancient, violent flows.

At the center, connected to the chamber by thick, pulsating umbilical cords of crystalline flesh and metallic cabling, was the Worm.

The Litho-Thanatic Bore Leviathan. It was a grotesque fusion of biology and brutal engineering.

· Segmented body the color of cooled slag.
· A carapace of interlocking, hexagonal crystal plates that glowed with stolen heat.
· No head, just a forward bore-ring lined with fractal drill-teeth, slowly grinding against the sealed primary artery.
· It made no sound but a deep, subsonic vibration—the frequency of endless, blind consumption.

It wasn't a guardian. It wasn't even truly alive. It was a machine with a hunger, an Anunnaki terraforming weapon left to gorge until the planet's heart stopped.

It sensed them. Not through sight or sound, but through a disruption in the thermal flow it was sucking dry. The bore-ring ceased its grinding. The entire, massive body oriented toward them.

The vibration changed pitch. From consumption to hostility.

It attacked. Not with rage, but with the mindless, hydraulic inevitability of a industrial press.

ACT 3: THE FIGHT - SURGICAL, NOT HEROIC

This was not a battle for glory. It was surgery in a furnace.

The chamber was their operating theater, cramped and lethally hot. The Worm was the tumor, and they had three very precise tools.

Geralt was the anvil. His dragon armor flared, absorbing and dissipating the searing radiant heat the Worm emitted. He drew its lashing, cable-like appendages, weathering blows that would have pulverized stone.

Oreck was the scalpel. He didn't roar. He calculated. He watched the rhythmic pulse of the crystal plates, finding the moment of vulnerability in the mechanical cycle. His cleaver struck not to hack, but to pry, targeting the junctures between segments, seeking to disconnect, not destroy.

Kyh was the needle. He flickered in and out of shadows, appearing at the Worm's flanks or above it. He carried no weapon but the Hydros Crystal. He would press its freezing blue light against a glowing segment, flash-cooling a plate from blinding orange to brittle, crystalline black. Then he was gone, a shadow dissolving.

Now, Green would signal.

Oreck’s cleaver would strike the blackened plate. The Terros Crystal on his belt would pulse, transmitting a shockwave of controlled fracturing. The plate would not just break—it would disintegrate into harmless gravel, revealing the pulsing, vulnerable tissue beneath.

It was a timed, silent, brutal dance. Remove the armor. Expose the weakness. Repeat.

The Worm screamed, a subsonic torrent of data-noise and agony that made their bones ache and the chamber walls weep molten glass. It thrashed, causing new cracks to race across the ceiling.

It knows it is dying, White whispered, her voice a pained gust. It is trying to take the chamber with it.

ACT 4: THE KILL

The Worm was failing, its carapace half-gone, its movements becoming erratic, hydraulic fluid the color of embers leaking from severed lines. But the central bore, the core feeder, still pulsed with stolen geothermal energy.

"The heart!" Geralt yelled over the din. "We have to stop the feed at the source!"

The plan was insane. It was the only one they had.

Geralt charged through a hail of lashing cables, armor shrieking in protest. Oreck cleared a path with sweeping, desperate blows. Kyh portaled him the last ten feet, directly in front of the grinding, fractal bore-ring.

Geralt didn't strike it. He offered it the Pyros Crystal.

The blazing red crystal, Vaelthane's contained ignition, flared like a newborn star.

The Worm, sensing a massive, pure source of thermal energy, did what it was designed to do. It consumed. The bore-ring dilated, and a cable-tendril snatched the crystal from Geralt's hand, retracting it into the core feeding chamber.

For one second, silence.

Then, a light brighter than the sun bled from every seam in the Worm's body.

Vaelthane's regulated ignition met the Worm's chaotic consumption. The result was a thermodynamic catastrophe contained within a living shell.

The Worm convulsed, a final, apocalyptic spasm. The subsonic scream peaked and then cut off.

The blockage was gone.

A torrent of pent-up, clean geothermal energy surged through the reopened artery. The chamber shook, not with collapse, but with release. The sound was a planet sighing in relief.

Then the ceiling, destabilized by the fight and the sudden energy surge, began to go.

"RUN!" Kyh screamed, his eyes wide with terror.

There was no path back. Only down.

Kyh threw his hands wide. Shadows did not form a door—they tore a wound in reality itself. A ragged, screaming portal leading not sideways, but up, through solid rock, following the path of the new thermal flow.

They leaped through as the chamber imploded behind them. It was a nightmare journey of flashing rock, blinding heat, and Kyh's desperate, sobbing breaths as he held the disintegrating portal open.

They were vomited out onto the scorched surface of the caldera, rolling across hot ash as, behind them, the entire maze network collapsed in a final, settling roar of dust and steam.

ACT 5: RETURN TO COIL

They stumbled, half-blind and burned, back to the Spire chamber. Coil's rotation, which had been a frantic wobble, had smoothed to a steady, graceful turn.

His voice, when it came, was the clear harmonic of a functioning machine. "I feel it. The flow is restored. Pressure is stabilizing. The planetary systems are… recalibrating. The fever breaks."

The light in the chamber softened. The hairline fractures in the walls ceased weeping molten light.

"The planet will survive," Coil stated. A pause, a flicker of something deeply personal in his facets. "Thank you. I was… I was never meant to do this alone."

Luminaire's presence warmed in Geralt's mind, weary but satisfied. "The Spire is stable. Returning to managed dormancy. The war above continues, but Pyroclast… Pyroclast will not die today."

Outside, with a gust of wind and the scent of ozone, Aelxion landed. The Warden of Breach looked at them, then at the now-gentle pulse of the Spire.

"The worm's death bought us time," he rumbled. "Not peace. The Anunnaki will dig elsewhere. The dragons will still fight. But time… time is the rarest currency in a war. You have given us a little more."

Geralt turned to Kyh, who was leaning against a wall, shaking from exhaustion. "You're with us now?"

Kyh looked at his hands, still faintly trailing shadow-smoke. He looked back the way they'd come, toward the war he was leaving. "I walked the Spire's paths for months without knowing what they were. I used them to save a handful. Today, I used them to save a world." He met Geralt's gaze, his twilight eyes finding a hard resolve. "Now I know what they are. And I know what they're for. Yes. I'm with you."

ACT 6: DEPARTURE

A soft, silver-gold light coalesced in the center of the chamber. Luminaire's emergency gate, powered by the last of her reserves and the newfound stability of the Pyroclast node.

Kyh hesitated at the threshold, looking back one last time at the burning sky of his home.

Oreck placed a heavy hand on his shoulder. "You're not leaving them to die. You're going to help cut the disease out of the network that's making this war inevitable. That's how you save them all."

Kyh took a deep, shuddering breath, and nodded.

They stepped through the gate.

Pyroclast vanished behind them, still a world at war, still a landscape of fire and ash, but now, beneath the chaos, a living heart beat steadily once more.

Partial victory.
Two spires stabilized.
Three to go.
A new navigator acquired.
The real enemy still hidden in the data-streams, watching, waiting.

The campaign continued.
