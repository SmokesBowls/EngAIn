The Sage Saga: Book 1 Canon & Continuity Guide

1.0 Introduction: Purpose of this Document

This document serves as the definitive and authoritative canon guide for the events, beings, and principles established in The Garden of Genesis, the first volume of the Sage Saga. Its purpose is to ensure absolute continuity in all subsequent works set within this universe. All future narratives—including sequels, prequels, and ancillary materials—must adhere strictly to the foundational truths, character histories, and world mechanics detailed herein. This guide is the single source of truth for the saga's foundational era.

The core premise of Book 1 is encapsulated by its defining axiom: “Before humanity, there was memory. Before memory, there was the Garden.” It is a creation myth chronicling the aftermath of a cosmic cataclysm, the birth of a new world, and the first fraught interactions between two nascent species—one born of light and purpose, the other of stone and trauma.

To maintain narrative integrity, it is essential to begin with the universe's foundational history. The following sections detail the cosmological canon that precedes and directly influences all events within The Garden of Genesis.

2.0 Cosmological & Historical Canon

A thorough understanding of the saga's deep history is of paramount strategic importance. These cosmic events are not mere backstory; they are the active cause of the primary conflicts and character motivations that drive the narrative of Book 1. The destruction of Tiamat and the subsequent evolution of the Pelagor directly inform the Giants' inherited trauma, which is the central obstacle that must be overcome.

The following timeline establishes the unalterable sequence of foundational events.

* The Primordial State: In the time before the cataclysm, two worlds existed in harmony: Mars, a planet of rivers and volcanic light, and Tiamat, a vast blue ocean world. Upon Tiamat, the Aeon Keepers and their luminous kin, the Nehereti, guided the flow of creation.
* The Cataclysm: The rogue god-planet Marduk appeared, its passage through the void described as a "furious sun." It first destroyed Mars, burning it to ash, and then collided with Tiamat, shattering the planet into a storm of dust and debris.
* The Birth of Earth: The newborn planet Earth formed from the remains of Tiamat. Tiamat's heart now beats within this new, smaller world. The Akashic Records, an ethereal library containing the memory of Tiamat, settled into orbit around Earth.
* The Long Vigil: Following the cataclysm, the six Aeon Keepers retreated into the Akashic Records to preserve the cosmic infrastructure. They remained in the Ethereal Realm for a precisely recorded duration of 3,417 years, observing as Earth slowly reformed and became habitable.
* The First Coming: After approximately 3,500 years, Marduk returned. Its reappearance is considered a "crimson omen of the next reckoning," establishing a future threat that looms over the saga.

From this cosmic crucible emerged the key beings who survived the cataclysm, their very natures forged by the history that preceded them.

3.0 Principal Factions and Beings

The narrative of The Garden of Genesis is built upon the interactions of three core groups of sentient beings: the Aeon Keepers, the Neferati, and the Giants. A precise understanding of their distinct origins, intrinsic natures, and complex interrelationships is critical for maintaining narrative integrity and respecting the established character motivations.

3.1 The Aeon Keepers

The Aeon Keepers are six ethereal consciousnesses who survived the destruction of Tiamat. They are not physical beings but exist as distributed awareness within the Ethereal Realm, functioning as willing guardians of the cosmic infrastructure. Their base of operations is the Akashic Records, a "library of pure knowledge" that orbits Earth. From here, they monitor and maintain the flow of vrill between dimensions.

Key Limitation: The Aeon Keepers exist without physical form and cannot directly intervene in the physical world. Their influence is limited to observation and sending faint, harmonic impressions through the vrill currents. The "Prime connection" they establish with Senareth is the primary example of their indirect guidance, offering encouragement and clarification but never direct control.

3.2 The Neferati

The Neferati are a race of one thousand beings manifested directly by the Aeon Keepers from "thought-seeds" and vrill. They were created to serve as ground-level intermediaries on the newly formed Earth.

* Physical Characteristics: Humanoid in form, their bodies are composed of crystallized vrill, giving their skin a natural bioluminescence. As newly manifested beings, they experience physical sensations like weight, temperature, and gravity with overwhelming and often disorienting novelty.
* Core Purpose: The Neferati were created to be autonomous guides for the Giants. Their mission is to teach the Giants how to refine their innate abilities through cooperation, not to rule or control them. The Aeon Keeper Lyaris explicitly crafted them with "curiosity" and "compassion" to facilitate this goal.
* Key Individuals:
  * Senareth: Designated Prime upon manifestation, serving as the leader, coordinator, and primary conduit for the Aeon Keepers' faint impressions.
  * Elyraen: Possesses a deeply analytical and observant nature, focused on data collection and understanding causality.
  * Vairis: Embodies a practical and pragmatic worldview, focused on immediate needs like shelter and defense.
  * Olythae: An artistic consciousness who uses the creation of patterns in sand and stone as a primary form of communication.
  * Kyreth: A gentle and patient being who successfully made first contact with the Giant Torhh through the shared manipulation of vrill in water.
* Crucial Ancestral Distinction: The newly created Neferati must not be confused with their ancestors from Tiamat, the Nehereti. The source texts establish that the Nehereti harassed and drove the ancient Pelagor from land. This history is the source of the Giants' inherited trauma and is a canon fact of critical importance.

3.3 The Giants (Transformed Pelagor)

The Giants are the descendants of the Pelagor, a sentient, octopus-like species that inhabited the oceans of Tiamat. Following the cataclysm, surviving Pelagor retreated into deep thermal vents, where they evolved over more than 3,000 years into their current form.

* Physical Characteristics: Giants are massive beings, standing between 20 and 45 feet tall. Their bodies are composed of living stone and crystallized minerals, granting them immense strength. They possess powerful, albeit nascent and unrefined, geomantic abilities, allowing them to manipulate earth and water through consciousness.
* Geomantic Tribes: The Giants have diversified into distinct groups based on their elemental adaptations:
  * Jade-Greens: Descended directly from the sea-born Pelagor; carry the deepest ancestral trauma. (Disposition: Watchful, traumatized, hesitant).
  * Burnt-Reds: Adapted to desert environments, with bodies resembling weathered sandstone. (Disposition: Unafraid, curious, pragmatic).
  * Charcoal-Greys: Composed of volcanic rock. (Disposition: Observant, fascinated by Neferati phenomena).
  * Frost-Veined: Mountain-adapted Giants whose bodies are striated with patterns resembling snow and ice. (Disposition: Ancient, wise, open to parley).
  * Copper-Greys: Associated with underground minerals and caves. (Disposition: Creative, engaged in collaborative art).
  * Green-Browns: Forest-adapted beings whose forms incorporate moss and plant life. (Disposition: Creative, engaged in collaborative art).
* The Source of Fear (Canon Directive): The fear exhibited by the jade-green Giants toward the Neferati is the central conflict of Book 1. It is an inherited, biological memory of being harassed, chased, and driven from the shores of Tiamat by the Neferati's ancestors, the Nehereti. This trauma is not a misunderstanding; it is a historical fact encoded into their very being. (Canon Directive): All future narratives must treat this trauma not as a simple misunderstanding to be overcome, but as a foundational, biological truth for the jade-green Giants. It is a legitimate historical wound, and its healing must be portrayed as a slow, complex process of earning trust, never as a quick fix. To downplay this history is to violate the core conflict of the saga's foundational text.

The very substance of their reality, and the means by which they interact, is governed by a set of inviolable metaphysical principles.

4.0 Metaphysical & Arcane Principles

The "magic" of the Sage Saga is not arbitrary but is a function of a structured, physics-based system. Adherence to these principles is paramount for maintaining the plausibility and internal logic of the world. All arcane effects must be rooted in these foundational concepts.

4.1 Vrill

Vrill is the fundamental cosmic and magical energy that flows between dimensions and connects all things. It is a substance of pure potential that can be shaped by consciousness. The Neferati were formed from vrill, and the Giants can instinctively manipulate it to affect their environment. It is the underlying medium for all "magical" phenomena.

4.2 The Dimensional Structure

The cosmos is composed of several distinct but interconnected realms and locations.

* The Ethereal Realm: A higher dimension of pure consciousness, existing "between thought and form." It is the domain of the Aeon Keepers.
* The Physical Realm: The lower-frequency world of matter, weight, and linear time, where Earth is located.
* The Veil: The dimensional friction-space between the Ethereal and Physical realms. It is here that the pressure of "higher and lower vibrations grinding against each other" compresses thought into tangible form, as seen in the manifestation of the Neferati.
* The Akashic Records: A vast, ethereal library that serves as the base of operations for the Aeon Keepers. It orbits the Earth in the Ethereal Realm and contains the "memory of a world undone."

4.3 Consciousness-Touch

Consciousness-Touch is the primary method for manipulating vrill and, by extension, physical reality. It is an act of focused intention that allows both Neferati and Giants to shape elements. This is demonstrated when Kyreth creates whirlpools in the water and when Giants shape sand into intricate patterns without physical contact. It is the fundamental mechanism of all active magic in this era.

These inviolable principles govern every interaction within the saga, culminating in the precise state of the world at the conclusion of Book 1.

5.0 State of Play: End of "The Garden of Genesis"

This section summarizes the established political, social, and relational statuses at the conclusion of The Garden of Genesis. It serves as the mandatory starting point for any narrative set immediately after the events of Book 1. There is no alternative starting point; this is the established truth of the world at this moment.

The resolution of The Convergence event has reshaped the world, leaving a complex and fragile balance of power and trust.

1. A Fragile Truce: Senareth's act of apology to the Giant Torhh has successfully cracked the wall of inherited fear, but it has not erased it. Trust has been initiated but is far from being fully earned. The historical trauma of the jade-green Giants remains a significant, unresolved tension.
2. A Divided Giant Society: The various Giant tribes are not unified in their response to the Neferati. The mountain (frost-veined), desert (burnt-red), underground (copper-grey), and forest (green-brown) Giants have responded with curiosity and are willing to engage in collaborative creation. The jade-green Giants, however, remain separate and watchful. While Torhh has expressed a willingness to trust, their people as a whole are undecided and are still processing their ancestral trauma.
3. The Neferati Mission: The Neferati's original purpose of simple "guidance" has fundamentally shifted. Their mission is now a more complex process of demonstrating trustworthiness, making amends for ancestral harm, and fostering collaborative creation as a means of healing deep-seated fear.
4. The Foundation of the Garden: The philosophy of cooperation has successfully replaced the potential for conquest. However, the "Garden" is merely a foundation, not a completed structure. The real, long-term work of building a unified, multi-species civilization has only just begun.

This state of play is a direct result of the saga's core philosophical underpinnings, which must remain consistent in all future stories.

6.0 Inviolable Thematic Pillars

Beyond plot points and lore, the Sage Saga is defined by a set of core philosophical themes. These themes are the narrative's soul and must remain central to all future storytelling to maintain the series' unique identity and emotional resonance.

* Cooperation Over Conquest: The primary narrative engine of the saga is the building of alliances, not the winning of wars. Progress is achieved through mutual understanding, shared creation, and partnership, as explicitly stated by the goal to lay the Garden's foundation on cooperation.
* The Weight of Memory and Trauma: History is not static backstory; it is an active, living force that shapes present-day fears, prejudices, and motivations. The Giants' fear is the primary example of this, demonstrating that ancestral wounds must be acknowledged and addressed before true progress is possible.
* Creation Through Vulnerability: True progress and trust are achieved not through demonstrations of power, but through acts of vulnerability, patience, and apology. Senareth kneeling before Torhh is the quintessential act of Book 1, proving that acknowledging harm is a greater strength than denying it.

These pillars are the essential DNA of the Sage Saga and must inform all creative decisions moving forward.
