**Chapter 9: Stalemate and Departure. The First Coming**

**Year 87, Month 11, Day 22 - Southern Nexus Site, Construction Assessment**

The needled pushed against the sky, three hundred feet of crystalline cruelty. It was functional, maybe even pretty in a cold, geometric way, but it was also a failure.

Not a catastrophic one. Enlil allowed himself a moment of professional bitterness to agree with that. They had beaten the schedule. The structure’s lattice held; it channeled vrill-energy with a sixty-percent efficiency that engineers would have killed for. In a vacuum, in a peaceful star system, they would have walked away with a win.

But they didn't have a vacuum. They had thirteen cycles.

Orbital mechanics were absolute; Nibiru was a predator moving through the throat of a giant. In thirteen cycles, the gravitational drag would become prohibitive. They couldn't stay; they couldn't even linger. To push the vessel past the next convergence point would require the energy reserves to pump the shields, reserves the Engineers were saving for atmospheric entry.

"Give me the map," Enlil said, his voice cutting through the hum of the maintenance drones. He didn't need to look at the console. He knew the numbers would be ugly.

The holographic projection winked into life, pulling up the fractal geometry of the five remaining sites.

*Northern (Tidecaller Mountain):* Needle at seventy-three percent. Tribe interference high, but the Igigi were holding the line. *Trouble.*
*Eastern (Highland Peak):* Needle at fifty-eight percent. The Mountain Giants were... annoyingly sophisticated. They were trying to read the blueprints with their minds, creating resonance loops that destabilized the foundations. Enlil had lost eight Reptilians to that mountain. *Extreme trouble.*
*Southern (Desert Convergence):* Needle at sixty-one percent. They were bleeding out. Desert Giants were refusing to be herd, attacking the drones with primitive but fervent desperation. Reptilian casualties had stabilized at a grim lethality rate. *Unacceptable.*
*Western (Forest Valley):* Needle at forty-four percent. The Forest Giants were using the jungle against them. Living vines, mind-controlled aphids—it was a mess. *Indeterminate.*
*Central (Primary Convergence):* Needle at seventy-one percent. The site of the worst heat. The tribal unity there was a problem, but Enlil could fix tribal unity with a neutron pulse if he had to. *Manageable, but exhausting.*

"Conclusion?" Enlil tapped the edge of the console.

"We cannot finish the network," the tactical AI replied in Enlil’s head. "Sixty-five percent average completion. Too low for a full Second Coming deployment. If we leave, the indigs will cannibalize the tech."

"And if we stay?" Enlil asked.

"Rate of decay increases exponentially. We would be fighting a guerilla war for four more cycles, then abandoning the infrastructure in a rage. We win the war by losing the battle. We keep the siege."

Enlil stared at the hologram. The logic was sound. It was elegant. It was exactly what Eduhauana would want.

"Flag all sites for immediate entrapment," Enlil ordered. "Sensors up, drones on hot standby. We are turning the planet into a trap. We leave a skeleton crew of Reptilians—enough to keep the infrastructure humming, not enough to police the natives. We pull the brainy stuff: the Igigi keep breeding, the Reptilians keep order. We wipe the consciousness of the leaders who are smart enough to organize."

"And the psychological warfare?"

"We put it on autopilot," Enlil said, staring out over the desert where the Rebellion was digging in for a long winter. "The Queen’s work. If the indigs are too stupid to hate each other, we’ll help them learn. If they’re too smart... well, then we've already sent the lesson home."

He punched the authorization code. The needled deepened its hum. They weren't building to finish anymore. They were building to survive.

**Year 92, Month 6, Day 3 - Northern Nexus Site, Unexpected Cooperation**

The vibration of the needle was different up here. Higher frequency, softer texture. Pazuzu stood on a scaffolding beam, his elongated fingers brushing against the rising crystals.

He felt the programming trying to awaken in the back of his mind. It was a warm hum, a suggestion. *Be suspicious. This Giant is too soft. They have forgotten what the Golden Ones can do. They are naive.*

Pazuzu swallowed the urge. He looked down.

Torhh was down there, moving with a grace that made the Shaped look like clumsy children. The Giant wasn't just stacking stone; he was coaxing it. Where Pazuzu forced the atoms to rearrange, Torhh was asking them to listen. The Ash Giant’s consciousness-touch was a warm, wet thing, full of ancient water-memory and slow patience.

"I can't feel the resonance," Torhh rumbled, his voice like tectonic plates shifting. "But I can feel the intent. It’s old. Before the Golden Ones. Before the Second Coming. Someone cut these crystals. To hold energy?"

Pazuzu let the programming fade. He focused on the feeling of the stone. "Maybe. Or maybe it’s just geometry. Anunnaki are obsessed with right angles."

"Look at the pattern," Torhh said, pointing a massive finger.

Pazuzu looked. The connections didn't form a circle, which was standard for Anunnaki architecture. They formed a spiral. A spiral that mimicked the way the wind moved through the canyon. It was... beautiful. And dangerous. Beauty was always dangerous.

"It's safer," Pazuzu said quietly. "If the pressure builds, this spiral vents it. The other lines the Queen made? They just snap."

"That's why I'm doing it," Torhh said. He lifted a slab of quartz and fit it into the weave. His physical strength was immense, but his consciousness was delicate. "I'm building a vent. For the pressure building inside your people, too."

Pazuzu felt a heavy lump form in his throat. He hated that he felt things. He hated that he wasn't just a worker unit. But looking at Torhh’s massive, scarred back, the programmed hatred for Giants flickered and died.

"They're leaving," Pazuzu said. "The math. The orbital shift. They won't be here."

Torhh hesitated, the vibration of the stone stopping for a second. "When?"

"Soon. A hundred cycles."

"Then what?" Torhh turned slowly, his ocean-deep eyes locking onto Pazuzu. "We keep working? We maintain their death machines while they float away?"

Pazuzu thought of the lies. The programming whispered that Giants would just move in and destroy the Needles once the Anunnaki were gone. That the survivors would be too grief-stricken to function.

"But I know you," Pazuzu said. He stepped closer to the edge of the scaffold. "You don't seem like the type to break things when you're sad. You seem like the type to build."

Torhh’s lips pulled back, revealing the beginnings of a smile that was more like a grimace. "I remember when the sky was open. We didn't have anchors holding us down. We walked where we wanted to walk."

"And now?"

"Now we choose. The Golden Ones tried to take the choice away. They tried to say the only good path was their path. But here you are," Torhh gestured to Pazuzu, "smarter than your creators. Helping to fix a world that stole from you."

Pazuzu looked at his hands, the pale, clawed hands of the Igigi. "And you," he said. "You are choosing me. Not because you were told to. But because I asked you to."

"I chose you because I listened to the stone," Torhh said. "And it told me to share. If the Golden Ones are gone, and we have this place... can we build something that isn't a cage?"

Pazuzu looked out at the sprawling camp of Giants and Igigi below. They were all broken. The Sundering had taken its toll. They were terrified, grieving, and angry. They were exactly what Eduhauana wanted them to be.

But he also saw the spark. The memory of the Garden.

"If we build it," Pazuzu said, "it won't be for them. It will be for the ones who wake up after we are gone. To say that we didn't let them rot in the cage. That we tried."

Torhh extended his hand. Not for power, but for contact. Pazuzu took it. The biological contact sent jolts of resistance through Pazuzu’s nerve endings, a biochemical command to *flee*, but his mind overrode it.

"Then we build," Torhh said. "While the silence is still theirs."

**Year 99, Month 8, Day 14 - Crimson Citadel, Final Assessment**

The view from the bridge was vast. The blue marble hung below them, rotating with the lazy elegance of a dying world. In the distance, the Needles stood like needles of ice in a desert of gold—monuments to a conquest that was just ending, even if the war wasn't.

Queen Eduhauana stood at the observation port, her eyes unblinking. She did not look like a conqueror. She looked like a librarian closing a book.

"Report," she said. Her voice didn't carry; the ship’s systems amplified it.

"Nephilim protocols initiated," the tactical AI reported. "Garrison re-organizations complete. Biological remnants at 12%... wait," the AI corrected itself. "Twelve percent of the indigenous population has been identified as 'corrupted.' They are being purged."

Eduhauana’s eyes never flickered. "Corrupted. A technical term. A virus in the system. As expected."

"Waiting for extraction command. We have twelve cycles to clear the orbital vectors."

"Execute," Eduhauana said.

She walked away from the window. She had won. It didn't feel like a victory; it felt like an accounting. She had spent four thousand cycles here, a geological blip in her timeline, to break the unity of these creatures. She hadn't used violence to do it; she had used patience. She had left the truth behind—lies that looked like memories, distrust that felt like instinct.

The Nephilim would clean up the wreckage. The Reptilians would stay behind to secure the power grids. The Igigi would learn to hate the Giants just as the Giants had learned to hate the Igigi.

"Do you feel it?" Eduhauana asked, looking at the hologram of her son, Ninurta.

"What?" Ninurta’s face appeared on the screen, bruised and weary, his eyes holding the madness of the Engineers.

"The fracturing. The psychological dissonance. They are beginning to hate each other, Ninurta. And the best part is, they think they did it. They think the Giants are stupid and the Igigi are greedy, and that their hatred is their own."

"Efficient," Ninurta agreed. "The Dregans are proving useful. They hate themselves now. It saves us the trouble of building the hatred."

"And the Slaves?"

"The Igigi breed true. Their programming holds up under the pressure of isolation. They are learning to prioritize self-preservation over their former masters."

Eduhauana smiled. It was a thin, mathematical expression. "Then we simply walk away. Leave them in the debris of our departure. We give them a false hope. We let them think they escaped the trap."

She paused, looking at the swirling storm systems on the planet below.

"The trap isn't built of wire and stone, Ninurta. It's built of lies. By the time they realize the cage is still there, it will have rusted shut. They will have destroyed their own capacity to fight back."

"Three thousand cycles?"

"We return," Eduhauana said, her voice hardening. "And we will find them fighting each other over the scraps we left behind. We will find the tribes too fractured to see the Needle for the weapon it is. And then we will complete what we started."

"It will be... elegant," Ninurta said.

"Elegant," she echoed. "Yes. That is how we dismantle things that cannot be broken. With patience. With indistinguishable lies. With cruelty that looks like mercy."

The red alert klaxon sang out, low and mournful. The orbital restraints were disengaging.

"They are leaving," Ninurta said. "They think they won."

"They did not win," Eduhauana corrected, watching the small blue dot shrink in the viewport. "They survived. But survival is a luxury. Eventually, the predator always returns."

The Citadel moved. The gravity of the planet tugged at the hull, a tiny, annoying itch compared to the vastness of the void. Eduhauana closed her eyes and visualized the next convergence point, calculating the resources needed to dismantle another civilization. She made the universe cold again.

**Year 100, Month 1, Day 1 - Northern Nexus Site, The Morning After**

The sky was still there. The sun came up over the Needle, illuminating the crystalline lattice in a way that made it look like a cage with the bars removed.

Pazuzu stood in the command tent. It was quiet. Too quiet.

He expected a cheer. He expected a war cry. He expected the tribes to run out and punch the unconscious bodies of the Reptilian guards.

Instead, they just stood there.

Torhh emerged from the cavern mouth, looking old. His eyes held the weight of a thousand winters of suffering. The Ash Giant walked to Pazuzu’s side, and for the first time in years, the two of them stood shoulder-to-shoulder without the pressure of a command to do so.

"They're gone," Pazuzu whispered. "The machines. The commands."

"Vanished," Torhh agreed. "Just like they said they would."

Pazuzu looked at the Reptilian guards standing at the perimeter. They were just standing there. Drones. They had protocols for "Retreat," protocols for "Hold," but no protocol for "Free."

"Do you remember the Garden?" Pazuzu asked.

"I remember," Torhh said.

"We didn't have Needles then. We didn't have math or vrill-energy or stars that hung in the sky like judgment. We just had the dirt and the sky and each other."

"And the Neferati," Torhh added. "They were there too."

"And now?"

"Look around, Pazuzu," Torhh said. The Giant swept his massive arm across the horizon. "They are still here. The Giants are grieving, the Igigi are confused, the Reptilians are waiting for orders. The world is broken. Just like we thought."

"But the cage is open," Pazuzu said. "Or at least, the door is unlocked."

"Is it?" Torhh asked. "You still carry the thoughts. The whispers. 'They are watching.' 'They will return.' 'They are smarter than us.'"

Pazuzu flinched. "I don't tell myself that."

"Did you stop sending the broadcasts, Pazuzu?"

Pazuzu looked down at his hands. "No. I was just reporting the status. For efficiency."

"And the Giants? Do they suspect you?"

"They suspect me," Pazuzu admitted. "They look at me, and they see an Igigi. A slave. They think I am the enemy."

"Then the cage hasn't opened."

Pazuzu sighed, a sound that rattled in his chest. "But we didn't die, Torhh. They left. They thought they broke us."

"And we did," Torhh said grimly. "But we didn't break completely. Look."

Torhh pointed to the Igigi camp. A group of them were gathering stones near the base of the Needle. Not to attack, not to destroy, but to build. A small shelter.

"They are building something," Pazuzu observed.

"A shelter," Torhh said.

"Or a tomb?" Pazuzu suggested. "A monument to what we were told to build?"

Torhh looked at him, and the giant’s eyes held that terrifying, dangerous hope again. "Or a library. A place to store the memories the Queen wanted to erase. Where we remember that we *could* have been friends."

Pazuzu looked at the Needle—a dark, sharp spike of alien tyranny that still pointed toward the sky, demanding worship.

"They won't come back," Pazuzu said. "Math says they won't."

"They have a First Coming," Torhh countered. "They have a Second. And a Third."

"Then we need a Fourth," Pazuzu said, the words striking him with sudden, terrifying clarity. He looked at Torhh, really looked at him. "A time when we aren't running. When we aren't hiding. When we stand as one."

Torhh nodded. "We will need help. The Giants are divided. The reptilians are broken. The Igigi are confused."

"We are all in the same boat," Pazuzu said. "Pulled by the same water. Carrying the same poison in our blood."

"And we want to be something different?"

"We have to be," Pazuzu said. "Not because they want us to. Not because they programmed us to. But because the alternative... the alternative is just silence."

Torhh extended his hand again.

Pazuzu took it. The biological alarm blared in his head—*BONDING ALERT*. But he ignored it. He leaned into the hug, the fragile union of two species bound together by trauma, a refusal to let the Anunnaki's cruelty be the final word.

"Then we build," Torhh rumbled. "When the silence is full, we fill it with noise. We speak the First Tongue until it is the only one anyone understands."

"It will take a long time," Pazuzu said. "The poison needs to settle."

"Then we wait," Torhh said. "Three thousand years. Whatever time it takes."

The sun rose higher, bathing the desolate landscape in harsh, white light. The Noisy Gang was still there. The broken people were still there. And somewhere in the shadows of the Needle, a seed of something new—something that looked nothing like Anunnaki geometry—was beginning to grow.

**EPILOGUE**

The deep places beneath Ironspire did not keep secrets. They preserved the trauma and the triumph alike.

In the archives of the Stone-Singers, a record entry stood out:

*Year 100. The Day the Golden Ones left.*
*They thought we were broken. They thought our unity was a tumor to be excised by our own hands.*
*But we did not tear each other apart. We folded instead. We became tighter. We became more honest about our hate, and more desperate for our love.*
*In the silence that followed, we learned to listen. Not to the vrill-energy of the Needles, but to the voices of one another.*

The Queen looked at this record from the distant stars, three thousand years in her future. She calculated the probability of its accuracy: 98.4%. The division was inevitable; the hatred was permanent.

She approved the elimination of the Stone-Singers.

But in that future, the survivors knew what the Queen didn't. That in the vast, cold mathematics of the universe, grief was not an equation with a zero sum.

*Somewhere, the poison was being mixed into antidote.*

*The Second Coming was coming. And the world would not be what she planned it to be.*
