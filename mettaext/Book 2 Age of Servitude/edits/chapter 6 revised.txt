### **Chapter 6: The First Coming**

## **Phase 1: The Harvest Begins**

**Year 0, Month 1, Day 1 - Crimson Citadel, High Orbital Position**

The planet turned beneath them like a jewel carved from water and stone, its two continental masses rising from oceanic blue like the backs of sleeping titans. Queen Eduhauana stood before the observation aperture—not window, for the Crimson Citadel did not require such primitive interfaces—and felt the planetary vrill-signature resonating through crystalline decks that had witnessed the birth and death of civilizations.

"Analysis," she commanded, her voice a harmonic that bent local physics to attention.

The tactical consciousness that managed orbital operations manifested as geometric patterns in the air before her—compressed probability rendered visible through techniques that predated most species' understanding of matter.

"Primary Dominion designation confirmed," the patterns spoke, their voice like mathematics given sound. "Planetary vrill-density exceeds projections by fourteen percent. Seven primary convergence points detected. Indigenous biological presence confirmed at moderate cooperative development."

Eduhauana’s golden skin, terrible in its beauty, caught the alien sun. She had ruled star systems when this world was still molten. Had shaped the genetic destinies of species that no longer remembered their own names.

This world was a resource. Its inhabitants were data points in an extraction algorithm.

"Indigenous capability assessment," she continued, her mind already calculating resource extraction timelines across multiple probability matrices.

"Biological survey identifies two primary species classifications. First: massive stone-based entities demonstrating geomantic consciousness-touch capabilities. Estimated population: four thousand. Second: smaller bio-luminescent forms showing advanced vrill-manipulation techniques. Estimated population: twelve hundred. Both species demonstrate symbiotic cooperation patterns. Technological development: primitive construction, no metallurgy, no energy weapons, no orbital capacity."

"Threat level?"

"Minimal. Stone-entities possess physical strength and vrill-conductivity but lack coordinated military structure. Luminescent forms show sophisticated consciousness-crafting but minimal defensive applications. Neither species demonstrates awareness of our presence. Recommended containment protocols: standard biological management with optional selective harvesting for vrill-extraction."

Eduhauana allowed herself a moment of satisfaction. Reconnaissance was accurate. This world was rich, its populations manageable.

"Deployment authorization. Standard extraction configuration. Ground operations: infrastructure establishment, workforce deployment, network construction. Timeline: complete primary objectives within one hundred solar cycles."

"Acknowledged. Dispatching Overseer Council for ground coordination. Initiating manufacturing protocol. Preparing Igigi transport vessels and Reptilian genesis facilities."

The Queen turned from the aperture, her presence making the air dense with consequence. "And the indigenous biologicals?"

"Standard protocol: monitor, assess, contain if interference occurs. Harvest if vrill-extraction becomes necessary. Eliminate only if direct threat to construction objectives is confirmed."

"Efficient."

The Crimson Citadel began its slow descent toward optimal orbital position. Across its vast interior, manufacturing systems awakened. Transport pods prepared for atmospheric entry. And in the genesis chambers deep within the Citadel's core, Reptilian embryos began their accelerated development in crystalline wombs filled with processed Earth biology.

The First Coming had begun.

**Year 0, Month 1, Day 1 - The Garden, Island Nexus Point**

The unnatural night had lasted for weeks. Not darkness, but a **void**—a sucking absence in the sky where stars and resonance should be.

Senareth stood at the central Nexus, hands pressed against the singing stone, and felt nothing. Not the gentle pulse of planetary vrill, nor the distant, comforting hum of the Prime connection. Only a silence so complete it was a sound in itself.

*They have severed us,* Elyraen sent, their mental voice thin with a terror no Neferati was designed to feel. *The Keepers... the network... it's being throttled. As if a hand has closed around the world's throat.*

A tremor shook the island—not geological, but **manufactured**. A harmonic wrongness that made the Tidecaller mountain's water channels scream and several of the smaller singing obelisks crack. From the north, where the first geometric horror had touched down, a wave of cold rolled across the land, killing the vibrant moss on the forest Giants and frosting the sand.

Torhh's massive form emerged from the tree line, their jade-green skin dulled. "The stone is afraid," the Giant rumbled, the words ground out like grinding continents. "It feels... command. Not request. Not communion. It is being *told* what to be."

Senareth looked at their hands, once conduits for a gentle, cosmic will. Now they were just... hands. The Prime connection was a severed limb, phantom pain flaring where guidance should be.

"This is not a battle for territory," Senareth said, their voice hollow in the new silence. "It is a war for reality itself. They are not here to share our world. They are here to unmake it and build their own from the pieces."

Around them, the Garden held its breath. The 500-year song had ended. The first note of the dirge had been struck.

**Year 0, Month 2, Day 15 - Landing Site Gamma, Northern Continental Mass**

Overseer Marduk stood at the edge of what would become the primary manufacturing complex and calculated efficiency parameters.

The site was optimal. Distant from the largest indigenous population concentrations. Proximate to required mineral deposits. Adequate water sources. Defensible terrain.

His form was typical of Anunnaki overseers—twelve feet of golden-skinned perfection, features that combined terrible beauty with absolute authority, eyes that reflected the cold fire of suns witnessed across eons.

Three other overseers accompanied him—Enlil, Ninurta, Ishkur. Each commanded different aspects of ground operations. Each had overseen countless extractions.

"Foundation assessment," Marduk directed.

Enlil extended consciousness-touch into the ground beneath them. Not magic as the indigenous biologicals would understand it, but applied physics so advanced it resembled divine power to primitive perception.

"Bedrock stable to depth of four hundred meters. Vrill-flow optimal for energy extraction. Geological composition compatible with Reptilian genesis requirements. No indigenous presence within fifty kilometers."

"Commence Phase One construction. Manufacturing facilities first. Worker deployment second. Network infrastructure third."

The four overseers raised their hands in perfect synchronization, and reality responded to Anunnaki will.

The ground didn't just move—it reorganized. Stone flowed like liquid, arranging itself into foundations designed to last millennia. Mineral deposits rose from deep earth, already processed. Water diverted from underground aquifers, creating channels for life support and genesis chambers.

Within hours, the skeleton of the manufacturing complex stood. Not elegant—efficiency rarely was—but functional. Massive structures of stone and processed minerals, their surfaces covered in geometric patterns that focused vrill-energy and amplified Anunnaki will.

By the end of the first month, the complex was complete.

Genesis chambers lined one entire wing—crystalline wombs where Reptilian embryos developed at accelerated rates, their genetic code derived from Earth's indigenous reptiles but enhanced for labor, warfare, and obedience.

Breeding facilities occupied another wing—vast chambers where Igigi would reproduce under controlled conditions.

Command centers formed the complex's heart.

And deep beneath, hidden from all but Anunnaki perception, lay the vrill-extraction infrastructure. Should the indigenous biologicals prove useful for energy harvesting, the facilities were ready. Should they prove troublesome, the facilities were ready for that too.

"Status report to Citadel command," Marduk instructed once construction completed. "Manufacturing complex operational. Ready for workforce deployment."

The response came immediately through consciousness-channels: "Acknowledged. Igigi transport vessels dispatched. Estimated arrival: six solar cycles. Reptilian genesis: embryonic development proceeding. First generation maturation: forty-five solar cycles."

Marduk allowed himself a moment of satisfaction. Everything proceeding according to plan. Everything efficient.

He did not think about the indigenous biologicals. They were resources to be managed, not persons to be considered. The mathematics of power were absolute.

**Year 0, Month 3, Day 21 - Igigi Transport Vessel, Atmospheric Entry**

Pazuzu pressed his elongated form against the transport pod's interior wall and tried not to calculate the probability of survival.

The mathematics were not encouraging.

"Calculations will not change our trajectory," Ishara observed from the adjacent restraint station. Her bio-luminescent skin flickered with patterns that would have been beautiful under other circumstances. Now they simply marked her fear.

"Calculations are all we have," Pazuzu replied, though his voice carried none of the authority he'd once possessed as Elder of the Igigi councils.

Around them, hundreds of other Igigi waited in various states of resignation. They had been selected based on genetic diversity and reproductive capacity—breeding stock, essentially.

The pod shuddered as it struck atmosphere.

"I can see it," Ishara whispered, extending her perception through the pod's sensory systems. "Water and stone. Green growing things. It's... beautiful."

Pazuzu extended his own perception. She was right. The planet carried a richness that their former homeworld had possessed before the Anunnaki stripped it to bedrock.

And somewhere down there, indigenous biologicals lived their lives unaware that everything was about to change.

"Do you think they'll fight?" Ishara asked quietly. "The natives?"

"The mathematics suggest they lack the capability," Pazuzu responded, though his heart hoped otherwise. "But hope operates outside mathematical certainty. Perhaps they will surprise us all."

The transport pod's deceleration systems engaged with brutal force. When the pods finally settled and the hatches opened, Pazuzu's first breath of Earth's atmosphere carried scents he couldn't identify. Growing things. Water. Stone warmed by an alien sun. And underneath it all, a resonance, a sense that this world was alive in ways their former home had never been.

Anunnaki overseers waited outside, their golden forms terrible in the morning light. Consciousness-commands flooded through established channels, directives that bypassed choice.

*Proceed to breeding facilities. Undergo population assessment. Begin reproductive protocols. Await work assignments.*

The Igigi moved as directed. Pazuzu walked with the others, his consciousness processing the manufacturing complex's layout, the distant mountains where indigenous civilizations might dwell, the terrible beauty of a world about to be harvested.

And deep in his mathematical mind, he began calculating. Not probabilities of survival—those were already known. But probabilities of rebellion. Of escape.

The mathematics were not encouraging.

But mathematics, as he had learned across centuries of slavery, were not everything.

**Year 1, Month 7, Day 3 - Genesis Chamber Complex, Landing Site Gamma**

Overseer Ninurta stood before the first mature Reptilian and assessed its design with clinical precision.

The creature stood nine feet tall on reverse-articulated legs, its body covered in scales that shifted from deep green to mottled brown. Claws capable of rending stone. A ridge of bone protecting an enlarged cranial cavity.

Most importantly, the genetic modifications ensuring absolute obedience were functioning within acceptable parameters.

"Cognitive assessment," Ninurta commanded.

The Reptilian's eyes—vertical slits that could see across multiple wavelengths—focused on him with appropriate deference. Its consciousness was simple but sufficient. It could understand orders. Apply violence. Recognize threats when programmed.

"Command structure integration." Ninurta extended consciousness-touch into the Reptilian's neural pathways. The creature's mind opened without resistance. "Acknowledged. Overseer authority recognized. Awaiting directives."

Perfect.

"Deploy first generation to security positions," Ninurta instructed the chamber's tactical consciousness. "Assign patrol routes. Interface with Igigi workforce coordinators. Prepare combat protocols should indigenous biological interference occur."

The Reptilians moved with impressive coordination despite their youth, their genetically-enhanced instincts allowing them to organize into units without extensive training. They were not intelligent in the way Igigi were intelligent. But they could follow orders. They could apply violence efficiently.

They were tools. And tools should perform their function without complaint or hesitation.

**Year 2, Month 4, Day 12 - Breeding Facilities, Landing Site Gamma**

Ishara held her newborn and tried not to calculate the probability that this child would live to adulthood in freedom.

The mathematics were not encouraging.

The breeding facilities were efficient. Clean. Well-maintained. The Igigi were fed adequately, housed in spaces that mimicked their former homeworld's parameters, given medical care that prevented disease from reducing population numbers.

They were treated with the same care one might give valuable breeding animals. Which, from the Anunnaki perspective, was exactly what they were.

"She's beautiful," Pazuzu said, though his tone carried the weight of grief. He had become something like a leader among the Igigi—not through designation, but through the quiet authority of age and wisdom. "What will you name her?"

"Naming feels like hope," Ishara replied, her bioluminescent patterns dim with exhaustion. "And hope is dangerous here."

"Hope is all we have."

Around them, hundreds of other Igigi families existed in various states of reproduction. The Anunnaki had calculated optimal population growth rates. Pairs were selected based on compatibility matrices. Reproduction was scheduled according to labor requirements.

Love, when it occurred, was incidental to the mathematics.

"They're preparing work assignments," Pazuzu continued quietly, reading the shifts in the facility's operational frequencies. "Seven sites identified across the planet. They're calling them... Needle Points. Convergence Locations."

"What will they have us build?" Ishara asked, though she already suspected.

"Something that will break this world," Pazuzu replied. "Something that will transform it from what it is into what they need it to be. The same thing we built on our world."

He was silent for a moment, processing the cold equations. Then: "But there are others here. Indigenous biologicals. I've calculated their probable capabilities based on the planetary vrill-signatures I can sense. They're powerful, Ishara. More powerful than we were."

"Will they fight?"

"Mathematics suggests they will resist. Whether that resistance proves sufficient..." He shrugged, the gesture carrying centuries of defeat. "But perhaps. Perhaps these natives will surprise us."

Ishara looked down at her newborn. "I will name her Layla. After the ancient word for 'night' in a language our people spoke before the Anunnaki. Because she was born in darkness, but perhaps she will live to see dawn."

Pazuzu placed his hand on Ishara's shoulder—a gesture that connected them to who they had been before they became livestock. "Then let us hope, Ishara. Hope that Layla lives to see freedom. Hope that somewhere in the mathematics of the universe, there exists a probability branch where slavery ends."

Outside, Reptilian guards marched their patrol routes. Anunnaki overseers coordinated construction timelines. High above, Queen Eduhauana calculated resource extraction rates and approved deployment protocols for the network infrastructure that would soon be built across this small blue world.

The harvest was proceeding according to plan.
