ACT 3: THE FIRST WHISPER

They broke camp at first light. The pressure to move east was now a physical tug, a compass needle in Geralt’s bones.

At midday, as they navigated a field of shattered megaliths, the Companion Protocol spoke. Not in a dream. In the waking air.

“The ledger-keeper’s gaze shifts.”

The voice was not sound. It was the absence of sound given meaning. It came from everywhere and nowhere. Oreck stiffened, his senses detecting the wrongness in the air.

“What?” Geralt whispered.

“Karvex was a minor function. The true Ledger is awakening. It has noted the anomaly.”
“What anomaly?”
“Me. Us. The partitioned function that should not persist. The debt it seeks to collect is the energy I borrowed to partition. The Light I spent to save this world… was never mine to give.”

The truth landed like a stone. Luminaire hadn’t just sacrificed herself. She had defaulted on a cosmic loan.

“The Federation summons is a coincidence. A temporal shadow. The real threat comes from the place of no shadow. The place before light.”

A coordinates string, not in numbers, but in sense-memory, flooded Geralt’s mind: the smell of static and cold stone, the taste of dormant electricity, the sound of perfect silence. A location. Buried.

< Cross-referencing sensory data with geological survey fragments... Match found. Designation: Forgotten Valley. Formerly: Site Prime. Anomaly: No energy signatures detectable for 800+ years. Designation: “The Ghost Spire.” >

“A Void Spire,” Geralt breathed. “You hid one. Put it to sleep.”
“It is the root of my being. The only power source that can reassemble me. It exists in a null state. Inverted. Unobservable. To awaken it is to light a beacon in the house of the Ledger.”

The choice crystallized, brutal and clear.

ACT 4: THE CALCULATION OF WAR

They huddled in the shade of the stones. Geralt laid it out for Oreck: the Void Spire, the reassembly, the beacon.

Oreck’s mind worked visibly, his chroniton-flecked eyes darting as he processed. “So. We can run from the Federation, play their political game, maybe save Ironspire for a season. Or we can race to this valley and try to wake a dead god, which will definitely bring the actual cosmic debt collectors down on our heads, possibly destroying everything anyway.”

“Yes.”
“And if we do nothing?”
“The Ledger will eventually send a function that cannot be suspended. It will repossess the Anchor. Geralt will cease. I will be catalogued. The network will destabilize. Your world’s peace was built on a defaulted loan. The payment is due.”

Oreck stared at the horizon. “My children…”
“If we awaken Luminaire fully, she might be able to… renegotiate. Protect them. She’s the original architect. She understands the rules.”
“Might.”

The armor across Geralt’s back heated, then cooled rapidly. A series of sharp, distinct vibrations tapped against his spine. It took him a moment to understand. Morse code. An old, dead language from Earth’s wars.
It tapped: .--- ..- ... - / -.. --- / .. -
JUST DO IT.

The applicator flashed. < Warning: Armor core is exhibiting heuristic independence. Suggest neural lockdown. >
“No,” Geralt said. “It’s right. We’re out of clean choices.”

He looked at Oreck. “You should go back. Protect your clan. This is my—”
Oreck’s massive hand clamped on his shoulder, cutting him off. “My clan dies if the sky falls. My mate knows this. My firstborn knows this.” He bared his teeth. “We do not hide. We dig in. We go to your valley. We wake the god. And when the debt collectors come, we show them the receipts for a universe we already paid for in blood.”

The Five Mikas made their stance known without appearing. The wind died. The air became preternaturally still and warm. The very sunlight seemed to thicken, painting the red stones in hues of cobalt, ember, and violet. They were voting. They were all in.
