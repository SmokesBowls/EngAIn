ACT 5: THE ROAD TO THE FORGOTTEN VALLEY

The journey changed. It was no longer a trek; it was a convergence.

They moved with urgent purpose. The Companion Protocol spoke rarely, but when it did, it offered guidance—a safe path through a predator’s territory, a hidden spring in the waste. It was preparing its vessel.

Geralt felt the assimilation progressing. His senses sharpened to painful clarity. He could see the heat-haze of distant creatures, hear the subsonic grind of continental plates. The world was too loud, too bright.

< Neural load exceeding safe parameters. Anchor symbiosis at 59%. Recommend enforced stasis. >
“No,” the Companion Protocol whispered, this time through Geralt’s own lips. “He must hold. The threshold is 80%. He must be strong enough to touch the Spire.”

Oreck watched him, a medic watching a fever patient. “You are becoming less you.”
“I have to,” Geralt rasped. “To make room for her.”

Two days from the projected location of the Forgotten Valley, the Federation found them.

Not with soldiers. With a single, polished diplomatic skiff that settled on the canyon floor ahead, silent as a leaf. The hatch opened, and a man in the crisp grey uniform of a Federation Remnant Emissary stepped out, hands open.

“Geralt,” the man said, his voice calm, reasonable. “Please. Let’s talk. You don’t have to do anything drastic.”

The armor across Geralt’s body went rigid, locking into full battle configuration with a sound like shattering ice. The applicator screamed in his head.
< Threat! Threat! Energy signature masked! Subject is not biological! >

The Emissary smiled, a perfect, empty expression. “Oh. The tools are clever.” Its form flickered, just for an instant, revealing something angular and made of cold, grey ledger-stone beneath.

Karvex had been a bailiff. This was an auditor.

“Run,” screamed the Companion Protocol, the stillness in Geralt’s mind breaking into a tsunami of panic and data.

Oreck didn’t hesitate. He threw a smoke-charge from his belt, grabbed Geralt, and dove off the canyon trail into the raging river below as the false Emissary’s hand extended, not to grab, but to serve papers of foreclosure that would have unmade them from history.

The cold water swallowed them, and the current swept them toward the Forgotten Valley. The chase was over.

The war for the ledger had just begun.
