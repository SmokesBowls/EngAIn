The cost was tallied in the sudden, deafening silence.

Kyh sank to the floor, trembling. The tracking glyph on his shadow remained, but its light had changed. No longer the cold blue of Zar’Vokh’s law, it had become a neutral, persistent white. A permanent fixture. He held up a hand, and the shadows that once answered his call now hesitated, repelled by the light he carried. “It’s not that I can’t hide,” he whispered, voice hollow. “It’s that the dark… doesn’t want me anymore. I’m a beacon in a world of shadows.”

Oreck stood staring at where the construct mirroring him had vanished. He looked at his cleaver, then at his own hands. The fight had not been in his muscles, but in his mind, and he felt the bruises on his spirit. The offer of perfect order had been a poison, and he’d felt its lure. His certainty, his warrior’s code, felt like a clumsy, dangerous thing.

Geralt clutched his chest. The Anchor bond was intact, but the pathway Luminaire had used—the conduit for the raw, founding Architect authority—was gone. Not damaged. Consumed. The crystallized scars on his skin had turned a dull, opaque white, like marble. They were no longer wounds; they were monuments to a power spent forever.

“You invoked the Foundational Signature,” Luminaire said, her voice soft with a sorrow beyond grief. “It was the one argument it could not compute. But the signature was a one-use key. It is spent. We cannot challenge authority on that level again.”

A low groan vibrated through the spire. Not a threat—a symptom.

SPIRE-9 CORE DIRECTIVE: NULL. JURISDICTIONAL INTEGRITY: FAILING. INITIATING NETWORK DECOUPLING PROTOCOL.

The chamber began to dissolve at the edges. The glowing legal code on the walls peeled away like old paint, evaporating into static. Zar’Vokh was not exploding. It was filing for divorce from reality. It was sealing itself off, becoming a dead circuit in the network.

“Time to go,” Geralt said, his voice rough.

Kyh, moving with the stiffness of the newly crippled, tried to open a shadow path. The attempt sputtered, shadows recoiling from his marked presence. He let out a choked sob of frustration.

“Use the light,” Oreck said suddenly, pointing to Kyh’s glowing glyph. “Don’t fight it. Push it. Use the beacon to find a path out.”

Kyh’s eyes widened. He closed his eyes, focusing not on concealing, but on projecting. The white glyph on his shadow flared. And in its harsh, unforgiving light, the crumbling architecture of the decoupling spire revealed its fractures—not as shadows, but as glaring flaws in the structure. He saw the weak points, the tremulous connections not yet severed.

“There!” he yelled, pointing to a shimmering fault line in the air. He didn’t weave a portal. He forced the fault open with a concentrated pulse of his own exposed signature. It wasn’t graceful. It was a desperate, screeching tear.

They dove through as the world behind them erased itself from the network’s ledger.
