Zar’Vokh wasn’t a planet. It was a filing cabinet for reality.

They stood in a vast, hollow planetary shell. The “ground” was a smooth, metallic alloy etched with endlessly scrolling fine print. The “sky” was a vaulted lattice of glowing blue clauses, override protocols, and sub-jurisdictions. The air hummed, and the hum had weight—it pressed against their eardrums with the density of precedent.

Sound behaved strangely. Geralt’s boot scuffed the floor, and the scrape didn’t fade. It hung in the air, a persistent auditory fact, until it was slowly absorbed by a nearby glyph.

This place remembers every mistake, Violet whispered, her harmonic voice subdued. It does not forgive.

A shape coalesced from the humming air: a humanoid form woven from glowing contract-text. A Legal Sentinel. It had no face, only a smooth plane where one should be.

It spoke. Its voice was flat, devoid of inflection, and each word landed with the finality of a stamped seal.

“STATE YOUR AUTHORITY TO OCCUPY THIS JURISDICTIONAL LAYER.”

Geralt opened his mouth, instinct driving him to declare his purpose, to challenge.

“NO!” Luminaire’s warning was a lance of pain in his mind. “Do not speak! Any declared intent becomes a binding contract! Your purpose will be adjudicated, and you will be bound to its outcome!”

He clenched his jaw shut.

The Sentinel waited. The humming grew louder.

Through Geralt, Luminaire pulsed a packet of data—not a statement, but a reference. An ancient, deprecated Architect clause: AUTHORITY PENDING REVIEW.

The Sentinel processed it. The scrolling text across its form shifted.
INTRUDER → APPEAL CASE P-7719.
RESPONSE: ESCORT TO HOLDING. AWAITING ADJUDICATION.

It didn’t attack. It turned and gestured for them to follow. They had been classified. They were no longer invaders. They were a pending administrative issue.

“Move without intent,” Kyh murmured, his eyes wide as he took in the logic-bound architecture. “Walk where it leads, but don’t mean to go there.”

It was the most mentally exhausting task Geralt had ever undertaken. Every corridor was a test. A door wouldn’t open until he consciously framed a desire to go through it. A floor-panel activated only if he acknowledged its existence as a path.

Oreck became their guide. The reptilian warrior moved with pure, unthinking physicality. He saw an obstacle, he walked around it. He saw an opening, he passed through. He didn’t choose; he acted. His mind, focused on the immediate, the tangible, was the perfect shield against the procedural traps.

Kyh used his shadow-weaving not to attack, but to bypass decision nodes. When a fork in the corridor demanded a declared destination, he’d find a sliver of architectural shadow—a place the glowing glyphs didn’t quite cover—and peel it open into a fleeting shortcut.

They reached a vast chamber where the gravity was annotated. GRAVITY VECTOR: STANDARD (JURISDICTION A-1). A bridge crossed a chasm of blinking data-streams. The sign at its head read: CROSSING REQUIRES DECLARATION OF CERTAINTY.

“We have to be sure of something to cross?” Oreck muttered.

“Not just something,” Kyh said, his face pale. “Sure of which way is down.”

The moment Kyh thought, ‘The bridge is the way,’ the world rotated. The bridge became a vertical pillar. The chasm yawned beside them. He cried out, disoriented.

Oreck, without a thought, grabbed him. His certainty was not intellectual; it was biological, gravitational. Feet on ground. Ground down. He hauled Kyh forward, and the world shuddered back to its original orientation, the bridge now horizontal before them.

“Doubt is mobility here,” Geralt realized, breathing hard. “Certainty gets you stuck.”

They crossed.

Deeper in, they encountered their first Enforcement Constructs. These were not sentinels. They were taller, denser, their forms woven from outcome-logic and historical precedent. Two blocked a narrow passage to the inner strata.

One turned its blank face to Oreck. Its voice boomed, citing law: PRECEDENT AVAILABLE: TRIAL BY COMBAT (REF: DRACONIC CODE 881.4). DO YOU ACCEPT JURISDICTION OF THE BLADE?

Oreck’s warrior spirit flared. His grip tightened on his cleaver. To be offered an honorable duel, even here… his head began to nod.

“Oreck, no!” Geralt shouted.

It was too late. The construct took the micro-gesture as assent. Bands of solid light, like paragraphs of law given form, snapped from the walls to encircle Oreck’s wrists. CONTRACT FORMED. COMBAT PARAMETERS SET.

Kyh moved. Not with a warrior’s cry, but with a weaver’s precision. He didn’t attack the construct. He slid a blade of concentrated shadow into the forming contract itself, severing the clause before it could be fully instantiated.

The light-bands shattered. Oreck stumbled back, free.

The second construct didn’t move. It simply recorded. A beam of cold light shot from its chest, not at Kyh, but at his shadow. It etched a complex, pulsing glyph into the darkness at his feet.

MARK APPLIED: UNREGISTERED ACTOR. U-779. TRACKING ACTIVE.

Kyh gasped, clutching his chest as if struck. The glyph on his shadow burned with a cold, relentless light. He looked at his hands, then at Geralt, horror dawning in his twilight eyes.

“I’m… visible,” he whispered. “I can feel it. The system sees me. It knows my shape. I can’t… I can’t hide anymore.”

The constructs stepped aside, their task complete. The path to the innermost chamber was clear.

Before them stood the final door to the Core Adjudication Layer. It was not a physical barrier, but a curtain of solid sound—the layered echoes of every verdict ever passed here, woven into a sonic wall.

And from beyond it, a voice spoke. Calm. Reasonable. Echoing not from a point, but from the walls, the floor, the air itself.

“APPEAL CASE RECOGNIZED. PRESENT YOUR ARGUMENT FOR NETWORK INTERVENTION.”

The Hybrid Observer wasn’t asking why they broke in.

It was asking them to justify their very existence.
