The silence after a Void Jump was usually brief—a heartbeat of null-space before the new world rushed in.

This time, the silence lingered.

They hung in a non-place, the Ghost Spire’s energy still crackling around them like dying lightning. Before them, instead of a world, hung a question, etched in cold, blue data-light across the void:

JURISDICTION QUERY: STATE ARRIVAL AUTHORITY FOR ZAR'VOKH (SPIRE-9).

“It knows we’re coming,” Oreck growled, his hand on his cleaver. “We haven’t even arrived.”

“Worse,” Luminaire’s voice was a tense hum in Geralt’s mind. “It has pre-emptively filed a motion of inquiry. To step onto Zar’Vokh is to accept its jurisdiction. To accept is to be bound by its verdicts.”

Geralt stared at the pulsing glyphs. They weren’t a barrier. They were a contract, waiting for his signature in the form of a single step.

“So we don’t arrive,” said a quiet voice.

Kyh floated beside him, his eyes fixed on the space between the glowing legal text. His twilight gaze saw what they didn’t: the seams in the procedural reality. “We don’t use the door. We use the gap where the door forgot to meet the wall.”

He explained the Shadow Null Ingress. Not a Void Jump to a location, but a slide through a crack in the filing system. A path that existed in the administrative silence before the first law was written.

“It will be… cold,” Kyh warned. “Not temperature. Conceptual. It is the absence of being acknowledged. Most minds fray at the edges there.”

“Do it,” Geralt said.

Kyh nodded. He didn’t open a portal. He pressed his hands against the fabric of reality and parted it, not into light or darkness, but into a profound, silent grey. A corridor of unregistered potential.

“Don’t think of a destination,” Kyh whispered, stepping through. “Think of… nowhere.”

They followed.

The sensation was instant and visceral. It was the feeling of being unmade as a fact. The Anchor bond in Geralt’s chest didn’t fade—it strained, screaming against the erasure. Luminaire hissed in psychic pain. Oreck grunted, his physicality, his sheer thereness, fighting the nullity.

And then, they were through.
