ACT 8: THE NEW BASELINE

The Guild’s healers tended to Kyh. Oreck oversaw the few slaves they’d managed to pull through the door, his presence a steady, protective rock. Kulla sat in a corner, silently repairing his damaged leg with mortar pilfered from a guild wall, grumbling about the poor quality.

Geralt stood at a crystalline window, looking out at the Earth, a blue-green gem in the black.

The Asteroid Spire was offline. A victory.
Kyh had joined the location to the network. A victory.
They had saved a handful of lives. A victory.

But the cost was etched into him. The numbness of the Anchor. The scorched feel of the Applicator in his mind. The sight of hundreds more they couldn’t save. And the certain, chilling knowledge of the Queen’s now-personal attention.

This wasn’t an end. It was a pivot. The next phase of Luminaire’s plan was now in motion. The network had its first, hard-won extension. The long, slow work of building a web to cradle a sleeping Earth could begin.

And he, Geralt, the Anchor, was now prepared for his part. The generations of solitude. The vigil in the solar cocoon. He had the discipline to endure it.

He felt a presence then, not in the room, but in the elements. A whisper in the geothermal vent’s steam, a sigh in the stone, a chime in the floating orbs of light.

The Five Mikas. They didn’t speak words. They communicated a feeling. A complex, layered emotion of acknowledgment, of sorrow for the cost, and of unwavering, elemental support. They were with him. They were in the world he was fighting for. Her legacy was alive.

He placed his hand against the cool crystal of the window.

The quiet war had begun. And he was its first, and most patient, soldier.
