ACT 5: THE BRIDGE OF BONES

The asteroid belt was not a field of rocks. It was a wound.

Geralt felt it the moment they translated from the void-jump: a psychic scream frozen in stone, a grief so vast it had crystallized into gravitational eddies and recursive time-loops. The shuttle’s sensors screamed warnings—impossible mass readings, temporal distortions, pockets of reality where physics just gave up.

And there, in the chaotic heart of it, was the Queen’s work.

It was not a clean, technological facility. It was a graft. Organic, Anunnaki-grown support structures—like metallic coral—crawled over the surface of the largest asteroid, which pulsed with a sick, violet light. This was the Amygdala Analogue. And crawling across the void between it and other fragments were lines of living light. Gravity tethers. And along those tethers, like ants on a string, were figures. Slaves. Hundreds of them, minds broken by neural spikes, their collective telekinetic will being harvested to literally drag the god-fragments closer.

“They are the mortar,” Kulla grumbled from the rear of the cabin, his clay fingers tracing stress fractures on the shuttle’s hull he seemed to sense through his feet. “Bad mortar. Full of voids and fear. It will never set right.”

“We need to get closer,” Kyh said, her eyes closed, her form blending with the shuttle’s shadow. “I can feel the network’s scream from here. The Spire is in the center of that… growth. It’s the needle stitching the madness together.”

Their stolen slaver shuttle had the correct transponder codes. They drifted, pretending to be another delivery. As they neared the “processing line,” the scale of the horror became clear. Slavers in void-suited armor guided exhausted, hollow-eyed people from a holding dock onto the tethers. 90% wouldn’t survive the psychic backlash or vacuum exposure. Their bodies were simply ejected, adding literal mass to the structure.

Oreck’s growl vibrated through the deck. “We breach the dock. We free who we can.”
“And we draw every enemy right to the slaves,” Geralt said, his voice calm with the numbness of Level III. “We must be a surgical strike. We use the bridge they’re building.”

The plan was terrifying in its simplicity. They would land on a secondary fragment. Kyh would use her shadow-weaving to camouflage them as they traversed one of the active, slave-powered gravity tethers—the most direct path to the central asteroid. They would be walking the very bridge of suffering they sought to destroy.
