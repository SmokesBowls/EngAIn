ACT 3: REGROUP — THE CALM AND THE STORM

Oreck was waiting in the Guild’s material vestibule, a place of solid stone and rushing geothermal waters. He was cleaning blood—dark, oily, not his own—from his claws. His report was terse.

“The Orion remnant is a ghost town. But their docks are not empty. A slaver convoy, using old Sirian jump-ships. They are loading ‘cargo’ in the subterranean hangars. The cargo is alive. Terrified.”

Kyh flowed from the shadows, her form coalescing. “I have been… listening. Through the network’s nascent connection here. The slavers are not just trading. They have a contract. They are delivering workers to the asteroid belt. A mining operation of impossible scale.”

“A mining operation,” Geralt repeated, the pieces clicking with dreadful synergy. “Pulling the fragments of the Shattered Mind back together.”

“The slaves are the tools,” Oreck growled. “Human bridges. They link the fragments with crude gravity tethers. The mortality rate is the point. The dead are just… discarded mass.”

The image was horrifying. It was slavery reduced to its most brutally literal form: using living bodies as literal glue to reassemble a dead god’s skull for a mad queen.

“We need that shuttle,” Geralt said, the numb certainty of his training overriding the surge of rage. “It’s our ticket in, and it’s our first strike.”

The Plan: A heist. Not a frontal assault. The slaver captain was known to be arrogant, greedy, and fond of gambling on pit-fights in the lower lunar taverns. Oreck would be the bait—a fearsome, exotic challenger. Kyh would be the shadow, infiltrating the ship’s systems. Geralt would be the anchor, applying causal inertia in tiny, crucial moments: a guard’s hesitation, a lock’s failure, a distracted glance at the wrong time.

It was a messy, dangerous, physical job. The absolute opposite of cosmic time-weaving. It was exactly what he needed.
