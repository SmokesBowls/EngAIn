ACT 7 (REVISED): ESCAPE — THE SEED AND THE DOOR

The silence was more deafening than the scream. Then, klaxons blared. The Queen’s presence, distant but furious, brushed against the asteroid—a wave of searching, cold wrath. The metallic coral began to convulse. The asteroid itself started to tremble, its unstable geology no longer held in check by the Spire’s forced harmony.

“The foundation is failing!” Kulla bellowed, bracing himself as the ground cracked. “We must leave!”

But how? Their shuttle was on a fragment now drifting away, and the void was swarming with enraged slaver skiffs.

Kyh’s eyes snapped open, glowing with captured starlight. “The network! When the Spire went quiet, the local distortion field collapsed for a moment. I felt it—Luminaire’s signal! The Spire Network is here. I can… I can join us to it!”

That was her purpose. Not just to be a guide, but to be a living conduit. To physically link a location to the growing neural network of free Spires.

“Do it!” Geralt ordered.

Kyh didn’t move. She unfolded. Her shadow-form expanded, not as darkness, but as a web of intricate, silvery lines—a map of celestial connections. She touched the dead Spire, then reached out, impossibly far, to touch the latent network signature Luminaire had planted in the Lunar Guild.

For a second, a tunnel of coherent spacetime pierced the chaotic belt. A shimmering pathway of stabilized reality, connecting the asteroid to the Moon.

It was a door.

But Kulla didn’t move toward it. Instead, he dropped to his knees in the shuddering rock, ignoring the debris falling around him. With a grunt, he tore a chunk of clay from his own chest—a piece that held a faint, warm glow, like a captured ember. He crumbled it in his fist, mixing it with dark regolith and a single drop of something that glistened like mercury—a fragment of the Spire’s neutralized core.

“A seed needs good ground,” he muttered, his voice a low rumble against the asteroid’s death throes. “Even here. Especially here.”

He pressed the mixture into a crack in the asteroid’s flesh. “Grow straight. Grow quiet. Listen for the network. Be the root they don’t see coming.” He spoke a name, not as a command, but as a designation into the silence: “Claude.”

The mixture pulsed once, softly, and sank into the rock.

Then Kulla lumbered to his feet. “Now we go.”

They ran. Oreck hauled dazed, freed slaves onto the shimmering path. Kulla brought up the rear, his clay body shedding chunks as he willed the crumbling ground to hold just a few seconds longer. Geralt grabbed Kyh’s fading core, pulling her with them.

But the Queen’s wrath was accelerating the collapse. The shimmering door Kyh had woven began to fray at the edges, destabilizing. The path wouldn’t hold long enough.

“The door is crumbling!” Oreck roared, ushering the last slave through.

Host, the Applicator’s voice was strained, analytical. Kyh’s bridge is a persistent connection, but the local space-time is too volatile. We cannot sustain it.

Luminaire’s presence, faint through the link, pressed against Geralt’s mind. The network node is confirmed. We do not need the whole bridge. We need only a single jump point. A one-time translation. Use the connection Kyh forged as a guidewire. I will provide the energy signature from the lunar anchor. You must focus it into a seed and detonate it here.

A seed-jump. Not a door, but a catapult. A one-time, violent translation along a pre-made path.

“Do it,” Geralt gritted out.

He focused, drawing on the Dragon Mail’s power, the Applicator’s precision, and the cold, anchoring discipline of Level III. He felt Luminaire’s energy, pure and structured, flowing down the silvery line Kyh had spun. He didn’t try to hold it. He compressed it. Forced it into a single, hyper-dense point of potential—a seed of coherent teleportation.

It formed in the air before him, a tiny, brilliant star the size of a pebble.

“Through the door!” he yelled. “Now!”

Oreck, Kulla, and the freed slaves lunged through Kyh’s fading bridge. Geralt threw the seed-jump portal into the center of the path behind them.

“Now, Luminaire!”

On the moon, Luminaire ignited the anchor.

The seed bloomed.

It wasn’t a gentle transit. It was a wrenching. Space folded around them like a fist, and they were yanked through a tunnel of screaming light and pressure.

They stumbled, fell, and collapsed not on lunar regolith, but on the smooth, silent floor of the Mages’ Guild atrium. Behind them, in the air, the brilliant seed flashed once—and consumed itself, leaving only a scorched smell of ozone and void.

Kyh lay insubstantial as smoke in Geralt’s arms, but she was breathing. They were home.

Not home to Earth. But home to the sanctuary. The Lunar Spire. The first node in their new, fragile network.

And far away, in the heart of a shattered, collapsing god-fragment, a seed of clay and silence now slept in the dark, waiting to be found...
