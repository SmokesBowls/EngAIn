ACT 2: THE WORM ENCOUNTER

They reached the heart of the blockage: a vast, spherical chamber that had once been a magma confluence. The walls were smooth, glassy, scarred by ancient, violent flows.

At the center, connected to the chamber by thick, pulsating umbilical cords of crystalline flesh and metallic cabling, was the Worm.

The Litho-Thanatic Bore Leviathan. It was a grotesque fusion of biology and brutal engineering.

· Segmented body the color of cooled slag.
· A carapace of interlocking, hexagonal crystal plates that glowed with stolen heat.
· No head, just a forward bore-ring lined with fractal drill-teeth, slowly grinding against the sealed primary artery.
· It made no sound but a deep, subsonic vibration—the frequency of endless, blind consumption.

It wasn't a guardian. It wasn't even truly alive. It was a machine with a hunger, an Anunnaki terraforming weapon left to gorge until the planet's heart stopped.

It sensed them. Not through sight or sound, but through a disruption in the thermal flow it was sucking dry. The bore-ring ceased its grinding. The entire, massive body oriented toward them.

The vibration changed pitch. From consumption to hostility.

It attacked. Not with rage, but with the mindless, hydraulic inevitability of a industrial press.

ACT 3: THE FIGHT - SURGICAL, NOT HEROIC

This was not a battle for glory. It was surgery in a furnace.

The chamber was their operating theater, cramped and lethally hot. The Worm was the tumor, and they had three very precise tools.

Geralt was the anvil. His dragon armor flared, absorbing and dissipating the searing radiant heat the Worm emitted. He drew its lashing, cable-like appendages, weathering blows that would have pulverized stone.

Oreck was the scalpel. He didn't roar. He calculated. He watched the rhythmic pulse of the crystal plates, finding the moment of vulnerability in the mechanical cycle. His cleaver struck not to hack, but to pry, targeting the junctures between segments, seeking to disconnect, not destroy.

Kyh was the needle. He flickered in and out of shadows, appearing at the Worm's flanks or above it. He carried no weapon but the Hydros Crystal. He would press its freezing blue light against a glowing segment, flash-cooling a plate from blinding orange to brittle, crystalline black. Then he was gone, a shadow dissolving.

Now, Green would signal.

Oreck’s cleaver would strike the blackened plate. The Terros Crystal on his belt would pulse, transmitting a shockwave of controlled fracturing. The plate would not just break—it would disintegrate into harmless gravel, revealing the pulsing, vulnerable tissue beneath.

It was a timed, silent, brutal dance. Remove the armor. Expose the weakness. Repeat.

The Worm screamed, a subsonic torrent of data-noise and agony that made their bones ache and the chamber walls weep molten glass. It thrashed, causing new cracks to race across the ceiling.

It knows it is dying, White whispered, her voice a pained gust. It is trying to take the chamber with it.

ACT 4: THE KILL

The Worm was failing, its carapace half-gone, its movements becoming erratic, hydraulic fluid the color of embers leaking from severed lines. But the central bore, the core feeder, still pulsed with stolen geothermal energy.

"The heart!" Geralt yelled over the din. "We have to stop the feed at the source!"

The plan was insane. It was the only one they had.

Geralt charged through a hail of lashing cables, armor shrieking in protest. Oreck cleared a path with sweeping, desperate blows. Kyh portaled him the last ten feet, directly in front of the grinding, fractal bore-ring.

Geralt didn't strike it. He offered it the Pyros Crystal.

The blazing red crystal, Vaelthane's contained ignition, flared like a newborn star.

The Worm, sensing a massive, pure source of thermal energy, did what it was designed to do. It consumed. The bore-ring dilated, and a cable-tendril snatched the crystal from Geralt's hand, retracting it into the core feeding chamber.

For one second, silence.

Then, a light brighter than the sun bled from every seam in the Worm's body.

Vaelthane's regulated ignition met the Worm's chaotic consumption. The result was a thermodynamic catastrophe contained within a living shell.

The Worm convulsed, a final, apocalyptic spasm. The subsonic scream peaked and then cut off.

The blockage was gone.

A torrent of pent-up, clean geothermal energy surged through the reopened artery. The chamber shook, not with collapse, but with release. The sound was a planet sighing in relief.

Then the ceiling, destabilized by the fight and the sudden energy surge, began to go.

"RUN!" Kyh screamed, his eyes wide with terror.

There was no path back. Only down.

Kyh threw his hands wide. Shadows did not form a door—they tore a wound in reality itself. A ragged, screaming portal leading not sideways, but up, through solid rock, following the path of the new thermal flow.

They leaped through as the chamber imploded behind them. It was a nightmare journey of flashing rock, blinding heat, and Kyh's desperate, sobbing breaths as he held the disintegrating portal open.

They were vomited out onto the scorched surface of the caldera, rolling across hot ash as, behind them, the entire maze network collapsed in a final, settling roar of dust and steam.
