ACT 3: VAELTHANE - CRYSTAL OF FIRE

Vaelthane was a storm of crimson scales and controlled fury.

They found him not on the ground, but in the midst of an aerial engagement, tearing through a flight of Anunnaki skirmishers. Aelxion gave a piercing shriek—a draconic battlefield signal—and Vaelthane broke away, landing before them with a wash of heat that made the air shimmer.

He was smaller than Aelxion, but radiated a more focused, dangerous energy. His eyes, the color of molten brass, scanned Geralt, and for a fleeting moment, something unrecognizable flickered in them.

“Aelxion vouches for you. And you smell of my son’s cause.” His voice was sharp, tactical, devoid of the warmth Geralt might have hoped for. “You want the Pyros Crystal.”

“We need it to reignite the core’s flow after we clear the blockage,” Geralt said. “To restart the heart.”

Vaelthane’s nostrils flared, venting smoke. “This crystal is the regulator of my internal flame. The focus of my combustion. Without it, my fire is wilder. Less efficient. I am weakened in battle.”

He looked past them, to where the war continued unabated. “You ask me to fight a war with a crippled weapon.”

“We’re asking you,” Oreck said, his voice flat, “to choose between winning a battle and saving the battlefield.”

Vaelthane’s gaze returned to Geralt. The scrutiny was intense, personal, and utterly devoid of sentimental fatherly recognition. He was assessing a tool, a variable in the equation of survival.

“You are not my son,” Vaelthane stated, the words clean and surgical. “But you carry his purpose. His… conviction.” He paused, the fire around his claws guttering slightly. “Humility is a dragon’s rarest virtue. To admit the war cannot be won by flame alone.”

He opened his jaws. From the furnace at the back of his throat, he coaxed forth not fire, but a condensation of it—the Pyros Crystal. It blazed a fierce, contained red, hovering before them. It didn’t burn; it contained the principle of ignition.

“Take it. Restart the heart. I will fight my war with tooth and claw and rage. It will have to be enough.”

He took the crystal back only long enough to breathe upon it once, a final binding, then let it float to Geralt’s hand. The moment it left his possession, the brilliant corona of heat around him dulled. He was no less deadly, but the sure, controlled focus was gone. He gave them one last, unreadable look, then launched himself back into the sky without another word, his flight just slightly less precise than before.

His fire is now a wildfire, Red observed, a note of sorrow in her crackling voice. It will burn bright, but it will burn out faster.
