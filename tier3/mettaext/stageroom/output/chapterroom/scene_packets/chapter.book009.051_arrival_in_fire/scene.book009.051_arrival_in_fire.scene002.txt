ACT 4: MEETING COIL

Aelxion cleared a brutal path to the Spire—a crystalline structure of obsidian and frozen fire at the edge of the massive central caldera. It was marred by hairline fractures weeping molten light.

Inside was an oasis of comparative calm and crushing despair.

The chamber was cooler, the air humming with a stabilizing frequency. At its center, suspended above a pool of shimmering liquid metal, rotated Aetheric Coil.

He was a geometric crystalline being, facets shifting and reflecting the chamber's light. His voice, when it came, was a harmonic layered with raw, metallic panic.

"Anchor. Luminaire. HELP."

The words were a distortion wave that made Geralt's teeth ache.

"The planet's core temperature is rising. The magma arteries are blocked. I regulate the thermal flow, but the blockage—I can't reach it. If I leave this chamber, even for a moment, the planetary systems I'm manually stabilizing collapse immediately. I am trapped. The war outside damages the crust faster than I can compensate. I am... failing."

He is the heart trying to pump through a clenched fist, Green rumbled, feeling the planetary distress. The fist is winning.

ACT 5: THE DIAGNOSIS

Luminaire extended her perception through Geralt, examining Coil's fraying network connection.

"Something is consuming the excess thermal flow," she reported, her voice tight. "Not regulating it—overfeeding. Creating pressure differentials. That's what's causing the explosive venting."

Coil's rotation hitched. "Yes. Deep in the primary caldera nexus. I feel it. An alien presence. Not draconic. Not any known species in my database. It is... hungry. And growing."

Aelxion, who had followed them in, let out a low growl. "Anunnaki seeder organism. We've suspected they planted something when they began their digs. A biological weapon to cripple the planet before full invasion. We could never confirm—the caldera maze is collapsed. Impassable."

Geralt looked from the panicking Keeper to the weary dragon. "Can we kill it?"

Coil's facets flashed. "You must. But the path is sealed. Rubble, magma flows, tectonic shifts... You would need—"

A shadow detached itself from the corner of the chamber.

ACT 6: KYH ARRIVES

A portal of seamless darkness opened, and a figure stepped through, supporting a wounded Lyrian blade-dancer.

Kyh. A Shadow Weaver.

He was young, battle-worn, his clothes simple and stained with soot and alien blood. His eyes were the color of a twilight sky, holding a weight beyond his years. He gently set the wounded Lyrian down against the wall, his movements efficient and kind.

Then he saw Geralt. Saw the faint, silver luminescence of Luminaire's presence shimmering around him. He froze.

"You're... the Anchor?"

His voice was quieter than expected. Tired.

Geralt nodded. "You've been using the spire's tendrils."

"I've been using shadow-paths to evacuate wounded from the front lines. Walking the spire's energy currents without knowing what they were." He looked at Coil, then back at Geralt, realization dawning. "I thought I was just... hiding from the war. Using old, quiet places."

"You navigate collapsed spaces," Geralt said. "You portal through solid walls."

Kyh met his gaze. There was no bravado there. Only the grim acknowledgment of a skill that had become a curse and a responsibility. "I do."

"Then we need you."

Kyh looked at the wounded Lyrian he'd brought, then at the fractured Spire, then at the distant sounds of a world dying in fire. He let out a slow breath.

"I'm already standing on the planet's arteries," he said, his voice firming. "Might as well try to clear the blockage."

He stepped forward, joining the circle. The party was formed.

In Geralt's mind, the Five Mikas stirred, their attention focusing on the new presence.

His shadow is not cold, Violet observed. It is shelter. He does not fear the dark. He uses it to protect.

Then let's begin, Geralt thought.

The mission to save Pyroclast—and its trapped, panicking Keeper—was now a race into the deepest, hottest, and most collapsed maze on the planet, guided by a Shadow Weaver who knew how to walk through walls.
