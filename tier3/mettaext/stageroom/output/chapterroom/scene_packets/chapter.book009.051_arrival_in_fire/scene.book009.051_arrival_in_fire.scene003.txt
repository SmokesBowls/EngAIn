Epic 2 : THE THREE CRYSTALS

ACT 1: NYXTHORIA - CRYSTAL OF WATER

They found Nyxthoria, Keeper of Void Flames, at the edge of a reality-wound.

It was a place where the war had cut too deep—a jagged, vertical rift in the air, through which poured not light, but a slow, chilling anti-flame: void-fire that consumed heat and left absolute cold in its wake. Nyxthoria, a dragon of polished obsidian scales and eyes like smoked quartz, stood guard before it, her wings spread to contain the bleed.

She did not turn as they approached. “Aelxion sent you. For the crystal.”
Her voice was the sound of ice forming on dead stars.

“The planet’s thermal regulation is blocked,” Geralt said, the heat of Pyroclast making the words steam in the air. “We need the Hydros Crystal to re-establish the pressure gradient after we clear it.”

Nyxthoria finally looked at them. Her gaze was heavy, ancient. “This crystal is not a tool. It is a seal. Its resonance contains the absorption principle—the ability to take in excess heat and pressure without breaking. I use it to guard these rifts. If I give it to you, I cannot hold the void-fire back. It will bleed into your physical realm. Not today. Not tomorrow. But eventually.”

She speaks true, whispered Blue, her presence a cool tremor in Geralt’s mind. The water here is not wet. It is absence given shape. The crystal is the cork in a bottle of freezing dark.

“What comes through those rifts,” Nyxthoria continued, “will be on your conscience. Not mine.”

Geralt looked at the rift. Through it, he saw not another place, but a concept: the end of thermal energy. The death of fire. It was terrifying in a way violence could never be.

He looked back at the burning sky, the exploding magma vents, the desperate battle to save a world.

“We’ll deal with what comes through later,” he said. “Right now, we need to ensure there’s still a ‘later’ to deal with it.”

Nyxthoria held his gaze for a long moment. Then, with a sound like a glacier calving, she lowered her head. A single scale on her chest, darker than the others, irised open. From within, she drew forth the Hydros Crystal.

It was the size of Geralt’s fist, pulsing with a deep, resonant blue light. It did not glow—it drank the light around it, holding it in frozen suspension. The air near it grew still and cold.

“Use it well, Anchor,” Nyxthoria said, her voice already fading as the void-fire behind her began to seep forward, slower, but inevitable. “And remember: you have traded one apocalypse for another. The debt will come due.”

As they left, Geralt felt Blue reach out toward the crystal, not to take it, but to hum in recognition. A kindred spirit. It knows how to hold what is too hot to touch.
