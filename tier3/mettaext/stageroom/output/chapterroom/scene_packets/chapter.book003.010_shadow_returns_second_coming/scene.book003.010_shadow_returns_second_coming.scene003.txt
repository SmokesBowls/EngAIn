scene 010.3 — the cracks in the mountain: assault on the singing peaks

scene meta:
location: The Aerie of the Singing Peaks, Ash Giant Territory
time: year 10,500, month 3, day 7
participants: Korrhan, Ash Giants of the Aerie, Gruulith swarm
focus: The Gruulith swarm ambushes the isolated Ash Giants on the Singing Peaks; Korrhan realizes the fatal flaw of their isolationist pride and projects a desperate distress call across the kha to the Tidecallers and Forest Giants.
continuity: 33 days after the Gruulith deployment in scene 010.2; leads into the escalation in chapter 011
presentation: gameplay-to-cutscene
cutscene purpose: The Gruulith assault on the Aerie of the Singing Peaks and Korrhan's desperate distress call across the kha to other tribes

Korrhan—whose burnt-red stone-flesh was a living map of ancient lava flows and mineral veins—felt the disturbance not with his ears, but with his kha, the deep-earth consciousness that all Giants shared. It was a tremor of wrongness in the mountain's soul.

The Song of the Singing Peaks, a constant, low-frequency harmonic born of wind through specific crystalline formations, faltered. The mountain's consciousness, which Korrhan and his kin had communed with for generations, flinched. Not from earthquake or the slow breath of magma chambers, but from presence. Multiple presences, descending from the cold silence above, their kha-signatures unnervingly simple, like a single, sustained, dissonant note against the mountain's complex symphony.

"Perimeter breach," Korrhan's voice rumbled, a bass note that resonated through the stone, carrying alarm harmonics across the network to the other forty-two Giants of the Aerie. "Orbital descent. Entities... their composition is like ours, but sterile. A weapon."

They emerged from their dwellings—great halls carved not with tools, but with patient, centuries-long persuasion of the living stone. Their faces, hewn from obsidian and basalt, were etched with a sudden, deep-seated dread. They were the smallest of the Giant factions, proud and self-reliant, scattered across seven peaks. The First Coming's lies had taken root here most deeply: the Tidecallers were fickle, the Forest Giants invasive, the Mountain Giants arrogant hoarders of knowledge. Isolation was purity. Isolation was safety.

The first Gruulith crested the ridge. For a moment, it was indistinguishable from the weather-scarred granite. Then it moved, and the illusion shattered. Its form flowed, stone becoming clay, flowing over obstacles that should have required climbing. Its blank, mineral eyes held no spark of awareness, only a predatory lock-on. It did not roar a challenge. It did not pause. It simply attacked.

The assault was a lesson in brutal economy. Korrhan, expecting some form of communication, some declaration of intent, was caught off-guard. The Gruulith's limb wrapped around his leg, the clay-state flesh hardening instantaneously to unbreakable stone. The crack of stressed mineral was louder than any thunder. As Korrhan stumbled, a second Gruulith detached itself from what had been a boulder mere feet away.

"They are the mountain!" another Giant cried out, her voice a shiver of panic. All around them, the very landscape was coming alive, revealing itself as the enemy. Dozens, then hundreds of the Gruulith, their camouflage perfect. They had not occupied the mountain; they had infiltrated it.

Korrhan fought back, his kha flaring, trying to commune with the entity, to find some spark of reason within that simplified consciousness. He pushed a wave of questioning intent, an image of parley, a feeling of confusion. He was met with a psychic void, a wall of static. There was nothing there to reason with.

The Ash Giants were masters of their element. They raised stone barriers, triggered localized rockfalls, and used their sophisticated kha to disrupt the Gruulith's coordination. But for every one they shattered with a well-placed tectonic shock, two more would emerge from the ground itself. They were tireless, relentless, and their numbers seemed infinite.

"To the central spire!" Korrhan commanded, his voice strained as he shattered the stone binding his leg. "We must consolidate our kha! Reach the other Aeries—"

But the network was a cacophony of panic and pain. His consciousness brushed against the other six settlements. Each one was screaming. The same silent, methodical slaughter was happening simultaneously across the entire northern range. This was not a raid; it was an extermination.

"Who commands you?" Korrhan bellowed at the advancing horde, the futility of the question a poison in his heart. "What is it you want? Our land? Our lives?"

The only answer was the grinding of stone on stone and the silent, suffocating advance.

And in that moment, surrounded by the walking, murderous ghosts of his own people's simplified flesh, the foundational lies of three thousand years began to fracture. The pride of isolation now felt like suicide. The suspicion of their kin felt like a carefully planted poison.

The other tribes possessed what they lacked. The Tidecallers could summon mists and tsunamis. The Forest Giants could animate the very flora into a defensive army. The Mountain Giants, in their vast libraries of inscribed crystal, might hold the knowledge of what these creatures were and how to break them.

To ask for help was to admit catastrophic failure. It was to shatter the identity they had built for millennia. It was to trust those they had been taught to fear.

The choice was between a proud, solitary death, or a humbled, unified chance at life.

As another Gruulith lunged, its featureless face a mask of absolute, thoughtless purpose, Korrhan made his choice. He reached out, not to the mountain, but beyond it, pushing his kha further than it had ever gone, across the vast distances, toward the domains of the Tidecallers and the Forest Giants. The message was not one of strength, but of utter, desperate need, a single, clear harmonic that cut through the static of war:

We are broken. The sky has fallen. Help us.
