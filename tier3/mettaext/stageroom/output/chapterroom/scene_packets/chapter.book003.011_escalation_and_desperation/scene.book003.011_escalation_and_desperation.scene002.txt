scene 011.2 — the scream that shatters stone: assault on the oasis clans

scene meta:
location: Sun-Scorched Dunes & Grand Oasis Chamber (Desert Pyramid Complex)
time: year 10,547, month 8, day 19
participants: Saresh of the Oasis Clans, Desert Giants, Dregans
focus: Dregans assault the Oasis Clans with sonic resonance that fractures Giant stone-flesh and turns their sanctuary against them; Saresh retreats into the Grand Oasis Chamber, recognizes the Anunnaki's coordinated multi-front strategy, and accepts that survival now requires renewed cooperation between the divided Giant tribes.
continuity: 2 months, 16 days after the Dregan authorization in scene 011.1
presentation: playable-cutscene
cutscene purpose: The Dregan sonic assault on the Oasis Clans and Saresh's realization of the multi-front Anunnaki strategy

Saresh of the Oasis Clans felt the scream in the marrow of its stone. It was a wrongness that preceded sound—a vibration deep in the kha, a dissonance that made the sand grains dance and the very air tighten. Then the sound itself arrived, and it was annihilation.

It was not a voice. It was a weapon given sonic form. The frequency sliced through Saresh’s forty-foot frame, a precise and vicious scalpel finding the crystalline structures that bound its stone-flesh together. A web of fine cracks erupted across its chest and limbs. The desert around it began to change; the sand fused and melted, the air shimmering with a billion microscopic glass shards that refracted the sun into blinding, painful needles of light.

What in the deep earth—? Saresh’s thought was interrupted by the agony of resonance.

The creature crested the dune. It was magnificent and horrifying in equal measure—a thing of powerful, leonine build and vast, intricate wings that were a cruel joke, forever unable to lift its dense, earthen body. Its eyes, burning with a cold, ancient light, locked onto Saresh. And in that gaze, the Desert Giant did not see mindless aggression. It saw a hatred so profound, so personal, that it felt like a physical blow. This thing hated Saresh not for what it had done, but simply for what it was: whole, unified, and standing upon the earth without the burden of being a living mistake.

The Dregan’s tail, a whip of segmented stone and muscle, lashed out with impossible speed. It wrapped around a sandstone monolith, lifted the multi-ton stone with terrifying delicacy, and hurled it. Saresh barely had time to raise an arm in defense. The impact was catastrophic, its forearm exploding into gravel and dust.

"To the deep chambers! Now!" Saresh’s command was a fractured rumble, barely audible over the sustaining scream that was systematically dismantling its body. "The earth will shield us! It cannot follow—"

But it could. More shapes emerged from the heat haze, their forms blending perfectly with the desert until they moved. They had been waiting, their immense rage held in check by a patient, predatory intelligence. As the Giants turned to flee into their underground sanctuary, the Dregans followed.

Their pursuit was a nightmare of efficiency. They didn't dig; they unmade the earth. Their tails and claws excavated with brutal precision, their powerful bodies bulldozing through supports and barriers. Their screams pursued the Giants down into the dark, the sound waves channeling and amplifying in the tunnels, making the very walls vibrate and shed dust as the resonance sought to reduce their ancient home to powder.

Saresh collapsed in the Grand Oasis Chamber, a vast, hidden geode-city that had been a safe haven for millennia. Its body was failing, pieces of its consciousness flaking away like dry clay. The beautiful, crystalline structures of the chamber itself were singing a discordant, deadly song in response to the Dregan frequencies.

"They are not here to conquer," rumbled another Giant, its voice gritted with damage. "They are here to desecrate. To prove that our form, our very substance, is fragile. That we are no better than glass, waiting for the right note to shatter us."

The realization was a deeper crack than any the sonic scream could create. The enemy's hatred was born from envy, from a twisted reflection. The Dregans saw the Giants' wholeness and sought to reduce it to their own level of brokenness.

"The other tribes," Saresh gasped, the admission a surrender of three thousand years of pride. "The Tidecallers... their water magic could dissipate the sound. The Forest Giants could grow living baffles... even the Mountain Giants, with their hoarded knowledge... they must know what these things are!"

"The Ash Giants are being crushed by walking stone that wears their face!" a voice echoed through the kha-touch, strained and desperate. "They plead for the alliance we once scorned. And the Tidecallers... something moves in the deep oceans. The Brajor. They are not yet attacking, but their presence is a blockade, a threat. The golden ones are hitting us everywhere at once."

The truth settled in the chamber, as heavy and inescapable as a cave-in. The Anunnaki strategy was now horrifyingly clear: a simultaneous, multi-front assault designed to exploit each tribe's unique vulnerability and prevent them from uniting. The Sundering they had engineered with lies three millennia ago was now being exploited with overwhelming force.

Saresh felt another wave of sonic vibration shudder through the chamber, and a large crystal formation near the ceiling exploded into dust. The Dregans were close. The choice was no longer between pride and survival. The pride was already dead, pulverized. The choice was between a solitary, meaningless death in the dark, or a unified, desperate gamble for a future.

Alone, they were already glass, beginning to crack. Together, perhaps, they could become something strong enough to withstand the note that shatters.
