scene 011.1 — the flawed sword: dregan authorization

scene meta:
location: Genesis Chambers, Crimson Citadel
time: year 10,547, month 6, day 3
participants: Overseer Ninurta, Tactical Consciousness, Dregans
focus: Ninurta inspects the matured Dregans—failed attempts to replicate the cosmic Dragon species whose Dragon-adjacent consciousness is trapped in dense earthbound bodies—and authorizes their deployment against the desert Oasis Clans despite their unstable obedience and extreme rage.
continuity: 47 years, 2 months, 36 days after the assault on the Singing Peaks in scene 010.3
presentation: cutscene
cutscene purpose: Reveal of the Dregan genesis chambers, their sonic disruption capabilities, and Overseer Ninurta's deployment order against the desert Oasis Clans

Overseer Ninurta observed the Dregan maturation chambers, and for the first time in centuries, he felt a sensation akin to unease. It was not fear—fear was an illogical biological response—but a sharp recognition of a contained cascade failure. He was calculating acceptable risks against strategic necessity, and the variables were alarmingly volatile.

The creatures were… problematic. Unlike the Gruulith’s serene, geological obedience or the Brajor’s placid neutrality, the Dregans were a storm of contradictions. Their rage was not a programmed aggression subroutine; that would have been clean, manageable. This was something far more complex and dangerous: a genuine, seething hatred, baked into their very essence. It was a byproduct of the fundamental schism between their intended design and their realized form.

Failed Dragons. The classification was a cold, technical term in Anunnaki archives, a footnote from their ambitious, and ultimately futile, attempts to replicate the cosmic Dragon species for their legions. The physiological templates were flawless, reverse-engineered from data collected across a dozen purged worlds. The failure was metaphysical. The consciousness that awoke within the Dregan form was Dragon-adjacent—proud, ancient, and vast—but it was trapped in a physical shell that was a mockery of that heritage. They possessed the soul of a storm, caged in a body of earth and stone. Wings, beautiful and intricate, were grafted to frames too dense and heavy for flight, leaving them eternally earthbound. They were creatures of air and fire condemned to crawl.

Their intelligence was their curse. They were aware of their own degradation, capable of understanding the magnificent beings they were meant to be, and thus, capable of infinite rage at what they had become.

"Behavioral assessment," Ninurta requested, his voice a low hum in the charged air. The tactical consciousness had already provided this data seventeen times, but he needed to hear the confirmation again, to measure the risk.

"Aggression levels: maximum, exceeding safe parameters for intra-legion cooperation. Obedience to consciousness-commands: sporadic and conditional. They parse commands for alignment with their own rage-based objectives. Cooperation with other units: minimal. They see other Dregans not as comrades, but as mirrors of their own flawed existence. Self-preservation instinct: critically degraded. They demonstrate a preference for mutually assured destruction if it allows them to inflict maximum harm on a target."

"Combat effectiveness against projected desert Giant defenses?" Ninurta pressed.

"Exceeds all projections. Their vocal sonic disruption is uniquely tailored to crystalline-polymer matrices—the precise composition of Giant stone-flesh. Sustained exposure induces catastrophic harmonic resonance, reducing solid matter to inert glass dust. Their tail-manipulators demonstrate fine motor skills and tool-use sophistication rivaling sentient species. They can wield crafted weapons, execute complex siege engineering, and their physical strength, while smaller in stature, is hyper-dense, making them comparable to the largest Giants."

Ninurta processed the data. The Dregans were a weapon that hated the hand that held it. They were dangerous to the enemy, dangerous to themselves, and posed a non-zero threat to Anunnaki overseers. But the strategic calculation was undeniable. The desert Giants’ stone-flesh was uniquely vulnerable to this form of attack. The psychological impact of facing a corrupted, raging mirror of their own potential would be devastating.

"Authorization granted for desert deployment," Ninurta stated, the words a formal acceptance of liability. "Target the Oasis Clans of the southern expanse. Establish minimal oversight protocols—attempting to leash them will only turn their rage upon us. Their autonomy, however chaotic, serves our purpose: to demonstrate that even our mistakes can break them."

The genesis chambers hissed open. The Dregans emerged not with the slow certainty of the Gruulith, but with a shuddering, violent intensity. Their forms were a brutalist interpretation of a sphinx, all heavy angles and trapped motion. When the first one vocalized, it was not a roar of challenge, but a scream of pure, existential fury. The sound wave hit the chamber walls, and reinforced Adamantine supports spider-webbed with fractures. The frequency was a physical force, seeking out the resonant frequency of everything around it and demanding it shatter.

Ninurta withdrew, the data stream confirming his calculations. These were not mere soldiers. They were a philosophical argument given flesh and fury. They were the embodiment of Anunnaki power: the ability to not only destroy a species, but to pervert its very potential into a weapon of self-loathing against others.

The Dregans would bring ruin to the desert. And in the cold mathematics of conquest, that ruin would be another carefully placed wedge in the crumbling foundation of Giant unity.
