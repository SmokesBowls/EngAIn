The stone lifted. Smoothly. Silently. Three feet into the air, where it hung, turning slowly.

Mika stared at it, then at her hand. There had been no strain. No epic struggle. It had felt... obvious. Like recalling a melody she'd forgotten she knew.

Zephyr was staring at her, his usual placidity replaced by sharp, assessing interest. "That," he said slowly, "should have taken months of meditation. Years, for most."

"It felt natural," Mika whispered, lowering her hand. The stone settled back to the earth without a sound.

That night, by candlelight in the archive, Mika took out a blank ledger. Her hand did not shake. "I need to document this," she said to Geralt, who watched from the doorway. "Before I forget how easy it felt."

She titled the first page: On Resonance and Intent.
And she began to write.

ACT 5: TIME PASSING

Year 2. The archive grew, its shelves filling with Mika's precise handwriting. The Needle Spire on the horizon began to glow with a soft, steady light at night. Drawn by its energy, a new settlement sprang up at its base—the Sundrift family and other pioneers, building a community of engineers and mystics. Falcon Ridge remained smaller, a place of quiet study and green growth.

Year 3. Zephyr became a regular visitor, though never a resident. He and Mika would spend hours in the archive, her writing, him offering corrections that felt less like instruction and more like reminders. "The word for 'binding' has a secondary resonance for 'healing,' but only if your heart- tone is in the minor key," he'd say, and she would nod, scribbling in the margin. Geralt learned by osmosis, the concepts weaving into his understanding of the dragon armor's power.

Year 4. From the ridge, they could see Ironspire's expansion—new walls, watchtowers, the smoke of industry. Trade caravans now moved regularly between the two settlements and the Sundrift Spire-town. Falcon Ridge supplied rare herbs and knowledge; Ironspire provided metalwork and grain; the Spire-town offered technical skill. A fragile, thriving network.

Year 5. Oreck stood at the valley entrance at dawn, a pack on his back. Geralt and Mika came to see him off.

"I'm not built for quiet," the massive man said, his voice still layered with the faint hum of his Anunnaki augmentations.

"Where will you go?" Geralt asked.

"Somewhere that needs defending. Somewhere that needs... action." He looked at Mika. "Thank you. For everything."

"You saved us as much as we saved you," she said.

"We're even, then." He turned and walked down the path, into the rising sun. He never returned. The valley felt quieter, more peaceful, and somehow less complete without his solid, restless presence.

ACT 6: THE QUIET YEARS

Year 6. An evening in late autumn. The fire was low. Zephyr was away on one of his endless walks. The soldiers, now farmers and builders, had long since learned to give their commanders this evening silence.

Mika was sixty-three. Her hair was fully silver, her face lined with smiles and sorrows in equal measure. She watched the embers die, her hand resting on the completed, massive tome in her lap—The First Tongue: A Primer for Those Who Would Speak Creation.

"The book's done," she said, her voice soft but clear.

Geralt sat beside her. Time had not touched him. The scars that crisscrossed his skin were as stark as the day he’d earned them. His face, though hardened by millennia of memory, bore no new lines. "I know" he said

"I'm done."

"I know."

The silence that followed was full and final. The stars above Falcon Ridge were impossibly bright.

She went to bed first. He kissed her forehead, the gesture worn smooth by decades of repetition.

"See you tomorrow," he said.

"See you tomorrow," she whispered.

She did not wake in the morning.

ACT 7: AFTER

Weeks later, Geralt walked through the bustling streets of Ironspire alone. He carried a large, cloth-wrapped package. The town was noise and life—children shouting, blacksmiths hammering, the smell of baking bread. At the center of town stood the new library, a grand structure of stone and glass, Celly Sundrift's greatest project.

Geralt entered the hushed interior. Sunlight streamed through high windows onto a central pedestal of polished black stone. He approached, unwrapped the package, and placed the book upon it.

THE FIRST TONGUE: A PRIMER FOR THOSE WHO WOULD SPEAK CREATION
By Mika Covenant

He opened it to the first page, where her dedication was written in a firm, elegant hand: For those who come after. May you build with kinder words.

He left it there, for the scholars, the seekers, the next generation.

He walked back through the gates, nodding to guards who saluted him with deep respect. At the growing Sundrift settlement around the Needle Spire, he stopped at a market stall. He bought a loaf of dark bread and, from a small garden plot, a bunch of late-blooming blueflowers.

"For Mika," he told the gardener, a young woman with Merra's eyes.

"She'd like those," the girl said softly.

He walked the now-familiar path up to Falcon Ridge. He left the bread on the porch of their hut—one of the veterans would find it. He continued to the valley entrance, where the banner still flew. The scarf was threadbare now, the bloodstain faded to a rust-colored whisper, the pattern forever ambiguous.

Beside it was a simple stone marker.
