scene 005.1 — the first pyramid collapse

scene meta:
location: Desert beach / pyramid site
time: year 15
participants: Vairis, Senareth, Elyraen, burnt-red Giants
focus: The first 50ft sandstone step-pyramid collapses due to thermal expansion, but the Giants and Nephoretti analyze the failure data to refine future construction.
continuity: Fifteen years after the Day 0 landing; follows the convergence and settlement established in chapter 004
presentation: undecided

The first pyramid collapsed on a morning that had started with such promise.

Vairis and a cluster of desert Giants had been working for three months on the structure—layering sandstone blocks in a step-pyramid formation that was supposed to amplify vrill-resonance through geometric progression. The theory was sound. The mathematics checked. The consciousness-touch techniques were properly applied.

The execution was catastrophic.

The collapse started at the base—one block shifting fractionally as desert heat expanded the stone unevenly. That shift propagated upward through fifty vertical feet of carefully stacked architecture, each layer magnifying the instability of the one below. For a moment, the entire structure seemed to hang suspended, geometry fighting gravity, vrill-binding struggling to maintain cohesion against physical reality.

Then it fell.

The sound was like thunder compressed into a single point—crack of stone against stone multiplied by hundreds of tons of falling mass. Dust exploded outward in a wave that temporarily obscured the sun. Giants scattered, their massive forms surprisingly nimble when survival required it. Nephoretti dove for cover behind surviving structures or simply threw themselves flat against sand and hoped the debris wouldn't reach them.

When the dust settled, Vairis emerged coughing, their practical nature already assessing damage. Three Nephoretti injured but alive. No Giants hurt—their stone-flesh could withstand impacts that would obliterate smaller beings. And where the pyramid had stood, now only rubble remained, scattered across two hundred yards of desert beach.

"Expansion coefficient miscalculation," Vairis reported to Senareth, their voice carrying frustration rather than defeat. "Desert stone behaves differently under direct sun exposure than it does in shade. The base heated first, expanded first, destabilized the whole structure."

"So we adjust," Senareth replied, examining the wreckage with consciousness-touch that could read the failure-patterns in broken stone. "Smaller blocks at the base to accommodate expansion. Or we build at night, let morning heat test the bonds before adding upper layers."

The burnt-red Giants who had been primary builders gathered around the ruins, their rumbling harmonics clearly expressing disappointment. But one of them—the tall sandstone-textured Giant who had first approached Olythae's art—knelt and began reshaping the debris. Not clearing it, but studying it. Their consciousness-touch explored how the stones had fractured, where the stress-points had accumulated, what the failure itself could teach about geometry and resonance.

"They're not discouraged," Elyraen observed, their analytical mind reading the Giants' body language and vrill-patterns. "They're learning. Treating failure as data."

It became the pattern for the next eight decades.
