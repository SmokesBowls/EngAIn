scene 005.2 — the mountain obelisks

scene meta:
location: Island mountain peak / slope
time: year 47
participants: Mountain Giants, Nephoretti
focus: Mountain Giants carve the first 30ft singing granite obelisk, discovering that geometric proportions amplify vrill-resonance and consciousness-touch.
continuity: Takes place 32 years after the first pyramid collapse in scene 005.1
presentation: undecided

The mountain Giants' obelisk project succeeded where the pyramid had failed—partially because vertical structures had simpler stress-dynamics, but mostly because charcoal-grey Giants possessed an instinctive understanding of how stone wanted to stand.

The first successful obelisk rose thirty feet from a mountain slope overlooking the beaches—a single piece of granite shaped through consciousness-touch rather than carved or stacked. The mountain Giants had learned to ask the stone what shape it preferred, then help it remember that preference through patient communion. The result was a pillar that sang when wind passed through microscopic channels within its structure.

Not music exactly. More like sustained harmonic that made other stones nearby vibrate in sympathy. Nephoretti discovered that standing near the obelisk while practicing vrill-manipulation made their consciousness-touch more precise, more responsive. The geometry itself seemed to focus intention, amplifying what was already present without adding external power.

"Accidental discovery," the lead mountain Giant rumbled—they had learned basic spoken language now, though their deep voices made every word sound like distant thunder. "Built for beauty. Found utility."

More obelisks followed. Different sizes, different proportions, different harmonic frequencies. Some stood alone on prominent peaks. Others clustered in groups of three or five, creating interference patterns that the Nephoretti were still trying to understand. A few failed catastrophically—stone cracking under its own weight or shattering when underground water expanded during freeze-thaw cycles.

But enough succeeded to transform the island's silhouette. Where once only natural mountain had dominated, now stone spires rose like fingers pointing at sky, each singing its own frequency, all contributing to a harmony that made the entire island feel more alive, more resonant.
