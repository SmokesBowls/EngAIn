scene 005.4 — the first true pyramid

scene meta:
location: Valley pyramid site
time: year 156
participants: Elyraen, mountain Giants, desert Giants, forest Giants, Nephoretti assembly
focus: A multi-clan effort constructs a successful 40ft stepped pyramid whose acoustic inner chambers concentrate vrill-energy for accelerated healing and mental clarity.
continuity: Takes place 63 years after the cliff stabilization in scene 005.3
presentation: undecided

The first true pyramid stood.

Not in the desert where Vairis's early attempt had collapsed, but in a valley where three different Giant factions had worked together on a structure that incorporated all their hard-won lessons. Mountain Giants provided foundation stability. Desert Giants contributed knowledge of thermal expansion. Forest Giants added understanding of how living growth could reinforce stone through root-systems that bound rather than cracked.

And Nephoretti provided the mathematics—precise angles calculated to maximize vrill-resonance while minimizing structural stress.

The result was a stepped pyramid forty feet tall, each layer set back from the one below, creating terraces where vegetation could grow. The interior contained chambers—spaces shaped through consciousness-touch to create specific acoustic properties. Sound resonated strangely inside, frequencies amplifying or canceling based on position and tone.

Elyraen discovered that certain vrill-frequencies, when projected inside the central chamber, created effects that felt almost medicinal. Nephoretti who entered with minor injuries found their healing accelerated. Giants who had overextended their consciousness-touch during construction work reported faster recovery of mental clarity.

"It's the geometry," Elyraen explained to assembled Giants and Nephoretti during the structure's dedication ceremony. "The angles focus vrill-energy inward, concentrate it, make it more available to anyone inside. Not creating new power—just organizing what's already present."

Other pyramids followed, each experiment teaching something new. Some had interior chambers that hummed at specific frequencies when occupied. Others developed unexpected properties—one pyramid in the northern desert somehow stayed cool even under direct sun, its stone remaining comfortable to touch while surrounding sand baked.

Not all succeeded. A pyramid built too steeply collapsed inward when its own weight exceeded the stone's compression limit. Another cracked from foundation to peak when underground water froze and expanded. Each failure taught lessons that informed the next attempt.

But by year two hundred, a dozen pyramids stood across the island and mainland—monuments to cooperation between species, between factions, between beings learning to build something larger than survival required.
