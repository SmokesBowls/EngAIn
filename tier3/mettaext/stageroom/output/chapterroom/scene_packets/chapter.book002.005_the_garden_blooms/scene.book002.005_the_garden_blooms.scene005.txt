scene 005.5 — the tidecaller mountain chambers

The Tidecallers finally invited Nephoretti into their mountain.

It had taken over two centuries for the jade-green Giants to move beyond cautious coexistence into genuine trust. Torhh had been first, of course—the ocean-colored Giant whose curiosity had overcome instinct during those first terrible days. But convincing the entire Tidecaller faction required proof measured in generations, demonstrations of patience and non-harm that couldn't be faked or rushed.

The invitation came through Torhh, who approached Senareth at the water's edge where they still sometimes worked patterns together. "Our mountain. We wish to shape it. Make it home. But our communion alone is... crude. We lack precision. Will you help?"

The mountain at the island's center had always been the Tidecallers' territory—the place they retreated to when fear overwhelmed curiosity, where they maintained their largest population concentration. Allowing Nephoretti inside meant allowing them into the heart of Tidecaller identity, into spaces where vulnerability couldn't be hidden.

Senareth brought only a small group—Elyraen for their analytical precision, Vairis for practical engineering sense, Olythae for artistic vision, and Kyreth whose gentle nature had first broken through Torhh's fear. Twenty Nephoretti total, vastly outnumbered by the hundreds of Tidecallers who called the mountain home.

The work took decades.

The Tidecallers wanted chambers—spaces carved from living rock that could serve as meditation halls, gathering places, storage for the few material possessions Giants maintained. But more than utility, they wanted beauty. They wanted their home to reflect the ocean they remembered even if conscious thought had forgotten, to carry the essence of coral forests and tidal rhythms into mountain stone.

Nephoretti taught precision-shaping through consciousness-touch. Giants provided the raw power and scale. Together, they carved spiraling chambers that descended deep into the mountain's heart, their walls polished smooth as glass, their acoustic properties creating spaces where even whispered harmonics resonated with cathedral clarity.

Water channels threaded through the mountain—not for drinking, but for music. The Tidecallers directed underground streams through carved passages that made the entire structure sing when tide changed, when rain fell, when ocean currents shifted in the waters surrounding the island. The mountain became instrument, playing harmonies that could be heard from the mainland when wind carried sound across the tidal causeway.

"This is what we build when fear doesn't rule us," Torhh said to Seraneth during one of the final polishing sessions, their massive form settled in a chamber whose walls reflected bioluminescence like captured starlight. "Not just shelter. Art. Home that remembers who we were while celebrating who we've become."

The Tidecaller mountain became legend among the other Giant factions—proof that cooperation between species could create beauty beyond what either achieved alone. Other groups requested Nephoretti assistance with their own territorial centers. Mountain Giants wanted help carving meditation chambers at high altitudes. Desert Giants sought aid in shaping structures that could withstand sandstorms. Forest Giants asked for techniques to encourage living architecture—trees and stone growing together into organic fortifications.

The Garden was no longer scattered experiments. It was becoming civilization.
