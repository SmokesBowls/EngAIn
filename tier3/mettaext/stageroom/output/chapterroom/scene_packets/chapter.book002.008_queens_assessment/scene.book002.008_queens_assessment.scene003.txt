scene 008.3 — the first seeds of the sundering

scene meta:
location: Northern Nexus Site (Tidecaller Mountain)
time: year 20, month 5, day 16
participants: Torhh, Pazuzu
focus: Pazuzu unknowingly delivers the programmed narrative during joint stone-shaping; Torhh becomes defensive about ocean rituals, taking the first step into the three-thousand-year Sundering of the Giant clans.
continuity: 2 years, 3 months, 7 days after the Igigi programming in scene 008.2; marks the visible beginning of the Sundering and concludes book 2 before the First Coming occupation continues in chapter 009
presentation: cutscene
cutscene purpose: Show the first programmed lie taking root in Torhh and establish the beginning of the long-term Sundering

Torhh worked alongside Pazuzu in the late afternoon light, their consciousness-touches cooperating to shape stone for the Needle's foundation despite the fundamental wrongness of what they built.

The Igigi had become... not friends exactly, but something close. Colleagues in suffering. Two species trapped by Anunnaki will, finding small dignities in cooperation despite the violence surrounding them.

"Your people seem troubled lately," Pazuzu observed, his tone casual, his consciousness-touch carrying genuine concern. "More arguments between the ocean-colored ones and the desert-dwellers. I've noticed tension during the tribal gatherings."

Torhh rumbled acknowledgment. It was true—tensions had emerged that hadn't existed before. Small disagreements about resource allocation, territorial boundaries, ritual scheduling. Nothing serious yet, but... present.

"The desert ones complain about moisture levels," Torhh replied. "They say the weather has become more humid since we intensified our ocean communion rituals. They struggle in the dampness."

"Have you considered reducing the ritual intensity?" Pazuzu asked, and the question seemed reasonable, helpful even. "Or perhaps coordinating timing with desert populations to minimize impact?"

"We shouldn't have to adjust our sacred practices for their comfort," Torhh responded, defensive in ways it didn't quite understand. "The rituals connect us to our oceanic origins. They're fundamental to who we are."

"Of course," Pazuzu agreed quickly. "I wasn't suggesting—I just thought perhaps the desert Giants don't understand how important those rituals are. Maybe if you explained?"

But Torhh's consciousness was already processing implications it couldn't quite articulate. Why should ocean rituals require explanation? Why should Tidecallers compromise their sacred practices? The desert Giants had their own territories, their own rituals. If humidity bothered them, perhaps they should remain in their preferred environments rather than complaining about coastal weather patterns.

The thought felt reasonable. Logical. Entirely Torhh's own.

It didn't occur to the jade-green Giant that suspicion had just taken root where cooperation once grew. Didn't recognize that Pazuzu's casual observation had planted a seed that would grow across centuries into genuine cultural division.

The Sundering was invisible because it was patient. Because it operated through natural-seeming tensions rather than obvious manipulation. Because it turned victims against victims while the true enemy calculated from orbital distance.

By the time anyone recognized what was happening, it would be too late.

The Garden's unity—carefully built across five hundred years of hard-won trust—had begun its slow fragmentation.

And somewhere in the cold mathematics of cosmic conquest, Queen Eduhauana smiled.
