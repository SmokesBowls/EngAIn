scene 008.2 — brainwashing of the igigi emissaries

scene meta:
location: Manufacturing Complex (Consciousness-Programming Chamber)
time: year 18, month 2, day 9
participants: Queen Eduhauana, Pazuzu, Ishara, forty-seven other selected Igigi
focus: Queen Eduhauana personally brainwashes forty-eight Igigi (including Pazuzu) with surgical false narratives to spread targeted suspicions among Giant factions; Pazuzu involuntarily plants the first suspicion in Ishara.
continuity: 4 months, 5 days after the Queen's descent in scene 008.1
presentation: cutscene
cutscene purpose: Eduhauana's brainwashing of the Igigi and planting of the Sundering narrative

Overseer Marduk stood in the consciousness-programming chamber and tried not to calculate the probability of what the Queen was about to do to him.

The mathematics were not encouraging.

He had been selected along with forty-seven other Igigi—chosen for their interface history with Giants, their demonstrated capability for complex communication, their positioning at various construction sites. The stated purpose was "enhanced diplomatic coordination protocols."

Pazuzu's mathematical mind recognized that as euphemism for something far more sinister.

Queen Eduhauana herself administered the programming—not through crude technological interfaces, but through direct consciousness-touch that bypassed all the defenses Igigi psychology might have mustered. Her presence in his mind was overwhelming, ancient, carrying the weight of civilizations she had personally destroyed.

She didn't ask permission. Didn't explain. Just inserted.

The lies came in layers, each wrapped in enough truth to survive logical analysis:

The Tidecaller Giants are creating weather patterns that threaten desert populations. You've observed the increased humidity. You've seen how desert Giants struggle during coastal visits. This is not accident—it's territorial manipulation.

The Forest Giants plan expansion into mountain territories. They speak of it during their communion sessions. They believe the high peaks would be improved by their vegetation. They don't understand—or don't care—that such changes would destroy mountain Giant habitat.

The Highland Giants hoard knowledge. They've discovered techniques during their aerial meditations that could help all factions, but they keep these secrets to maintain superiority. You've witnessed their private councils. You know they share less than they claim.

Each lie was crafted with surgical precision. Each contained observations Pazuzu had actually made during construction work. Each twisted truth just enough to plant suspicion while remaining believable.

And underneath each lie, a consciousness-command that would ensure delivery:

You will share these observations with Giants you trust. You will do so naturally, as casual conversation. You will not remember this programming. You will believe these concerns emerged from your own analysis.

When the Queen withdrew from his consciousness, Pazuzu felt violated in ways his mathematical mind struggled to process. She hadn't just implanted lies—she'd rewritten portions of his memory, made him complicit in manipulation he couldn't consciously recognize.

He stumbled from the programming chamber, his bioluminescent patterns flickering with distress that he couldn't name. Around him, other Igigi emerged with similar disorientation, their consciousness-touch carrying contamination they didn't know they carried.

"Are you well?" Ishara asked, her concern genuine, her awareness unable to detect the psychological weapons they'd all just been loaded with.

"I... yes," Pazuzu replied, and meant it because the programming made him believe it. "Just tired. The Queen's assessment was thorough."

"Did she give you any insight into her strategic planning?"

And there it was—the first trigger. Pazuzu felt thoughts rising that seemed entirely his own, observations that felt like independent analysis rather than implanted narrative:

"I've been thinking about the Giant factions," he said slowly, his mathematical consciousness organizing lies into logical framework. "The way the Tidecallers create those weather rituals at their mountain site—have you noticed how it's affecting humidity levels across the desert construction zones?"

Ishara's bioluminescence shifted to patterns of consideration. "Now that you mention it, the desert Giants have seemed more sluggish lately. I attributed it to combat stress."

"Perhaps. Or perhaps the ocean-adapted ones don't realize their rituals are creating environmental conditions that harm their desert cousins. It's subtle. Easy to miss unless you're calculating cross-site atmospheric patterns."

The lie slipped out naturally, wrapped in Pazuzu's genuine analytical capability, poisoned with implications that would fester across millennia.

Ishara absorbed the observation, her own mathematical mind processing it as valid concern rather than manufactured suspicion. And somewhere in her consciousness, the seed was planted.

By the end of the month, all forty-eight programmed Igigi had returned to their construction sites, carrying psychological weapons they didn't know they possessed. They would share their "observations" with Giants they'd learned to trust. They would plant divisions in soil that cooperation had carefully cultivated.

And three thousand years later, when the Anunnaki returned, they would find Giants who barely remembered they were once unified, who fought each other over resources and territories and ancient grievances that no one could quite trace to their source.

The Sundering had begun.

Not with armies or weapons or direct conquest, but with whispered lies delivered by victims to victims, poisoning trust with surgical precision.

Queen Eduhauana watched the psychological warfare deployment from orbital position and calculated satisfaction in mathematics well executed. The Giants would resist for the remainder of this Coming. They would delay construction. They would exact casualties.

But they would never know that their doom was already written, implanted in the consciousness of the very slaves they'd learned to see as allies.
