scene 015.3 — the choice

scene meta:
location: Ash Giant Main Mountain Chamber
time: year 10,550, month 1, day 20
participants: Korrhan, Ash Giant council, 29 conscious survivors
focus: Korrhan presents Enlil's terms and the surviving Ash Giants vote 23-to-6 to accept collaboration rather than face extinction.
continuity: 3 days after Enlil's offer in scene 015.2
presentation: playable-cutscene
cutscene purpose: The Ash Giant tribal council debate and the vote to accept Anunnaki collaboration over extinction.

The Ash Giant tribe gathered in their largest chamber—twenty-nine conscious survivors, their damaged stone-flesh bearing witness to the ritual's cost. Korrhan presented Enlil's offer without embellishment, without attempts to soften the implications.

"We become what we resisted. We betray everything the Garden represented. We trade principles for survival and make ourselves into cautionary tales that will poison Giant cooperation for thousands of years."

The chamber was silent except for the soft grinding of stone-on-stone as damaged bodies shifted.

"Or we maintain principles while our children die slowly," the eldest survivor finally rumbled. "While the ritual damage accumulates across generations. While we prove to the other tribes that honor without survival is just performative martyrdom."

"The other tribes already judge us," another added, its voice carrying the bitterness of accusations heard during the convergence. "They blamed us for requiring rescue. For making the ritual necessary. For imposing costs through our weakness. Whatever relationship we had with them fractured the moment Korrhan withdrew consciousness-touch."

"Then we've already lost what we'd be betraying," a third concluded. "The alliance is dead. The unity is shattered. We're choosing between dying for principles that other tribes don't credit us with holding, or surviving through arrangements that acknowledge what already exists: we're alone, and the golden ones offer what our supposed allies won't."

The logic was sound even as it broke what remained of Korrhan's hope that some alternative existed.

"Vote," Korrhan commanded, its voice carrying the weight of geological certainty. "Betray the other tribes and accept Anunnaki terms, or maintain resistance and accept inevitable extinction."

The vote was overwhelming. Twenty-three to six. Not unanimous—that would have made the choice easier to live with. But sufficient.

The Ash Giants would become collaborators.

And the Sundering would achieve the completion that three thousand years of subtle lies had prepared.
