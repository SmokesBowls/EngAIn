scene 014.1 — the ritual begins: elemental convergence

scene meta:
location: Central Valley Ritual Grounds
time: year 10,550, month 1, day 11
participants: Torhh, Korrhan, Saresh, Pazuzu, allied Igigi, Giant tribes (Tidecallers, Ash Giants, Desert, Forest, Mountain, Highland, Subterranean)
focus: Following seven days of preparation, the Giant tribes and allied Igigi initiate the Nephilim convergence; extreme heat, glacial cold, atmospheric pressure shifts, and gravitational flux simultaneously target the environmental vulnerabilities of the participating tribes while they struggle to maintain the shared consciousness network, causing old programmed suspicions to erupt under the strain.
continuity: 7 days after the Deep Communion in scene 012.2
presentation: playable-cutscene
cutscene purpose: The commencement of the Nephilim ritual, the elemental convergence, and the first visible fracturing of the alliance under shared suffering.

The central valley had been transformed across seven days of desperate preparation. Giants from all tribes positioned themselves in geometric patterns that the Aeon Keepers' instructions demanded—Tidecallers in the lowest depression where water naturally pooled, Ash Giants on the elevated ridges where heat would concentrate most intensely, Highland tribes at precisely calculated altitudes, Subterranean populations in excavated chambers that would experience maximum gravitational flux.

Each faction knew exactly what was coming. The Aeon Keepers had been explicit about the suffering each would endure. And yet they gathered anyway, consciousness-touches linking into the collective network that would channel the elemental forces required for Nephilim manifestation.

Pazuzu stood at the ritual's calculated center point—not as leader but as mathematical anchor, his Igigi consciousness providing the precision needed to maintain coherence when pain tried to shatter collective awareness. Around him, other Igigi formed supporting nodes in the network, their three-thousand-year experience with consciousness-crafting making them uniquely suited for this terrible work.

"When it begins," Pazuzu addressed the assembled thousands, his voice carrying across the valley through consciousness-channels, "remember: the pain is temporary. The unity we forge through surviving it together is permanent. The Aeon Keepers designed this convergence to either break us or bind us beyond any possibility of fracture."

"Easy to say when you're at the center where conditions are balanced," a Mountain Giant muttered, its charcoal-grey form already positioned at an altitude where atmospheric pressure would soon become crushing.

Pazuzu's bioluminescence flickered with acknowledgment. "You're right. I won't suffer as you will. Which means my role is witnessing. Remembering. Ensuring that when you emerge—if you emerge—someone carries clear memory of what you endured. That no one can claim later that the pain wasn't real, wasn't chosen, wasn't sacrifice for collective survival."

The admission settled some of the tension. Witness mattered. Memory mattered. If they were choosing to suffer, at least that choice would be recorded.

"Begin the convergence," Torhh commanded, its ancient voice carrying finality.

The planetary consciousness responded.

Not gradually—that might have been merciful. But instantly, with the absolute precision that cosmic forces demanded. The environmental extremes manifested simultaneously across the valley, each faction experiencing conditions calibrated to push their physiology to the absolute edge of survivability.

For the Ash Giants and desert tribes: Heat erupted from the earth itself. Not external fire that could be avoided, but thermal energy radiating from stone, turning their bodies into molten forms that barely maintained cohesion. Korrhan felt its burnt-red stone-flesh liquefying, consciousness fragmenting as the solid structures that defined identity became fluid, unstable, screaming.

For the Tidecallers and Forest Giants: Cold beyond imagination descended. Water in their ocean-adapted biology froze at molecular level, turning fluid consciousness rigid. Torhh experienced its ancient awareness crystallizing, thoughts becoming glacial, three thousand years of accumulated wisdom trying to shatter like ice under pressure.

For the Highland and Mountain Giants: Atmospheric pressure shifted rapidly—compression followed by expansion followed by compression, creating cycles that crushed and stretched their bodies beyond comfortable limits. Bones that were stone groaned under forces that geological patience couldn't absorb.

For the Subterranean populations: The planetary mass itself became weapon. Gravitational flux made the deep-earth forces they'd adapted to fluctuate wildly—heavy then light then crushing then floating, orientation becoming meaningless as "down" lost stable definition.

And through it all, consciousness-touches had to remain connected. The collective network that channeled these forces couldn't fragment or the ritual would fail. Every Giant had to maintain awareness of the others' suffering while experiencing their own, had to feel not just personal agony but the accumulated pain of every tribe simultaneously.

The temptation to break connection was overwhelming.

"Hold!" Pazuzu commanded through the network, his mathematical consciousness calculating stress points where the collective threatened to shatter. "The Nephilim are forming! I can sense vrill-energy coalescing in the quantum foam! If we break now, the convergence becomes explosion rather than manifestation!"

But words barely registered through suffering that exceeded anything the Giants had imagined possible. They had survived combat, occupation, three thousand years of hardship. Nothing prepared them for pain inflicted by cosmic forces that predated mercy.

"The Tidecallers caused this," came a thought bleeding through the collective—a Mountain Giant whose consciousness was fragmenting under atmospheric pressure. "They insisted on including water-based tribes. If this had been just stone-adapted populations, the ritual wouldn't require such extreme cold—"

"The desert tribes made it worse," a Forest Giant responded, its consciousness rigid with glacial cold. "Heat this intense wouldn't be necessary if we hadn't included populations adapted to arid conditions—"

"ALL OF YOU made this necessary," Korrhan screamed through the network, its molten form barely maintaining coherence. "The ritual requires ALL tribes because the Nephilim are constructs of COMPLETE elemental balance! If we fragment now, if we start blaming each other for requirements that COSMIC LAW demands—"

The thought never completed. Another wave of heat exceeded even Korrhan's tolerance, and the Ash Giant's consciousness dissolved into liquid agony that no longer possessed capacity for coherent communication.

The collective network was fracturing. Not completely—Pazuzu's mathematical precision and the Igigi anchors held core coherence. But cracks were spreading. Three thousand years of planted suspicions found perfect conditions to grow, fertilized by suffering that made blame feel justified.

In the quantum foam surrounding the valley, vrill-energy was indeed coalescing. Dark and light Ethereal forces mixing, consciousness and matter finding balance points, the Nephilim taking shape through processes that required precisely this level of sacrifice to manifest.

But the Giants could see their creation forming, and instead of unity in accomplishment, they felt rage at the cost different factions had imposed on each other through their inclusion.
