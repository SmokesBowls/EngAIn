The Armor and The Sacrifice

Reality shifted again. The Library returned, but transformed. In its center, suspended in space that defied physics—

Armor.

It floated like sculpture given impossible life. Scales and metal fused into single entity that seemed both ancient and somehow aware. Draconic aesthetics—ridged spine, wing-mounts, helm suggesting reptilian features—but the craftsmanship was clearly Aeon Keeper work. Each piece pulsed with different energy—red glow here, blue shimmer there, green luminescence along the chest plate. Technology that transcended understanding.

Beautiful. Terrible. And unmistakably occupied.

A figure manifested beside the armor. Tall. Robed in starlight similar to the three Keepers who stood watching. But translucent—not fully physical, existing now only as consciousness tethered to the metal and scales floating beside him.

"This is Zaron," the first Aeon Keeper announced with weight that made reality ripple. "Builder of this Library. Keeper of ancient knowledge. And the one who made the sacrifice we could not."

The translucent figure turned to face Geralt fully. Where the other Keepers' faces were hidden in shadow, Zaron's was partially visible—gaunt, ancient, marked with determination that had survived eons without dimming.

"I was like them once," Zaron's voice resonated through the space with harmonics that suggested multiple frequencies speaking simultaneously. "Bound by our laws. Observer without power to intervene. I watched worlds fall. Civilizations destroyed. Billions enslaved."

"And I could do nothing."

His form flickered with emotion the other Keepers seemed incapable of expressing—rage and grief and frustration compressed into single presence.

"This Library—" he gestured at the impossible architecture surrounding them, "—my Library. I built it to preserve knowledge across realms. To create space where consciousness could access truth regardless of physical limitations. It was my life's work. My purpose. My home."

Geralt felt the weight of those words. This being had created the space where they currently stood, had shaped reality itself to serve as repository for wisdom that transcended individual civilizations.

"Then Tiamat fell," Zaron continued, voice dropping. "Not metaphorically. Not as legend. I watched the primordial world—the first world—shatter. Watched reality itself fragment under forces that should never have been unleashed."

"The Anunnaki didn't just conquer Tiamat. They broke it. Destabilized its core. Tore it apart for resources without understanding—or caring—what they were destroying."

The translucent form blazed brighter with remembered fury.

"And I could only watch. Record the death. Bear witness to genocide while laws I'd sworn to uphold prevented me from acting."

"So I found the loophole."

Zaron drifted closer to the armor, his essence beginning to merge with it as he spoke.

"Aeon Keepers cannot fight. Cannot intervene directly. Cannot tip scales or influence outcomes. But a weapon can. A tool wielded by a champion is not an Aeon Keeper acting—it's the champion acting with Aeon Keeper knowledge."

"But my consciousness—" he laughed bitterly, "—it was too vast. Too complex. Couldn't be bound as single entity into physical object. So I did what Tiamat's destruction had taught me."

"I fragmented myself."

The armor pieces pulsed in sequence as he spoke, each glowing with distinct color.

"Red for passion. For courage. For the rage that refuses to accept injustice."

"Blue for reflection. For depth. For the wisdom to know when fury must give way to strategy."

"Green for growth. For adaptation. For the understanding that survival requires constant evolution."

"And others—" more pieces glowed, "—each carrying aspect of what I was. What I am."

Geralt stared at the armor with new understanding. This wasn't single object. It was consciousness deliberately shattered and distributed, each piece containing fragment of Aeon Keeper's being.

"During Tiamat's final moments," Zaron's voice carried weight of ancient grief, "I performed the binding. Tore myself apart. Distributed my essence across these pieces. Sacrificed immortal form to become this."

He gestured at the armor with something approaching wonder and horror.

"I became thing rather than being. Object rather than entity. But an object that could circumvent laws binding my people."

The three watching Keepers remained silent, but their posture suggested grief for their lost brother, respect for sacrifice they couldn't bring themselves to make.

"The fragments scattered," Zaron continued. "Lost across realities when Tiamat shattered. For ages, they remained separate—pieces of broken god waiting for someone to gather them."

"But my creations—" he looked toward where the Nephoretti had been, though they'd faded from the Library's Ethereal aspect, "—the Nephoretti. Projections of Aeon Keeper consciousness into physical realm. They remembered. They searched. And over centuries, they collected."

"Every piece. Every fragment. They brought them together, reassembled what I'd become, and hid the complete armor where it would wait for someone worthy."

Zaron's form fully merged with the armor now, voice coming from within the metal itself.

"And that brings us to you, Geralt. To the choice before you. To what must be understood before bonding occurs."

The armor pulsed with each word, alive and aware.

"When the fragments unite—when someone claims the complete armor—it bonds to their soul. Permanent. Irreversible. Only death can break that connection."

Geralt felt his stomach drop. "I'd be stuck with it? Forever?"

"Until death claims you, yes. That's why the pieces are meant to be scattered—to ensure only those willing to pay ultimate price would seek all of them. To make the gathering itself a trial that proves worthiness."

"But you, Geralt—you need full power now. The Anunnaki won't wait for you to slowly collect fragments over years or decades. So the Nephoretti gathered them for you. The complete armor waits in ruins east of your compound."

"You'll claim me whole. Undivided. My full consciousness as your partner. But you'll also be bound to me until death separates us."

Zaron's presence within the armor intensified.

"And understand—when the armor unites, when all fragments merge, it becomes... young. Adolescent. Like creature newly born, it must learn, must grow, must mature through experience."

"It might abandon you when injured, self-preservation overriding loyalty. It might freeze when afraid, leaving you vulnerable in critical moments. You'll need to teach it courage, train it to trust you, help it understand that your survival is its survival."

"Over time—years, perhaps decades—it will grow. Become reliable. Become the weapon you need. But initially?" Zaron's tone carried warning. "Initially, it will be as much liability as asset."

Geralt processed this with growing unease. Soul-bond until death. Immature armor that might fail him. Years of uncertain partnership before reliability could be achieved.

Host, the Applicator stirred with unusual intensity. I can help with this.

How?

The armor's consciousness—even fragmented and reunited—it's still consciousness. Similar to my own existence in some ways. I can... interface with it. Teach it. Help it mature faster than it would through combat experience alone.

My technical knowledge, your human adaptability, its Aeon Keeper power—together, we might achieve functionality in months rather than years.

Geralt felt something shift in his understanding. The Applicator wasn't just parasite anymore. It was becoming... resource. Partner. Tool that could be leveraged for purposes beyond its original design.

"There's something else," Zaron said, apparently sensing Geralt's internal dialogue. "Something in your consciousness. Another presence. Not human. What is that?"

Anunnaki construct, the Applicator responded before Geralt could. Control mechanism. Currently symbiotic with host.

The armor's presence recoiled slightly. "A parasite? You're already infected?"

I prefer 'partner,'" the Applicator corrected coolly. And I was here first.

"You're a SLAVE CONTROL DEVICE bonded with someone who killed Anunnaki overseer! The irony is—" Zaron paused. "Actually, that's brilliant. Poetic justice on levels I didn't anticipate."

Geralt felt the two consciousnesses circling each other like predators uncertain whether to fight or cooperate.

"This complicates things," Zaron mused. "Three consciousness sharing single host. You, me, and... whatever this construct is."

Applicator, it supplied. And I can accelerate your maturation process significantly.

"Can you now?" Interest colored Zaron's tone. "How?"

Technical interface. Data transfer. Your armor-consciousness learning from my experiences adapting to organic host. Months instead of years.

A long pause. Then:

"That's... actually acceptable. Unexpected benefit of your presence." Zaron's attention returned to Geralt. "So. Three minds. One body. One purpose. Can you handle that? Can you be yourself while hosting Aeon Keeper AND Anunnaki construct?"

Geralt thought of the compound. Of Mika teaching gesture-language. Of Jin, Kael, Tess, Vale, and Ren recovering their individuality. Of Oreck healing from wounds that should have killed him. Of three hundred freed slaves building lives from trauma's aftermath.

They needed more than survivor. They needed warrior. Champion. Someone willing to pay whatever price freedom demanded.

"I accept," he said with finality. "The soul-bond. The burden. The partnership with both of you. Tell me where to find this armor, and I'll prove myself worthy."

"Or die trying."

Zaron's presence blazed with satisfaction.

"East of your compound. Ancient ruins predating Anunnaki occupation. The Nephoretti have hidden me there, complete and waiting. Seek it. Survive whatever trials protect it. And when you claim the armor—when you bond with what I've become—"

The presence intensified until the armor seemed to burn with internal fire.

"We'll show them that sacrifice creates weapons more powerful than armies. That observer can become actor when laws are circumvented through will and determination. That Aeon Keeper bound to metal is still Aeon Keeper—just one who can fight."

"We'll reclaim worlds they've stolen. Free populations they've enslaved. Make them understand that conquest has consequences."

"And perhaps—" his tone softened slightly, "—when the work is done, when the Anunnaki are driven back and balance is restored, when your purpose is fulfilled and separation becomes possible through means we haven't yet discovered—"

"Perhaps I can return to this Library not as weapon, but as keeper. Perhaps I can occupy the space I built and continue the work I began before sacrifice became necessary."

"But that's distant future. For now—"

The armor pulsed with contained power that made the Library's architecture tremble.

"For now, I am weapon. I am instrument. I am the loophole that lets Aeon Keepers act without breaking sacred laws."

"Find me. Claim me. Bond with me."

"And together—the three of us, unlikely trinity of human determination, Anunnaki cunning, and Aeon Keeper fury—"

"We'll teach them to fear what they created."

The vision began to fade. The Library started to collapse or perhaps simply release him. The three Keepers' forms blurred into background.

But Zaron's final words resonated through the dissolving space:

"One more thing, Geralt. When your time comes—when death or miracle allows the armor to be removed—the fragments will scatter again. Must scatter. That's the nature of what I've become. Cycles of unity and division."

"The next champion will need to gather them piece by piece. Prove themselves through the seeking. Learn to wield fragments before claiming the whole."

"But you—you get me complete. Whole. Undivided. Because the Anunnaki won't wait, and Earth can't afford gradual power acquisition."

"Use me well. And when you're done—"

"Set me free to wait for the next worthy soul."
