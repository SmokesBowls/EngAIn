Epic 2: THE INFECTION

ACT 1: THE PROTO-DRACO SURGE

The sound was wrong.

It wasn’t a battle cry, not a roar of aggression. It was a low, synchronized hum, hundreds of throats producing the same frequency, vibrating through the pearlescent corridors of the City of Echoes. It was the sound of a single thought, repeated by minds that had never known unity.

The Proto-Dracos were coming.

Geralt stood with Oreck and Kyh at a curved overlook, watching the tide of scaled bodies surge against the city’s lower terraces. They moved with a chilling, insectile coordination—no tactics, no formations, just a relentless, single-minded push toward the mountain heart.

“They’re not trying to kill the Solantri,” Oreck growled, his grip tightening on his cleaver. “Look.”

He was right. The Proto-Dracos swarmed around the glowing barriers of light the Solantri defenders raised, not attacking them directly, but piling against them, pushing, clawing, climbing. They were treating the defenders as obstacles, not targets. Their glassy, dull eyes were fixed past them, up the mountain, toward the Old Observatory and the spire entrance beneath.

“They’re being called,” Kyh whispered, his twilight eyes wide with dread. “It’s a summoning. They can’t help but answer.”

Host. The voice in Geralt’s head was his Applicator, but its tone was… strained. I am detecting a carrier wave. Extremely low frequency. It is not targeting us. It is saturating the area. The Proto-Dracos are resonating with it.

Can you pinpoint the source?

A moment. A pulse of scanning focus, familiar and cold. Then, confusion. The source is… diffuse. It is not a single emitter. It is multiple points, synchronized. A network.

A network. Kethran’s warning echoed in Geralt’s memory. Other applicators.

“We need to get to the spire before they do,” Geralt said, his voice tight. “If they reach Lentharis while he’s confused and waking…”

He didn’t finish. He didn’t need to.

They joined the fray, cutting a path downward. Oreck was a battering ram of scale and muscle, his cleaver shearing through Proto-Draco limbs not out of malice, but necessity. They didn’t scream; they made that same pained, confused hum as they fell. Kyh flickered through the chaos, using shadow-steps to reposition, to trip, to create momentary gaps in the press of bodies.

Geralt fought beside them, the dragon armor responding to his will. But with each pulse of energy from his palm, each telekinetic shove, he felt a… feedback. A tiny, harmonic echo in the back of his skull. It was the feeling of shouting in a silent hall and hearing a whisper from another room answer.

The carrier wave is modulating, the Applicator reported, its clinical tone now laced with something Geralt had never heard before: apprehension. It is adapting to local energy signatures. My emissions are being… absorbed. Cataloged.

A cold knot formed in Geralt’s stomach. They’re listening to you.

It is a possibility.

They fought their way to the entrance of the Old Observatory catacombs—a grand, crumbling archway leading into darkness. The Proto-Draco river was flowing into it, vanishing into the mountain’s belly.

“This is the throat,” Kyh said, staring into the dark. “And we’re following the infection down.”
