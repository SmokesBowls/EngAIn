The Void Jump to Umbrageous wasn't a transition. It was a dulling.

One moment, they were in the hard, clear black of the void, the Ghost Spire's energy crisp around them. The next, everything softened. The light, the sound, even the concept of color seemed to leach away into a perpetual, weary twilight.

They materialized on a plain of ash.

It wasn't the violent, scorched ash of Pyroclast. This was a fine, grey powder that fell in a silent, never-ending drizzle from a starless sky the color of a day-old bruise. The ground was featureless, stretching to a horizon obscured by a mist of its own making. The air was cold, still, and carried a scent like old paper and damp stone.

“Ambient light source: unknown,” the applicator in Geralt’s head reported, its text subdued. “Chronometric analysis indicates local star absent for approximately 10,000 years. Illumination appears to be residual, reflected from distant cosmic structures. Probability of photosynthesis: 0%.”

“Cheerful place,” Oreck grunted, his scales already dusted grey.

Kyh took a step and froze. He looked down at the ash swirling around his boots, then out at the vast, silent plain. His twilight eyes widened.

“This ash… it remembers,” he whispered, his voice hushed with a weaver’s awe. “It’s not dead. It’s… recording. I can feel it watching. Every step we take, it’s writing us down.”

He is correct, Violet murmured in Geralt’s mind, her harmonics tinged with unease. This is not soil. It is memory given particulate form. The entire world is an archive of its own death.

Luminaire’s presence was a faint, sorrowful chime. “The Sundering of Light. A cosmic event that stripped this sector of its primary star. The Spire’s dormant protocol was a desperate act of preservation. To exist on borrowed, reflected light for ten millennia… it changes the nature of matter itself.”

In the middle distance, shapes moved. Hulking, bipedal figures with sloping shoulders and thick, scaled hides. Proto-Dracos. They moved with a shuffling, deliberate gait, heads down, claws sifting through the ash. As one, they stopped. Dozens of rough-hewn heads turned toward the newcomers. For a long, tense moment, there was only the whisper of falling ash.

Then, as if on a silent signal, they turned and shambled away, melting into the grey mist without a sound.

“They’re afraid,” Oreck observed, his hand resting on his cleaver. “But not of us. Of something else.”

Their destination was clear: a range of jagged, black mountains clawing at the twilight sky—the Sundered Spine. Nestled within their foothills, carved into the living rock, glimmered the City of Echoes.

The city was beautiful in a haunting, sepulchral way. Structures weren’t built; they were grown—smooth, flowing formations of a pearlescent material that caught the faint light and fractured it into soft rainbows. Solantri moved through the streets, their skin the same nacreous white as their city, their forms emitting gentle pulses of light that flashed in complex, silent patterns to one another.

At the city’s main gate, they were met not by warriors, but by a trio of Solantri whose light-pulses were slow, deliberate, and tinged with a cautious yellow. One stepped forward, and its voice emerged not from a mouth, but from the air around them, a harmonic resonance formed from modulated light.

“You carry the resonance of the old networks. You are expected, and also… feared. Enter. The Echo Crown awaits your words.”
