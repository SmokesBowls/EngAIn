They were led through silent, gleaming streets. The Solantri they passed stopped to watch, their light-pulses flickering in patterns too fast for Geralt to follow, but he felt the applicator in his head twitch, attempting to parse them.

The Echo Crown was the city’s heart: a vast, natural amphitheater open to the sky, at its center a single, massive tuning fork of the same pearlescent material, three stories tall. Around it, on concentric rings of stone, sat the city’s council. Their light was steadier, deeper in hue.

The lead councillor’s voice resonated in the hollow space. “I am Elara. We monitor the Spire’s dreams. For ten thousand years, they were the same: a memory of light, and a command to sleep. Now, the dreams are changing. They are becoming… screams. Lentharis, our Keeper, wakes in fear. And the Proto-Dracos, who have been our peaceful, simple neighbors since the Sundering, are becoming agitated. They gather. They move with purpose. They are drawn to the Spire, and we do not know why.”

“Drawn how?” Oreck asked, his voice echoing in the space.

“As if called. But we hear nothing. Our communications are light. Theirs are… something else. Something older. And now, you arrive. Your resonance is loud. It shakes the ash.”

A younger Solantri, standing at the edge of the council ring, stepped forward. His name-pulse came through as Kethran. His light was a sharper, more inquisitive blue. He stared directly at Geralt, his head tilted.

“Your resonance is… layered,” Kethran said, his harmonic voice curious. “I hear the Anchor’s bond. The Architect’s echo. And beneath it… a whisper. An echo that does not belong. A frequency that is answering a call we cannot hear.”

Geralt felt a cold trickle down his spine. “My applicator?”

“No. Something using your applicator. A sympathetic vibration. It is faint, but it is there. And it is growing.”

Before Geralt could respond, a series of sharp, crimson light-pulses flashed from the city walls—an alarm.

A guard’s voice resonated, tight with panic. “The Proto-Dracos! Hundreds of them! Massing at the western approach! They… they are not shambling. They are marching!”

Elara’s light dimmed with dread. “It has begun. Kethran, take them to the Underchamber. Find the source of this call. We will hold the walls.”

Kethran gestured urgently. “This way. Quickly.”
