He led them out of the Echo Crown and down a spiraling ramp deep into the mountain’s roots. The air grew colder, the pearlescent walls giving way to raw, black stone etched with swirling patterns that seemed to move in the corner of the eye.

The Underchamber was not a room. It was a cave of frozen light.

Crystalline formations hung from the ceiling and rose from the floor, each containing a shimmering, holographic record—a catastrophic choice, a moment of loss, a memory of the world before the Sundering. The air thrummed with sadness.

“These are the archives of our failures,” Kethran said, his light pulsing softly as he moved between the crystals. “The choices that led us here.”

He stopped before a large, central crystal. Inside, a star—brilliant and warm—exploded in silent, slow motion, its light tearing away into nothingness. The Sundering.

“Our star did not die naturally,” Kethran explained, his voice heavy. “It was murdered. A weapon of such scale it carved the light from reality. The Spire network enacted Protocol Deepest Dusk. All spires in the sector were ordered to dormancy. To conserve. To wait for a dawn that never came.”

He pointed to lines of flowing script etched into the crystal’s base—a language of light. “The order came from the original architects. The primary signature is fragmented, degraded by time. But the secondary executor… the one who carried the order to our Spire… his name remains. Viên.”

“Viên,” Luminaire echoed through Geralt, a note of profound recognition and sorrow in her voice. “The First Architect. The one who laid the cornerstone of the network. He was lost in the early wars. I thought his commands lost with him.”

“Why is Lentharis waking now?” Geralt asked, staring at the image of the dying star.

Kethran turned his luminous gaze back to Geralt. He focused, and his own light pulsed, scanning. He flinched.

“The answer is… inside you,” he said, his harmonics laced with confusion and a dawning fear. “That strange echo. It’s not just answering a call. It is… amplifying one. I can’t parse its source. It’s too deep. Too integrated. But it’s using your connection to the network, to the Anchor, as a relay. It’s whispering to the Spire. And the Spire is whispering back.”

Above them, the distant, muffled sounds of conflict reached the Underchamber. The walls trembled with a deep, subsonic rhythm—the march of hundreds of heavy feet.

The Proto-Draco surge had reached the city.

And somewhere, buried in the code of Geralt’s own mind, a sympathetic frequency was humming along, orchestrating the entire disaster.
