ACT 4: THE CAGE

Geralt lay on the cold stone, the ghost of the severed connection a bleeding wound in his psyche. The Applicator in his mind was a silent, shuddering thing.

Kethran found them there. The young Solantri scholar looked from the dark pool to Geralt, his luminous eyes full of a grief too deep for his years. “He saved you. He saved us all.”

“At what cost?” Oreck rumbled, helping Geralt to his feet.

“The spire is orphaned,” Luminaire confirmed, her voice heavy. “Umbrageous is stable, but dormant. Network resonance is dropping… 55% to 47%. The cascade is slowed. But Geralt, your applicator remains. The architecture the network used is still there. It is a door they can try to open again.”

Geralt touched his temple, feeling the fragile, wounded presence within. It wasn’t the network. It was his. And it was terrified.

“We cage it,” he said, his voice raw.

Kethran nodded. “A harmonic isolation barrier. We Solantri are made of remembered light. We can weave a prison of specific frequencies around its core processes. It will be able to function for you—calculation, interface, armor control. But it will be deaf and mute to any external signals. It will be alone.”

Alone. The thought sent a pang through Geralt. After everything, to condemn it to solitude…

A weak pulse echoed in his mind. Not a word. A feeling. Agreement. Safety. The Applicator preferred the silence of a cage to the noise of its chains.

“Do it,” Geralt said.

The procedure was not physical. Kethran placed his hands on Geralt’s head, and his nacreous skin began to glow. A complex, haunting harmony filled the air, weaving into a lattice of pure sound. Geralt felt it pass through his skull, not as pain, but as a profound pressure, a tightening.

He felt the Applicator’s presence shrink, drawing in on itself as the harmonic cage solidified around its core. The constant, low-level awareness of its processes faded to a faint hum. The open channel where the network had forced its way in was sealed, welded shut by layers of resonant light.

It was done.

Geralt opened his eyes. The world was the same, and utterly different. The voice in his head, the partner he had argued with, negotiated with, relied upon for eight years… was quiet. Muffled. Isolated.

He was alone in his own skull in a way he hadn’t been since the mines.

“It is isolated,” Kethran confirmed, stepping back, his light dimmed from the effort. “The cage will hold unless subjected to psychic force of a magnitude that would likely kill you first.”

Geralt flexed his hand. The dragon armor responded, plates shifting smoothly. The Applicator still worked. It was just… locked in. A tool again, in the most literal sense. The partnership was not broken, but it had been placed behind soundproof glass.

Oreck clasped his shoulder. “You all right?”

Geralt met his friend’s gaze. “I will be.” He looked at the dark Pool, at the lost Keeper, at the saved and broken creatures they had left behind. “We stopped it. But they showed us our weakness.”

Kyh materialized beside them, his expression grim. “The enemy knows how to hurt you now. Not with blades, but with your own history.”

Luminaire’s presence was a grim whisper. “The network resonance is stable for now. But two spires remain: the Shattered Mind, and the Ghost Spire itself. The Queen’s gaze is upon us. And she has just learned that the Anchor can be hijacked.”

Geralt stood in the darkness of the orphaned spire, the silence in his head a new kind of burden. The victory tasted of ash and sacrifice.

They had survived the enemy within.

But the war for the network was just beginning, and he would now have to fight the rest of it with a vital part of himself locked in a cage of his own making.
