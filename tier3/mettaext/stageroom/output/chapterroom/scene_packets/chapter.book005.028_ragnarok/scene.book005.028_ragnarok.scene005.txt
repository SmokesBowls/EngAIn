 The Duel

The Anunnaki Overseer was magnificent—there was no other word adequate to describe being who represented divine authority made manifest, who radiated power that transcended simple physical presence.

Twelve feet tall, golden flesh gleaming in doubled sunlight, features that combined humanoid structure with proportions that suggested different evolutionary path, different environmental pressures, different assumptions about what conscious beings should look like.

Eyes that weren't eyes—sophisticated sensors that processed information across spectrums human perception couldn't access, that made the Overseer's awareness exceed normal biological limitations.

And the voice—when it spoke, reality rippled:

"SLAVE."

Single word that carried weight of cosmic authority, of millennia of unquestioned supremacy, of genuine confusion that property had somehow acquired capability to challenge ownership.

"You dare approach this facility? You dare attack authorized Anunnaki operation? You dare claim armor you have no right to possess?"

Geralt stood in the compound's center—volunteers behind him, guards disabled around him, enslaved population watching from carefully maintained distance. Helm still retracted. Face still visible. Identity still claimed openly despite every tactical reason to hide.

"I am Geralt," he said with calm that came from consciousness contested by two other entities, from self-awareness forged through trials that tested character rather than simple strength. "I was slave in Compound 7-Alpha. Then I chose differently. Stopped the whip. Killed the Overseer. Freed my people. Claimed this armor."

"Now I'm here to extend that choice to others still enslaved."

The Overseer descended from the central structure—each step measured, deliberate, radiating confidence that came from never having experienced genuine threat, from existence where authority was absolute and resistance was impossible.

"You are aberration. Malfunction. Error that cosmic order will correct. The Federation has designated you Ragnarok—threat requiring elimination. I will claim that distinction. I will present your head to Anu himself as demonstration that even aberrations can be resolved through proper application of force."

Weapons materialized from the Overseer's form—not carried but integrated, biological and technological merged into systems that made distinction meaningless. Energy projectors that could level buildings. Defensive fields that could deflect projectiles. Combat capability that made individual Anunnaki worth entire military units of lesser species.

"Last chance," the Overseer continued. "Surrender. Accept that cosmic order is absolute. Die quickly rather than learning through prolonged suffering that gods cannot be defied by property."

Geralt felt the armor respond—Zaron's consciousness flooding with readiness that bordered on eagerness, the Applicator providing tactical analysis that suggested dozens of approach vectors and probability assessments, his own will hardening into determination that this being, this representation of everything wrong with cosmic order, would fall.

"No," he said simply. "You're not god. You're just being with better technology and cultural assumptions about supremacy. And I'm going to prove that superiority is choice not natural order."

He raised his armored hands—scales gleaming, power building, three consciousnesses unified in purpose despite constant internal disagreement.

"Let's see if gods bleed."

The Overseer attacked.

Energy beams blazed—not crude projectiles but focused particle streams that could vaporize targets or punch through armor depending on intensity selected. The air itself ionized, carrying ozone scent and death promise.

Geralt dodged—armor responding with increasing confidence as Zaron learned combat rhythms, as the Applicator provided predictions based on energy signatures, as three consciousnesses began functioning as true unified system.

He channeled telekinetic force—not trying to match the Overseer's technological advantage but leveraging capability the Anunnaki couldn't replicate, pulling environment itself into the battle. Sand rose in waves, creating barriers and projectiles. Debris from shattered gate became improvised ammunition. The compound itself became weapon against being who'd built it.

The Overseer adjusted tactics—energy projectors shifting from precision strikes to area saturation, trying to overwhelm defensive capability through sheer volume rather than targeted application.

Geralt felt the armor strain—Zaron's consciousness pushed to limits of what adolescent weapon could handle, the Applicator managing power distribution to prevent catastrophic failure, his own flesh beneath scales feeling heat that suggested boundaries were being approached.

We need advantage, the Applicator noted clinically. Current trajectory suggests mutual exhaustion rather than decisive victory. Recommend—

I know, Geralt interrupted, seeing the opening, recognizing the pattern in the Overseer's attacks. Zaron, can you give me flight? Real flight, not panicked ascent?

Maybe? Zaron said uncertainly. The wings work, I've been practicing while you sleep, but coordinated aerial combat is—

Try.

The wings deployed—not randomly this time but deliberately, catching air with precision that suggested Zaron had indeed been practicing, that adolescent armor was maturing faster than any of them had anticipated.

Geralt rose—not tumbling but ascending with control that approached grace, that put him above the Overseer's primary firing arcs, that changed three-dimensional combat from ground-based to truly volumetric.

From aerial position, he could see the Overseer's defensive fields—energy patterns that surrounded golden flesh with protection that could deflect most attacks. But patterns had gaps. Necessary weaknesses where joints required flexibility, where sensors needed unobstructed access, where even divine technology had to make compromises.

He dove—armor responding to intention, wings folding to maximize speed, entire mass becoming projectile that carried force magnified by gravity and acceleration.

The Overseer raised defensive fields—energy barriers that should have stopped impact, that should have protected against kinetic assault.

But Geralt wasn't relying on kinetic force alone. He channeled telekinesis through the armor—the Applicator amplifying power, Zaron providing animation that made his dive more dramatic than necessary, his own will focusing energy into single point that coincided with gap in defensive fields.

Impact.

Not with the Overseer's flesh but with the ground beside it—telekinetic force channeling through point of contact to create shockwave that bypassed defensive barriers, that struck from angle the Anunnaki hadn't protected.

The Overseer staggered—first sign of vulnerability, first crack in absolute certainty, first suggestion that perhaps cosmic order wasn't as absolute as claimed.

"Impossible," it said—not shouting but genuinely confused. "You're slave. Property. You shouldn't be able to—"

"That's your problem," Geralt interrupted, pulling himself upright, armor settling around him with confidence that weeks of practice had developed. "You keep thinking ownership determines capability. That designation defines possibility. That cosmic law reflects natural order rather than constructed hierarchy."

He channeled more telekinetic force—not attacking directly but pulling the Overseer's weapons systems, interfering with the energy projectors, creating friction in mechanisms that had operated flawlessly for centuries.

"I'm not your property. Not your slave. Not limited by your assumptions about what former slaves can achieve when they choose freedom over compliance."

The Overseer attempted counterattack—weapons charging despite interference, defensive fields reinforcing, combat systems adapting to recognize that this wasn't aberration that could be dismissed but genuine threat requiring full response.

But adaptation took time. And time was resource Geralt had denied through strategic positioning, through coordinated assault from multiple vectors, through being surrounded by allies who'd ensure the Overseer remained focused on single target.

Mika and her team positioned at building corners—not attacking but ready, weapons acquired from storage pointed at the Overseer should it try to flee or target enslaved population.

Oreck moved through the compound—ensuring guards remained contained, that casualties were minimized, that liberation remained controlled rather than becoming massacre.

The volunteers watched—bearing witness to confrontation between former slave and divine authority, to demonstration that cosmic order could be defied through choice and courage.

And the enslaved population—two hundred beings who'd lived under assumption that resistance was impossible—watched with expressions transforming from shock to desperate hope to slowly growing certainty that perhaps, perhaps, liberation was real rather than fantasy designed to crush spirits more thoroughly than simple oppression.

Geralt pressed the attack—not trying for elegant combat but recognizing this was demonstration rather than simply tactical engagement, that killing the Overseer mattered less than proving it could be done, that divine authority was negotiable rather than absolute.

He channeled telekinesis in patterns the Applicator suggested—pulling at joints, interfering with sensors, creating cascade failures in systems that had never been designed to withstand concentrated assault from single source.

The Overseer's weapons failed—energy projectors sputtering, defensive fields flickering, combat capability degrading under sustained assault that exceeded design parameters.

"This... this is not..." the Overseer struggled to articulate what its consciousness couldn't process. "You are ERROR. You are MALFUNCTION. You cannot—"

"I already did," Geralt said quietly, channeling final surge of telekinetic force that tore through weakened defenses, that reached golden flesh beneath technological enhancement, that proved even gods could bleed when confronted by beings willing to reject their authority.

The Overseer collapsed—not dying immediately but wounded beyond ability to continue, consciousness that had never experienced genuine defeat trying to process reality that violated everything it had been taught about natural order.

Geralt stood over the fallen being—armor gleaming, wings still deployed, face visible to everyone watching.

"Your authority ends here," he said—speaking to the Overseer but addressing everyone present. "Your assumption that ownership equals right. Your belief that cosmic order justifies enslavement. Your certainty that gods determine what conscious beings deserve."

"It all ends. Now. With choice rather than compliance. With freedom rather than submission. With recognition that consciousness deserves agency regardless of species or origin or designation in your precious hierarchies."

He looked at enslaved population—two hundred beings watching, waiting, hoping.

"You're free. All of you. Not because I grant freedom but because freedom was always yours. Stolen through modification and conditioning and cultural assumptions, but never truly lost. Just... suppressed. Until someone demonstrated that resistance was possible."

"Choose for yourselves now. Choose names. Choose voices. Choose lives that transcend designation and property and cosmic regulations."

"Choose to be people rather than slaves."

Silence held for heartbeat. Two. Three.

Then—one being stepped forward. Then another. Then dozens. The enslaved population moving toward the fallen Overseer not with violence but with recognition that the being before them no longer represented absolute authority, that divine mandate had limits, that cosmic order could be contested.

Mika approached Geralt—weapon still held but pointed safely away, expression showing pride and exhaustion in equal measure.

"The compound is secure," she reported. "Guards are contained. Casualties minimal—three dead on our side, seven among defenders. Communication systems destroyed. They can't call for help."

"And the newly freed?"

"Processing. Some are still in shock. Others are already asking questions about how this happened, how we did it, how they can help do it again somewhere else."

She paused, smile crossing features scarred by enslavement but illuminated by accomplishment.

"Liberation is contagious. You were right about that. It spreads faster than they can contain."

Geralt felt the armor settle around him—Zaron's consciousness pleased with combat performance despite earlier fears, the Applicator compiling tactical data that would improve future operations, his own awareness trying to process that they'd actually done it, that deliberate liberation campaign had succeeded, that proof of concept was complete.

Two compounds freed, Zaron observed. How many more until Earth is truly liberated?

Unknown, the Applicator replied. But each success increases probability of subsequent successes. Freed slaves become liberators. Liberators teach tactics. Tactics spread faster than Anunnaki can adapt. Momentum builds.

We're not stopping, Geralt thought to both of them. This was proof that it works. That liberation can be taught. That what happened at our compound wasn't miracle—it was pattern that can be replicated.

Two more compounds within marching distance. Then we expand operations. Coordinate between liberated facilities. Build network that transcends single compound. Create movement that can challenge galactic empire through accumulated choice rather than single dramatic confrontation.

He looked at the fallen Overseer—being who represented everything wrong with cosmic order, who'd viewed conscious beings as property, who'd died confused that slaves could possess capability to challenge authority.

"We're coming for all of them," Geralt said quietly—speaking to corpse that couldn't hear, to cosmic order that would learn slowly and painfully that some things couldn't be maintained through force alone. "Every compound. Every Overseer. Every population still enslaved because galaxy decided their freedom was negotiable."

"We're Ragnarok. And your world—your cosmic order built on enslavement—is ending. One liberation at a time."

The twin suns descended, painting the newly freed compound in shades that suggested either blood or promise.

Geralt chose promise.

Again.

Always.

Because freedom was worth any price.
