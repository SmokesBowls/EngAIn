scene 028.1 — the warning

scene meta:
  location: Liberated Compound / Eastern Boundary
  time: year 17,503, twenty-eight days after an Anunnaki patrol witnessed the liberated compound and departed to report it
  participants: Geralt, Oreck, Applicator, Zaron, Enforcer Vel'thara, Federation Compliance Personnel
  focus: Enforcer Vel'thara arrives at the liberated compound and demands the surrender of the Dragon Mail; Geralt refuses to hide his face or surrender the armor, initiating an open confrontation.
  continuity: Follows chapter 027 and twenty-eight days of preparation / Dragon Mail training; the patrol/report event referenced by the chapter is not presently shown in chapters 025–027
  presentation: cutscene
  cutscene purpose: Introduce Galactic Federation Enforcer Vel'thara, establish the threat of artifact confiscation, and mark Geralt's open defiance.

Four weeks.

Twenty-eight days since Anunnaki patrol had witnessed the compound's impossible defiance, since they'd fled Earth to report what should never have been possible—slaves speaking, Overseer dead, resistance organized and successful.

Twenty-eight days of preparation. Of training. Of transforming freed slaves into something approaching fighting force. Of Geralt learning to coordinate with adolescent armor that remained unpredictable despite Zaron's best efforts and the Applicator's technical mediation.

Twenty-eight days of waiting for consequences they all knew were coming.

Geralt stood watch on the compound's eastern boundary—the direction from which threats had historically arrived, the approach any sensible military force would use given terrain advantages and sight lines. The twin suns were descending, painting the desert in shades that made distance difficult to judge, shadows stretching long enough to hide entire armies if positioned correctly.

Beside him, Oreck scanned the horizon with predator's awareness that centuries of military service had ingrained too deeply to ever fully fade.

"It's been four weeks," the reptilian said quietly, breaking silence that had stretched for nearly an hour. "Standard Anunnaki protocol for anomaly assessment and response is thirty days. Sometimes less if the threat is deemed severe enough."

Geralt felt the armor shift slightly around him—Zaron's consciousness stirring at mention of potential combat, the Applicator running probability calculations that produced numbers neither comforting nor surprising.

"You think they're coming back."

"I know they're coming back." Oreck's vertical pupils contracted, focusing on something at horizon's edge that Geralt's merely human vision couldn't yet distinguish. "The question isn't if, but what they're bringing. We killed an Overseer. Broke a compound. Proved resistance is possible. They'll send enough force to ensure it never happens again."

"How much force?"

"Depends on who's responding." Oreck's claws flexed unconsciously—combat reflexes activating despite conscious control. "If it's Anunnaki military responding to Overseer's death, we'll face standard garrison. Thirty to forty soldiers, maybe two commanders, heavy weapons but conventional tactics."

He paused, and something in his posture shifted—awareness sharpening beyond normal alertness into recognition of specific threat.

"But that's not Anunnaki ship configuration."

Geralt followed Oreck's gaze, and now he could see it—distant gleam that suggested metal catching fading sunlight, shape that grew more distinct with each passing moment. Not golden angular Anunnaki design, but something sleeker, more elegant, carrying authority that transcended simple military function.

"Federation," Oreck said with tone that mixed respect and concern. "They're not here to punish. They're here to assess. To verify evacuation compliance and ensure galactic law is being properly maintained."

Host, the Applicator's presence consolidated with sharp attention. Galactic Federation operates under different protocols than Anunnaki military. They're authorized to confiscate unauthorized artifacts, to arrest individuals violating cosmic law, to enforce regulations that supersede local authority.

They can take the armor. Legally. And we would have no recourse.

Geralt felt Zaron's consciousness bristle at the implication—fragmented god-consciousness that had sacrificed everything to become weapon wouldn't accept being "confiscated" like contraband.

Over my scattered fragments, Zaron snarled. No bureaucrat takes what I've become.

"Should we hide?" Geralt asked Oreck. "Pull back into the compound? Pretend we're still simple freed slaves maintaining facilities?"

"Too late." Oreck gestured toward the approaching ship. "They're already scanning. Already cataloging. They know we're here, know we're armed, know we're organized beyond what freed slave population should be."

The reptilian turned to face Geralt directly, vertical pupils showing concern that transcended tactical assessment.

"Close your helm. Now. Before they get visual confirmation. If they identify you—if they record your face and correlate it with the armor—you'll be hunted across the galaxy for the rest of your existence."

Geralt's hand rose toward the helm controls, muscle memory prepared to trigger the sealing mechanism that would hide his features behind draconic faceplate.

Then stopped.

"No."

"Geralt—"

"I said no." He lowered his hand, meeting Oreck's gaze with certainty that came from consciousness contested by two other entities, from self-awareness forged through trials that tested character rather than simple strength. "I'm not hiding. Not my face. Not what I've done. Not who I've become."

"They'll flag you. Your identity will be recorded, distributed, marked as enemy of galactic order. Every bounty hunter, every Federation enforcer, every Anunnaki who wants revenge—they'll all know exactly who you are."

"Good." Geralt's voice carried conviction that surprised even himself. "Let them know. I freed these people. I killed the Overseer. I claimed this armor. I'm not ashamed of any of it, and I won't hide behind a mask like I did something wrong."

Oreck stared at him for long moment, then made sound that might have been laugh or sigh or resignation to inevitable consequences of partnering with someone whose defiance transcended survival instinct.

"Then we face them together. Openly. And deal with whatever follows."

The Federation ship descended—approach vector calculated for maximum intimidation, engines generating pressure waves that kicked up sand and announced authority before single word was spoken. It settled fifty yards from the compound's boundary, landing struts extending with mechanical precision that spoke to technology centuries beyond anything Earth possessed.

A ramp descended, and from the ship's interior—they emerged.

Six figures. Not soldiers, though they carried themselves with military discipline. Not diplomats, though their bearing suggested authority beyond simple force. Enforcers. Auditors. Beings who existed to ensure galactic law was maintained regardless of local circumstances or moral considerations.

The lead figure was tall—impossibly tall, easily eight feet in height—humanoid but clearly not human. Skin that gleamed like polished obsidian, eyes that were solid white without pupil or iris, features that suggested both beauty and complete absence of empathy. Robes that seemed woven from starlight itself, carrying rank insignia Geralt didn't recognize but understood instinctively meant authority that superseded planetary governments.

Behind the leader, five others—different species, different builds, all carrying scanning equipment and data tablets that glowed with information being collected in real-time.

The leader's white eyes fixed on Geralt—on the armor, on his exposed face, on the wing-mounts and scales and technology that screamed Aeon Keeper origin.

When it spoke, the voice carried harmonics that suggested multiple speakers despite single source:

"I am Enforcer Vel'thara of the Galactic Federation Compliance Division. We are here to verify Anunnaki evacuation protocols have been properly executed and to assess any irregularities requiring corrective action."

The white eyes never blinked, never wavered from Geralt's face.

"You are in possession of unauthorized Aeon Keeper artifact. Surrender the Dragon Mail immediately for confiscation and await transport to Federation processing facility for questioning regarding acquisition method and intended usage."

Geralt felt the armor tense around him—Zaron's consciousness reacting to threat of separation, the Applicator calculating probability of successful resistance, his own will hardening into refusal that transcended rational thought.

"No."

The single word hung in air between them—defiance given sound, rebellion distilled into syllable that carried weight beyond its brevity.

Vel'thara's expression didn't change—couldn't change, given features that seemed carved from living stone rather than formed through biological processes.

"That was not request. That was statement of legal requirement under Galactic Federation Artifact Control Regulation, Section 7, Subsection 12. Unauthorized possession of Aeon Keeper technology constitutes Class-1 violation punishable by artifact confiscation and mandatory detention."

"I don't care about your regulations," Geralt said with calmness that belied the fury building behind his words. "This armor was given to me. Prepared for me. Bonded to my soul by Aeon Keeper who chose to circumvent laws that prevented direct intervention. I'm not surrendering it because some bureaucrat decided freedom is illegal."

One of the other Federation personnel—shorter, with crystalline features that suggested silicon-based biology—stepped forward, data tablet glowing with information being compiled in real-time.

"Subject identification in progress," it announced in voice like wind chimes. "Species: Human. Origin: Earth, Sol System. Former designation: Slave 7-Alpha-9327. Current self-designation: Geralt. Genetic modifications detected—unauthorized vocal capability restoration, neural pathway alterations suggesting Nephoretti intervention."

The crystalline being's features shifted into what might have been surprise.

"Additional anomaly detected: Anunnaki Applicator construct present in subject's neural tissue. Symbiotic rather than controlling. Unprecedented behavioral deviation from standard operational parameters."

Vel'thara's attention shifted fractionally—white eyes showing first hint of something beyond cold assessment.

"You carry Anunnaki control device willingly? Explain."

"It's complicated," Geralt said, feeling the Applicator's presence stir with something approaching indignation at being referred to as "device."

Very complicated, it muttered.

"Irrelevant," Vel'thara continued. "The artifact must be surrendered. The Applicator must be extracted. You will be processed through standard Federation protocols for individuals found in violation of multiple galactic statutes."

"And the others?" Geralt gestured toward the compound, toward three hundred freed slaves who'd gathered to watch confrontation, toward Mika and the Five and all the others who'd claimed voices and names and lives that transcended designation. "What happens to them?"

"They will be relocated to appropriate facilities pending determination of their status. The Anunnaki evacuation order remains in effect. This compound will be sealed and decommissioned. All parties will comply with Federation directives."

Something cold settled into Geralt's chest—recognition that this being, this enforcer of cosmic law, viewed conscious beings as problems requiring processing rather than people deserving consideration.

"No," he said again, this time with finality that transcended negotiation. "We're not going anywhere. We're not surrendering anything. This is our compound now. Our home. We freed it through blood and choice and courage. Your laws don't apply to us because your laws enabled our enslavement."

Vel'thara's expression remained unchanged, but something in its posture shifted—recognition that compliance would not be achieved through declarations of authority.

"Then you leave us no choice. Enforcement protocols activate. Subject designated hostile. Artifact confiscation to proceed through application of necessary force."

The other five Federation personnel moved with synchronized precision—weapons emerging from concealed holsters, energy fields activating around their forms, positioning that suggested military training despite civilian designation.

Geralt felt the armor respond—Zaron's consciousness flooding with readiness that bordered on eagerness, the Applicator's calculations shifting from probability assessment to tactical analysis, his own body tensing with anticipation of violence he'd hoped to avoid but accepted as inevitable.

"Last chance," Vel'thara said without emotion. "Surrender peacefully. Minimize casualties. Accept processing."

Geralt's answer was simple:

He raised his armored hand—scales gleaming in doubled sunlight, power radiating from technology that had been prepared for exactly this moment—and channeled telekinetic force that would have been crude weeks before but had grown more refined through practice and partnership with consciousnesses that understood power beyond normal human capacity.

The sand between them erupted—wall of silicon and minerals rising to block sight lines, to create barrier that bought seconds of tactical advantage.

"ORECK!" Geralt shouted. "Get the compound to cover! This is going to get ugly!"

The reptilian didn't argue, didn't question—simply moved with speed that belied his massive form, herding freed slaves toward structures that might provide protection from energy weapons and Federation enforcement protocols.

Through the settling sand, Vel'thara's voice carried with unchanged calmness:

"Subject identified as hostile. Designation assigned: RAGNAROK. Threat level: Maximum. Enforcement personnel—neutralize with extreme prejudice. Artifact confiscation is secondary to threat elimination."

Ragnarok? Zaron asked with confusion. What kind of designation is—

Norse mythology, the Applicator supplied. End of world. Twilight of gods. Apocalyptic destruction. They're calling us the end of their order.

I like it, Zaron decided.

"Can we focus?" Geralt snapped, then threw himself sideways as energy bolt tore through space he'd occupied heartbeat before.

The battle began in earnest.
