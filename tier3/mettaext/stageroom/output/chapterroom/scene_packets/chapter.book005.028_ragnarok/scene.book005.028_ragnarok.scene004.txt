scene 028.4 — the approach and the chaos

 The Approach

Dawn broke over the compound with light that suggested normalcy—another day in endless series, another rotation of labor and compliance, another confirmation that cosmic order remained intact and slaves remained property rather than people.

Mika led her infiltration team toward mining operations that extended beyond compound walls—work parties supervised by single guard rather than full security detail, vulnerability that existed because Anunnaki had never conceived that slaves might coordinate resistance across compounds.

They looked like what they were—scarred, modified, bearing physical markers of enslavement that made them indistinguishable from the two hundred beings already laboring under Anunnaki authority. No weapons visible. No signs of coordination. Just another group of property performing assigned functions.

The guard barely glanced at them—vertical pupils registering presence but not threat, consciousness that had spent decades viewing slaves as objects incapable of defiance. Conditioning that was about to prove fatal.

Mika and her team integrated with existing work parties, moving with practiced subservience that hid awareness and capability beneath mask of compliance. They'd spent weeks learning to weaponize the Anunnaki's expectations, to use assumptions about slave incapability as tactical advantage.

One team member "accidentally" stumbled near guard post, creating distraction while another palmed crude weapon from concealed location. Another engaged overseer in gesture-language about work quotas, buying seconds for positioning that placed infiltrators near critical chokepoints.

By midmorning, Mika's team was distributed throughout the compound—six beings positioned near weapon storage, guard posts, communication systems, and the central structure where Overseer coordinated operations.

None of the enslaved population recognized them as outsiders. The guards noticed nothing unusual. Even the Overseer—visible occasionally through windows that showed golden flesh coordinating daily operations—remained unaware that his compound had already been compromised from within.

Mika checked positions through subtle gestures that looked like normal slave communication—confirmation that everyone was ready, that weapons were acquired, that chaos could be unleashed on signal.

Now they waited.

For the armored figure who would draw every eye while they struck from shadows.

For Ragnarok to announce himself.

---

Oreck positioned his assault team at the compound's rear boundary—fourteen volunteers spread along wall that separated facility from desert beyond, concealed behind rock formations and elevation changes that provided cover from casual observation.

The reptilian studied the wall with tactical assessment honed through centuries—identifying weakest points, calculating optimal breach locations, preparing to move with speed that would transform defensive barrier into open pathway within seconds of engagement beginning.

"Remember," he said quietly to those positioned near him, "we're not here to kill everyone. We're here to free everyone. The guards are tools, not architects of oppression. Disable if possible. Kill only if necessary."

Dren—young volunteer who'd spoken so eloquently about responsibility—nodded understanding while checking crude club he'd fashioned from salvaged materials.

"And the Overseer?"

"Geralt handles the Overseer," Oreck said with certainty. "That's not combat—it's demonstration. Proof that gods can be killed, that cosmic order can be defied, that freedom is possible even when everything suggests otherwise."

"Our job is everyone else. The guards, the slave drivers, anyone who tries to interfere with liberation. We're the hammer that compresses their forces toward the center where Geralt will be waiting."

He checked the sun's position—approaching zenith, time when shadows were minimal and visibility maximized. When armored figure approaching from open desert would be impossible to miss.

When Ragnarok would introduce himself to beings who'd never heard the designation, who didn't know liberation was spreading like fire through compounds that had seemed eternal.

"Soon," Oreck muttered. "Very soon now."

---

Geralt walked.

Not with stealth. Not with tactical awareness of cover and concealment. Not with anything approaching military discipline.

He simply walked up the main approach to the compound's front gate like he was visiting rather than attacking, like he had every right to be there, like the armor encasing him was normal rather than technology that screamed Aeon Keeper origin.

The Dragon Mail gleamed in doubled sunlight—scales catching radiance and refracting it in patterns that made him visible from miles away, that announced presence with authority that transcended verbal declaration. Wing-mounts folded but clearly present. Helm retracted, showing his face—human features that marked him as former slave rather than Anunnaki authority, that made his approach even more impossible to process through normal assumptions.

They see us, the Applicator noted clinically. Guard posts activating. Alert protocols engaging. Overseer being notified.

Good, Zaron said with satisfaction that bordered on anticipation. Let them see. Let them wonder. Let them try to understand why former slave approaches wearing technology they can't comprehend.

Geralt felt the armor settle around him with confidence that had been building over weeks of practice—Zaron learning to trust his judgment, the Applicator refining tactical suggestions rather than making demands, three consciousnesses functioning as unified system despite constant internal debate.

He reached the gate—massive barrier designed to contain rather than repel, to keep property from escaping rather than keeping enemies from entering.

A guard appeared above the wall—reptilian similar to Oreck but smaller, younger, carrying weapon with professional competence but expression mixing confusion with concern.

"Halt," the guard called out in Anunnaki dialect that Geralt's newly restored speech allowed him to comprehend. "Identify yourself. State your business. This is authorized Anunnaki facility operating under—"

"I am Geralt," he interrupted, voice amplified by armor's systems to carry across compound. "Also known as Ragnarok. Also known as the slave who killed his Overseer and freed his compound and claimed armor the Aeon Keepers prepared for exactly this purpose."

He gestured casually at the gate between them—scales gleaming, power radiating with intensity that made air itself shimmer.

"I'm here to liberate your compound. To free the slaves you call property. To kill your Overseer and demonstrate that cosmic order is negotiable rather than absolute."

The guard stared—incomprehension warring with recognition that something fundamental had shifted, that assumptions about slave compliance were being directly challenged by being who should have been cowering rather than confronting.

"You..." the guard managed, "you're insane. One slave against entire compound? Against Overseer blessed by Anu himself? Against guard force trained for—"

"Correction," Geralt interrupted again, allowing small smile to cross his features. "Not one slave. An organized liberation force. Some of whom are already inside your compound. Waiting."

He raised his hand—armored gauntlet catching light with dramatic flair that Zaron provided despite Geralt's eye-roll at the theatrical addition.

"Last chance. Open the gate. Release the slaves. Withdraw peacefully. Live to tell Federation enforcement that some fights aren't worth the casualties."

The guard's expression hardened—confusion giving way to determination born of centuries of conditioning, of training that said Anunnaki authority was absolute and slave resistance was impossible.

"We will never—"

Geralt didn't let him finish.

He channeled telekinetic force through the armor—the Applicator amplifying power, Zaron providing animation that made gesture more dramatic than necessary, his own will directing energy toward gate that had never been designed to withstand assault from outside.

The gate exploded inward—metal shrieking, stone cracking, barrier that had contained property for decades suddenly becoming scattered debris and open pathway.

The signal had been given.

Inside the compound, Mika and her team struck.

---

The infiltrators moved with synchronization that came from weeks of training—six beings acting as unified force despite physical separation, coordinating through gesture-language and shared understanding of tactical objectives.

Weapon storage was compromised first—guard positioned there collapsing as crude club connected with skull, keys acquired, energy weapons distributed to slaves who'd only ever held mining equipment suddenly grasping technology that could punch through armor or vaporize targets.

Communication systems went dark—crystalline relay that connected compound to Anunnaki network shattered through telekinetic force that Mika had been developing, that remained crude compared to Geralt's capability but sufficient for sabotage.

Guard posts erupted in confusion—beings trained to watch for external threat suddenly facing attacks from directions they'd never anticipated, from slaves who should have been compliant suddenly demonstrating coordination and capability that violated every assumption about property's limitations.

By the time Geralt's telekinetic explosion announced external assault, half the compound's guard force was already disabled or scrambling to understand attacks from inside.

The enslaved population—two hundred beings who'd lived under assumption that resistance was impossible—watched with expressions mixing shock and desperate hope as their world inverted, as slaves became liberators and guards became victims.

Some remained frozen by conditioning too deep to overcome through mere observation. Others began moving toward the fighting—not attacking but positioning, preparing, waiting to see if freedom was real or simply new form of torment designed to crush hope more thoroughly than simple oppression.

Through the shattered gate, Geralt entered—armor moving with confidence that weeks of practice had developed, that made him appear more competent than he felt given how recently the armor had been bonding and trying to flee from combat.

Guards converged on his position—seven beings carrying energy weapons, moving with professional coordination that suggested proper military training rather than simple enforcement capability.

Energy bolts blazed—Geralt dodged, throwing up telekinetic barriers that deflected most attacks, accepting minor hits on the armor that Zaron's consciousness absorbed and redistributed without allowing damage to reach flesh beneath.

He didn't try for elegant combat. Didn't attempt elaborate techniques. Simply channeled power the armor provided—telekinetically grabbing guards and slamming them into walls or each other, creating chaos that prevented coordinated assault, demonstrating that former slave now possessed capability that transcended everything they'd been taught about natural order.

From the rear, Oreck breached.

The wall exploded outward—not through subtle demolition but through reptilian applying brute force to structural weakness, through centuries of experience knowing exactly where to hit to transform barrier into rubble.

Fourteen volunteers poured through—not soldiers but freed slaves who'd chosen to extend liberation to others, who fought with desperation and determination rather than professional discipline.

The compound's guard force found themselves caught in three-way convergence—attacks from inside, outside, and rear simultaneously, coordination they couldn't match with training that had never anticipated slave resistance would be possible.

Within minutes, the tactical situation became clear:

The guards were losing.

Not through superior numbers or better equipment, but through strategic positioning and element of surprise and willingness to fight for cause that transcended simple survival.

One guard attempted to flee—Mika's team blocked the exit, weapons acquired from storage giving them capability to enforce containment they'd once been subjected to.

Another guard tried to rally defensive formation—Oreck's massive form shattered the attempt, reptilian strength scattering beings like leaves before storm.

A third guard raised weapon toward enslaved population—Geralt's telekinetic force caught the being mid-motion, slammed it into ground with enough force to stun but not kill.

"SURRENDER," Geralt's voice boomed across the compound—amplified by armor, carrying authority that transcended simple volume. "Your forces are disabled. Your Overseer stands alone. This battle is over. Choose survival over pointless resistance."

Some guards dropped weapons immediately—training versus preservation instinct producing quick calculation that staying alive mattered more than defending abstract authority.

Others hesitated—conditioning warring with reality, centuries of belief that Anunnaki supremacy was absolute colliding with evidence that slaves could coordinate, could fight, could WIN.

The few who continued fighting were disabled quickly—not through death but through overwhelming force, through being surrounded by beings who'd chosen mercy despite having every reason for vengeance.

Within twenty minutes of Geralt breaching the gate, the compound's entire guard force was neutralized.

Disabled. Disarmed. Contained.

And in the central structure—the Overseer emerged.
