ACT 2: THE TRACKING

The scout was good. Even injured, even mentally fraying, his tradecraft was impeccable.

They found false trails that dead-ended in unstable silica sinks. They passed through narrow defiles rigged with collapse charges that hadn’t been triggered—as if he’d recognized their signature and chosen not to fire. On the third day, they found his message.

It was carved into the face of a black obsidian cliff, not with tools, but burned into the stone with raw chroniton energy. The letters shimmered, existing in multiple time-states at once, fading in and out of perception.

STOP FOLLOWING.
I’M DONE.
THE WAR IS OVER.
LET IT STAY DEAD.

“He thinks we’re recruitment,” Geralt said, staring at the painfully etched words.

“He is not incorrect,” Luminaire replied, a note of sorrow in her harmonics. “But we are not recruiting a soldier. We are seeking a surgeon.”

The scout’s chroniton signature grew stronger, a lighthouse in the temporal fog, but also more chaotic. The applicator began glitching, showing multiple biosign readings layered over each other—a Vek’tar who was sleeping, running, fighting, dying, all at the same coordinates.

“He is phasing,” Luminaire warned. “Losing cohesion. If he slips fully between timelines, we will lose him forever.”
