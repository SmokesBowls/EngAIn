ACT 1: THE EASTERN WASTES

The Eastern Fume-Wastes were a geological epitaph. Three weeks of hard travel had taken them from the red stone of the badlands into a landscape of shattered obsidian plains and roiling, mustard-yellow fog. The air tasted of acid and regret. This was the scar tissue of an ancient cataclysm—a battle so fierce it had glassed the earth and poisoned the sky.

"Inversion threshold holding at 55.7%," Luminaire reported, her voice a constant, low-frequency hum in Geralt’s neural architecture. "But the bleed from Umbra Planes is now affecting adjacent dimensional strata. The clock is no longer ticking. It is accelerating."

Twice, Federation skirmishers had passed overhead—angular, grey ships moving in a precise search grid.

“They’re not patrolling,” Oreck grunted, his scales dulled by the toxic dust. “They’re hunting. Your face is on bulletins across three sectors. ‘Fugitive. Armed. Extremely dangerous.’ They even used your good portrait.”

Geralt’s armor, sensing the elevated threat, had maintained a low-level defensive charge, making the air around him smell of ozone. The applicator scrolled constant threat assessments, but the true guide was the resonance map Luminaire projected onto his senses—a pulsing, unstable star of chroniton decay ahead of them.

“His signature is five kilometers northeast,” she said. “The instability is… profound. He is experiencing multiple temporal states simultaneously. The loneliness has not helped.”

Oreck’s expression darkened. “Chroniton sickness eats the mind. You need an anchor. A person, a purpose, a place. Something to tell you which ‘now’ is real. Without it…” He didn’t finish. He didn’t need to.

They found the settlement by smell first—the acrid stench of recent phospor-fire, layered over the deeper rot of abandoned lives. A cluster of prefab domes, blasted open. Scorched earth. No bodies.

“Weeks old,” Oreck said, kneeling to examine the blast patterns. “He was here. They found him. He left before they could close the net.” He pointed to a single set of tracks leading east—an Igigi, but with a pronounced, dragging limp. A dark, crystalline substance flecked the dust at intervals. Dried chroniton-tainted blood.

The scout was running. And he was hurt.
