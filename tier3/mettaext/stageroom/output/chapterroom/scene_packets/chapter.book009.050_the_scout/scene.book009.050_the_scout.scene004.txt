Epic 2: UMBRA

ACT 1: ARRIVAL - THE COST OF PASSAGE

They didn't so much land as congeal into existence on the obsidian plain.

Gravity was wrong—a faint, apologetic pull that made every movement feel like drifting through water. The air wasn't breathable so much as tolerable, tasting of cold copper, ozone, and something older: the scent of stone that had never seen light.

Behind them, the ragged portal Luminaire had torn shimmered once and sealed with a sound like a vault door closing in a distant universe. The Federation was gone. The silence that replaced their pursuit was worse.

Geralt hit the ground on his knees, vomiting nothing but bile and light. The neural scars along his spine burned white-hot. The Anchor bond wasn't just stretched—it was lacerated. Each of Luminaire's three failed jumps had ripped through him like sympathetic damage, a psychic feedback loop of catastrophic arrival.

"Where is she?" Oreck growled, cleaver already in hand, his body a fortress in the formless dark.

A whisper, fractured and weak: "Here. Reconstituting."

Thirty feet away, light began to pool on the ground like spilled mercury. It coalesced, rose, and struggled to hold a shape. Luminaire manifested—not as her former radiance, but as a ghost of herself. Translucent. Flickering at the edges like a failing hologram. Her form wavered between a humanoid silhouette and abstract geometric patterns, unable to settle.

"Three attempts," her voice was static layered over broken glass. "I underestimated the dimensional hostility. This realm... rejects light on a ontological level. Each arrival was less a transit and more a forced surgery. The trauma is... significant."

She dimmed, her light guttering. "I need time to stabilize. Hours. Perhaps days."

Vek'tar was already on his knees, trembling not from injury but perception. His good eye was wide, the white one glowing with captured chroniton ghosts. "We don't have days. I see it—timelines collapsing toward a single event. The Umbra Sovereign knows we're here. And it's... curious."

He pointed a shaking finger toward the horizon. Shapes moved in the darkness. Not approaching. Converging. Humanoid shadows with no faces, walking in patterns that made the mind ache to follow. Dozens. Then hundreds.

Geralt forced himself upright. The dragon armor deployed across his shoulders and chest with a series of sharp, resonant clicks, its usual gold-and-crimson sheen muted in the lightless air. "Can you manage?"

Luminaire's laugh was the sound of crystal cracking. "I can barely maintain coherence. But I can observe. And warn. Those entities... they are not attacking. They are protocol. You are being summoned."

As if on cue, the shadow-figures formed two precise lines, creating a corridor of living darkness that led toward a distant, jagged structure—the Umbra Spire, a negative-image lighthouse against a starless void.

Oreck's tail lashed. "So we walk into the throne room of a primordial shadow god while our only advantage is a ghost who can't hold her shape."

Geralt wiped blood-tinged light from his lips. "We didn't come here because it was safe. We came because it was necessary. Now it's just... honest."

They stepped into the corridor of shadows, and the darkness closed behind them like a mouth.
