ACT 3: THE AMBUSH THAT WASN’T

They cornered him in a box canyon of jagged, purple crystalline formations. The air crackled with displaced chroniton particles.

“He’s here,” Oreck said, hand resting on his cleaver’s hilt. “Above us. To the left.”

A voice, raw and exhausted, echoed from the stone itself. “I said… stop.”

He phased into visibility halfway up the cliff face, his body merged with the crystal as if he were part of its structure. Vek’tar. His skin was the color of tarnished copper, streaked with grey fatigue-lines. One eye was a sharp, intelligent amber. The other was clouded solid white, a permanent scar from chroniton feedback. In his hand was a cleaver similar to Oreck’s, but its edge was serrated with crystalline teeth that hummed with temporal dissonance.

“I’m not coming back, Oreck,” he spat, his voice cracking. “I’m not fighting your wars. I’m not dying for another lost cause. I’m not—” His good eye locked on Geralt. He froze.

The applicator flared: Subject: Vek’tar. Chroniton Decay: 78% (Critical). Temporal Displacement: Active. Cohesion Risk: Extreme.

Vek’tar stared, his expression shifting through shock, recognition, and a deep, weary horror. “You,” he breathed. “The Anchor. The debt-walker. I’ve seen you. In the folds. Always at the center of the unraveling.” He phased fully out of the cliff, dropping twenty feet to land in a crouch before them, wincing as his injured leg took the weight. He never took his eyes off Geralt. “Luminaire sent you. She wants the broken scout to navigate the dying jumps. Of course she does.”

It wasn’t a question.

---

ACT 4: THE NEGOTIATION

“Your name was Vek’tar,” Oreck said, his voice low. “Scout-Sergeant of the 7th Deep Strikers. You mapped the shifting voids around Xal’Zirith when no one else would.”

“Was,” Vek’tar echoed bitterly. “Was.he is dead. He died in the Hub, with the rest of them. I’m what the chroniton left behind.”

Luminaire’s voice whispered through Geralt, who spoke her words as his own. “Your sensitivity made you the Legion’s best navigator. You could feel the shape of dimensional folds before we entered them.”

“I could feel all the shapes!” Vek’tar shouted, a sudden, fractured rage twisting his features. “Every possible route! Every collapse! Every death! I didn’t navigate, Oreck! I chose which hell to fly through! And every time, I carried the ghosts of the crews who died in the routes I didn’t pick!” He clutched his head, the white eye glowing faintly. “I don’t dream anymore. I rerun the missions. All of them. The successful jumps and the catastrophic ones. I can’t tell which memories are mine.”

He looked at Oreck, his anger dissolving into pleading despair. “Your children. In the folds, I see them. Grown strong. Lying dead in the dust. Never born at all. Which one is real, Oreck? Which timeline am I in right now?”

Geralt took a step forward. “The one you choose to make real.”

Vek’tar let out a laugh that was half sob. “The philosopher Anchor. You want me to do it again. Navigate corrupted Void Jumps to spires on the verge of collapse. To prevent a dimensional unraveling.” He gestured to his own chest, to the flickering, unstable aura around him. “Look at me! I’m not a navigator! I’m a walking, talking unraveling!”

---

ACT 5: THE OFFER

Oreck moved then, not with threat, but with deliberate, solid purpose. He stood before Vek’tar, a pillar of unwavering present-tense reality.

“You navigated seventeen jumps for the Legion,” Oreck said, his voice like bedrock. “You saved hundreds of lives. You think I forgot?”

“I also lost sixteen men and women in the eighteenth jump at Mir’s Reach,” Vek’tar whispered, the memory flashing in his good eye. “You think I forgot?”

The silence was broken only by the faint, sickly crackle of the toxic winds.

Geralt spoke again, Luminaire’s resonance giving his words a double harmonic. “We are not asking you to be whole. We are asking you to be useful. One last time. Five jumps. Five spires. Then you are done. We find you a Keeper who can stabilize your decay. Luminaire can—”

“Luminaire can’t fix chroniton rot!” Vek’tar screamed, spittle flying. “No one can! It’s terminal! It’s the price for seeing too much! I am a dead man walking through a dozen corpses of my own future!”

“I cannot reverse it.” Luminaire’s voice spoke directly now, the harmonic layer separating from Geralt’s, ringing clear in the air between them. “But I can slow it. The Ghost Spire’s resonance can create a buffer. A tether to the present moment. I can give you years instead of months. Clarity instead of chaos. A single, stable timeline to walk.” She paused, letting the offer hang. “If you help us.”

Vek’tar stared, his chest heaving. The desperate hope that flickered in his eye was more painful than his rage. He was a drowning man being offered a rope he knew might be an illusion.

“What,” he finally rasped, “is the first target?”

---

ACT 6: UMBRA PLANES

Luminaire projected the network map. It hung in the air before them, visible to all three—a web of light and shadow. She focused on the Umbra Planes node. It was a cancer of darkness, tendrils of pure shadow bleeding out, infecting the luminous lattice around it.

“The Keeper is active, but its authority has been usurped. This is not Anunnaki corruption. It is older. Primordial. The Umbra Sovereign—an entity born in the places before light—has claimed the node. It is weaving the spire’s power into the fabric of its own shadow realm.”

Vek’tar’s good eye widened as he studied the map. He pointed a trembling finger at the bleeding tendrils. “The folds there… they aren’t just unstable. They’re inverted. Reality is turning inside out. Jumping there isn’t flying into a storm. It’s flying into a negative of a storm.”

“Can you do it?” Geralt asked.

Vek’tar let out a shaky breath that was almost a laugh. “Navigate into a shadow dimension ruled by an entity that eats light. Using temporal senses that show me five different ways we die on entry. While my own body is trying to exist in two places at once.” He looked at Geralt, a grim, broken smile on his face. “Perfect. When do we leave?”

“Two days,” Geralt started. “We need to rest, gather—”

A sudden, searing harmonic dissonance shrieked through Geralt’s mind, emanating from Luminaire. It was pure alarm.

“Federation fleet! They triangulated the network access spike from the map projection! Three cruisers, twelve fighters. Converging. ETA: Twenty minutes.”

---

ACT 7: THE CHASE BEGINS

There was no discussion. They ran.

Vek’tar led, phasing through solid rock when the path narrowed, emerging gasping and coughing up flecks of chroniton-blood. Oreck moved with brutal, ground-eating efficiency, cleaver in hand. Geralt’s armor deployed its wings with a thunderous snap; he grabbed Oreck under the arms and lifted off, skimming the canyon walls.

“Vek’tar! Can you phase us out?” Geralt yelled over the rising whine of approaching thrusters.

“Not all three! Not this distance!” Vek’tar cried out from ahead, his form flickering. “The energy required… I’d scatter your molecules across six different timelines!”

The ships were overhead. Searchlights carved through the toxic fog. An amplified voice, cold and official, boomed across the wastes.

“GERALT OF THE DRAGON. YOU ARE IN VIOLATION OF FEDERATION CONTAINMENT PROTOCOL ALPHA. SURRENDER THE COORDINATES OF THE ACTIVATED SPIRE AND SUBMIT TO DETAINMENT. ANY RESISTANCE WILL BE MET WITH LETHAL FORCE. THIS IS YOUR FINAL WARNING.”

“I can create a gate,” Luminaire said, her voice strained. “A Void Jump. Here. Now. Using the ambient chroniton chaos and my own core resonance. It will be unstable. Untargeted. But it is the only way.”

“Untargeted?” Oreck growled, suspended in Geralt’s grip. “Where does it go?”

“Where the dimensional stress is greatest. Where the shadow bleed pulls strongest.” Luminaire’s meaning was clear. “The Umbra Planes. We start the mission now.”

Below them, Vek’tar skidded to a halt in a flat basin, turning to face them, his expression resolved into a kind of mad, accepting calm. He raised both hands, chroniton energy crackling around them like a storm.

“Do it!” he shouted.

Above, the lead Federation cruiser opened its ventral bays. Drop-ships descended.

Geralt landed, setting Oreck down. He looked at Vek’tar, then at the sky filling with enemies. “Do it, Luminaire.”

The air before them tore open. Not with light, but with an absence so profound it hurt to perceive. The Void Gate wasn’t a doorway. It was a wound.

Without another word, the three of them—the Anchor, the Father, and the Broken Scout—ran forward and leaped into the screaming, shadow-clotted unknown.
